## KQ1. Which acts need a licence (RPL §440, §440-a); does doing them "through software" change anything; the compensation rules (§442, §442-a, §442-d) and referral fees to unlicensed persons

### Takeaway
Listing, selling, buying, renting, "offer[ing] or attempt[ing] to negotiate" a sale, and collecting rent, each done "for another and for a fee," are broker acts that require a licence, whoever (or whatever) performs them; the statute regulates persons and entities, says nothing about software, and attaches the duty to the person or entity that "engage[s] in or follow[s] the business" or "hold[s] themselves ... out." A licensed broker may pay a share of a commission only to licensed persons (plus out-of-state brokers and a narrow all-licensee-owned entity), may rebate to the principals, and may not pay an unlicensed person anything that is compensation "for any service, help or aid" in the sale; DOS reads this as barring transaction-based pay to unlicensed people, which is the rule that bites a tech company or unlicensed co-founder paid per closing.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Nothing in Article 12-A refers to software, automation or "platforms"; the licensing trigger is functional ("lists for sale," "offers or attempts to negotiate," "for another and for a fee"). Software that lists, markets, solicits offers and negotiates on a consumer's behalf for a fee is performing §440 acts; the law attributes those acts to the person/entity that operates the software and takes the fee. If that operator is a licensed broker (individual or licensed entity), no additional licence is created or needed for "the software"; if the operator (or the party paid for the acts) is unlicensed, §440-a, §442-d and §442-e apply to it exactly as to an unlicensed human.
- [INFERENCE] §442-e(4) matters for a volume business: once there are "repeated and successive acts," the fee element is presumed, so an unlicensed party that is systematically "offering to negotiate" cannot escape by arguing it is not paid per act.
- [INFERENCE] "Referral fees" to unlicensed persons are not addressed by name; they fall under §442(1) because a referral is "service, help or aid rendered ... to the broker in ... selling." The only lawful recipients of a share of the broker's commission are: a licensed salesperson/associate broker associated with that broker, another licensed NY broker, an out-of-state broker, or a corporation/LLC wholly owned by licensees associated with the broker. DOS's assistants guidance confirms the department's operating rule: pay unlicensed people by salary, hourly rate or per-activity fee, never "on a completed transaction basis."
- [INFERENCE] The buyer/seller rebate carve-out is the one lawful way to move commission money to an unlicensed party, and only to a principal in that transaction and not "for performing any activity requiring a license."

## KQ2. The attorney exemption (RPL §442-f): what an admitted New York attorney may do without a broker's licence, when a licence becomes necessary, and what DOS requires of an attorney applicant

### Takeaway
§442-f says Article 12-A "shall not apply to ... attorneys at law," so an admitted attorney may personally perform broker acts and collect compensation without a licence; but the exemption is personal, a corporation or LLC is not an "attorney at law," and only a "licensed real estate broker" can have salespersons or associate brokers "associated with" it, so the moment the plan uses an entity, hires licensees, or joins an MLS, the attorney should hold a broker's licence. DOS grants it on application and a $185 fee with no course, no experience and no exam, and exempts admitted attorneys from continuing education.

### Cited Findings

- [PRIMARY] DOS Real Estate Broker Application DOS-0036-f-a (Rev. 09/23), instructions: "First Time Applicants: You may apply for a real estate broker license based on Experience, Education and Examination, or if you are an Attorney who is currently admitted to the New York State bar." and "Attorneys who are admitted to the New York State bar are exempt from the educational, experience and examination requirements. Submit a completed application and fee, indicating on the application that you are admitted to the New York State bar." The application's qualification checkbox list is: "Attorney Admitted to NYS Bar / Previously Licensed / Additional Broker License / DOS Approved Qualifying Education"; page 13 checklist: "STOP here if you are applying for an additional broker license or if you are an attorney admitted to the NYS Bar Association." Fee: "The nonrefundable application fee for a real estate broker license is $185; the license will be effective for two years." — [DOS broker application PDF](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-application)

- [PRIMARY] RPL §441(1)(b) (revision 2022-12-23) sets the exam/education/experience route for everyone else, including "proof that the person being tested to qualify to apply for a broker's license has a fair knowledge of the English language ... has taken a class on cultural competency training ..." and "at least one hundred fifty-two hours" of approved courses plus two years as a licensed salesperson or "equivalent experience in general real estate business for a period of at least three years". The statute itself contains no attorney clause; the attorney route is DOS practice resting on §442-f. — [RPL §441](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/441)

- [PRIMARY] DOS FAQ, "What is a Real Estate Broker?": "A real estate broker is responsible for the supervision and conduct of the real estate brokerage business. He or she applies for and holds the license on behalf of the brokerage. This person is known as the "representative broker." All listings, although perhaps negotiated by a salesperson, are accepted by the representative broker." — [DOS Real Estate Broker FAQ](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-frequently-asked-questions)
- [PRIMARY] GOL §5-701(a)(10) (statute of frauds for brokerage compensation) does "not apply to a contract to pay compensation to an auctioneer, an attorney at law, or a duly licensed real estate broker or real estate salesman" (page revision 2014-09-22), so an attorney's or licensed broker's commission agreement need not be in writing to be enforceable. — [GOL §5-701](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/5-701)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Because "the provisions of this article shall not apply to ... attorneys at law," §442-d's licence prerequisite to suing for a commission does not apply to an attorney acting personally; an attorney can list, negotiate and be paid without a licence. (The classic authority usually cited is the Court of Appeals' Weingast v. Rialto Pastry Shop, 243 N.Y. 113 (1926) [CLAIMED – not verified this session; the attorney-broker should pull it and the later Appellate Division cases].)
- [INFERENCE] The exemption is for "attorneys at law," i.e., natural persons admitted to the bar. A corporation or LLC that the attorney owns is a separate "person, firm, limited liability company or corporation" under §440(1) and §440-a and gets no exemption; if the business is run through an entity, that entity must be licensed (see KQ3). (Lead for verification: Pacific Carlton Dev. Corp. v. 752 Pacific, LLC, 62 A.D.3d 677 (2d Dep't 2009) is commonly cited for refusing to extend the attorney exemption to the attorney's corporation [CLAIMED – not verified this session].)
- [INFERENCE] Triggers that make a broker's licence practically mandatory for the attorney: (1) any salesperson or associate broker must be "associated with a licensed real estate broker" and may be paid only by that licensed broker (§440(3), §442-a, §442-c), so an unlicensed attorney cannot sponsor licensees; (2) a licensed entity needs a licensed officer/member as representative broker (KQ3); (3) §442(1) lets a licensed broker share a commission only with "a duly licensed real estate broker" (or salesperson/out-of-state broker), so a cooperating listing broker could refuse, and arguably may not, pay a share to an unlicensed attorney-broker, which undermines any cooperative-compensation model; (4) MLS participation rules [CLAIMED] require a licensed broker participant. The clean solution is for the attorney to take the licence, which DOS issues on the form and fee alone.
- [INFERENCE] Once licensed, the attorney is a licensee for all purposes (supervision, advertising, §443, SOP, discipline under §441-c), losing none of the exemption but gaining all of the obligations; DOS's CE exemption for admitted attorneys continues.
- [INFERENCE] Acting as both lawyer and broker in the same deal raises professional-responsibility and RESPA "multiple services" issues (12 CFR §1024.14(g)(3)); these were outside this research's scope but should be addressed before the attorney drafts the contract in deals in which his brokerage is paid.

### Gaps
- No DOS legal memorandum stating the department's official position on attorneys acting as brokers without a licence was found (the DOS memoranda index retrieved did not list one); the position is evidenced by the application form and FAQ only.
- Case law (Weingast; Pacific Carlton; cases on attorneys' recovery of commissions and on §442 payments to attorney-brokers) could not be retrieved without search; citations above are leads, not verified findings.
- Whether a cooperating licensed broker may lawfully pay a share of commission to an unlicensed attorney under §442(1) is unresolved in the sources found; the statutory text lists only licensed recipients.

## KQ3. Entity licensing (corporation/LLC), who may own it and share in revenue, DBA rules, and whether an unlicensed technology company may take a share of commissions

### Takeaway
A corporation or LLC becomes a licensed broker by having a qualified officer (corporation) or member/manager (LLC) apply as its representative broker; the statute requires disclosure and vetting of every officer/member but asks for trustworthiness findings only for those "for whom a license as a broker is asked," and the only ownership bar in the rules is that a salesperson may not hold voting stock in his own brokerage corporation. Nothing found prohibits an unlicensed person from owning equity and receiving profit distributions, but §442 bars paying him, or an unlicensed tech company, any part of a commission "for any service, help or aid" in the sale, and DOS treats transaction-based pay to unlicensed people as requiring a licence; a per-closing fee to the software company is therefore the single riskiest design choice.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Mechanics: form the LLC (or corporation), file its articles/assumed name with the DOS Division of Corporations, get the business name pre-cleared by the Division of Licensing Services, then the attorney (as a member/manager "who meets the qualifications for licensure," which the attorney does by bar admission) files DOS-0036-f-a as representative broker of the LLC; the licence is issued to the LLC and "entitle[s] the ... limited liability company ... to act as a real estate broker." Any other member/officer who will perform §440 acts needs his own broker licence ("Additional Broker License" on the form); the partnership instruction ("Any partner who wishes to engage in the business of a real estate brokerage must be licensed") states the same principle expressly for partnerships.
- [INFERENCE] Unlicensed ownership: §441 requires every member/officer to be named and their two-year history disclosed, but trustworthiness is tested only for those "for whom a license as a broker is asked," which presupposes members/officers who are not licensed. The one express ownership prohibition (§175.22) targets salespersons, not unlicensed persons. So the unlicensed co-founder may be a member/shareholder and take profit distributions as an owner; no DOS statement to the contrary was found. The risk is characterization: if his "distributions" are in substance pay for services in transactions (e.g., sized per closing), §442(1) and the DOS assistants rule are engaged. He must not perform §440 acts (negotiate, solicit listings, make offers) himself.
- [INFERENCE] Technology company revenue: a fixed subscription/licence fee, or a flat per-user fee, for software is payment for "goods or facilities actually furnished" and is not "part of a fee, commission or other compensation received by the broker ... for any service, help or aid" in a sale; a percentage of each commission, or a fee due only on closing, paid to an unlicensed company that helped make the sale, is the §442(1) violation on its face, and §442(1)'s narrow exception (entity wholly owned by the broker's own associated licensees) shows the legislature knew how to allow entity-level splits and did not allow this one. The same fee pattern is evidence of a RESPA §8 referral arrangement in deals with federally related mortgage loans. Safest structure found in the sources: the software company charges the brokerage a fixed fee not tied to deals, and the unlicensed co-founder's upside comes from equity in the brokerage (or the software company) rather than from transaction-linked payments.
- [INFERENCE] A virtual brokerage still needs "a definite place of business within this state" with a sidewalk-readable sign (§441-a(3)); a home office qualifies only if licensed as the office (19 NYCRR §175.25(c)(6): "A residence may be used as an office provided that it is properly licensed by the Department of State").

## KQ5. Supervision: RPL §441(1)(d) and 19 NYCRR §175.21; how DOS treats remote or technology-driven supervision; whether a one-broker brokerage with software "agents" meets the duty

### Takeaway
The regulatory duty is to give salespersons "regular, frequent and consistent personal guidance, instruction, oversight and superintendence," which DOS reads as policies, training and monitoring rather than "look[ing] over a salesperson's shoulder constantly"; the duty runs to licensees and to unlicensed assistants' work, and DOS expressly extends it to licensee websites "while the website is live." Nothing found addresses supervision of software, and a brokerage whose only licensee is the broker has no salespersons to supervise under §175.21; the real exposure is instead that the broker must himself be the person performing and controlling the licensed acts, and he carries direct liability (including for Human Rights Law violations and SOP compliance) for what the system does in the brokerage's name.

### Cited Findings
- [PRIMARY] 19 NYCRR §175.21(a), full text: "The supervision of a real estate salesperson by a licensed real estate broker, required by subdivision l(d) of §441 of the Real Property Law, shall consist of regular, frequent and consistent personal guidance, instruction, oversight and superintendence by the real estate broker with respect to the general real estate brokerage business conducted by the broker, and all matters relating thereto." §175.21(c): "Participation in the general real estate brokerage business as a licensed real estate salesperson shall consist of active service under the supervision of a licensed real estate broker for at least 35 hours per week for 50 weeks in each year required for qualification under the law." — [DOS Real Estate License Law (Jan. 2024) PDF](https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2024/07/re-law.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] RPL §441(1)(d) as it now reads (revision 2022-12-23) is a course-content provision: the 152 hours "shall include ... instruction on the licensed real estate broker's responsibility for ensuring that each licensed real estate salesman under such broker's supervision is in compliance with his or her obligations under applicable federal, state, and local laws, rules, and regulations pertaining to fair housing and discrimination". — [RPL §441](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/441)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] §175.21's cross-reference to "subdivision l(d) of §441" is to an older numbering; today's §441(1)(d) only prescribes course content, and the Metalios decision cites §441(1)(b). The duty is nonetheless settled in DOS practice and case law (Friedman v. Paterson), so the numbering mismatch is not a defence.
- [INFERENCE] DOS's standard is procedural: written policies, training, regular reminders, monitoring, and records. Nothing requires physical co-location; Metalios treats "weekly meetings" and no work-from-home as favourable facts, not requirements. No DOS statement on remote or technology-mediated supervision was found, so a remote model is neither approved nor disapproved; the §175.25(d)(3) rule shows DOS expects a broker to keep live supervision over digital channels.
- [INFERENCE] A brokerage with one licensee (the attorney-broker) and no salespersons has no §175.21 supervisees; the duty would attach the moment any salesperson/associate broker is added (and then §175.21(c)'s full-time concept, the office-manager rules and §442-c's actual-knowledge limit apply). What remains for the single-broker model is heavier: the broker is the only person who may lawfully perform the §440 acts, so the design must keep the human broker as the actor who accepts listings ("All listings ... are accepted by the representative broker," DOS FAQ), approves advertising, makes and conveys offers and conducts negotiation, with software doing the clerical/assistant-type work DOS lists for unlicensed staff. If the unlicensed co-founder's software autonomously negotiates, the better reading is that the brokerage (licensed) is acting, but DOS could view the co-founder as "act[ing] ... as a real estate broker" through the system he built and operates; no authority either way (KQ9).
- [INFERENCE] Automated intake is squarely regulated: the broker's SOP must say whether ID, an exclusive agreement and mortgage pre-approval are required, must be applied uniformly, and discriminatory outcomes of automated steering or screening are Human Rights Law violations for which the broker is directly disciplinable.

### Gaps
- No DOS opinion, decision or guidance on "remote," "virtual" or "technology-driven" supervision was found; the Metalios decision is the clearest statement of the practical standard that could be retrieved. A targeted search of OOAH decisions (2015–2026) for "virtual" or "remote" is the next step.

## KQ6. Written agreements: are buyer-broker agreements required in New York by law or by MLS rule since the NAR settlement (Aug. 2024) and any 2025–2026 legislation; the NYC FARE Act as a signal; what listing and buyer agreements must contain (19 NYCRR §175.24, §175.7, RPL §443)

## KQ7. Rebates, flat fees, limited-service (entry-only) listings: legal in New York? Disclosures triggered; any minimum-service rule?

### Takeaway
Commission rebates to buyers and sellers are expressly lawful under RPL §442 and confirmed by DOS; commissions are not regulated, so flat fees are lawful; no minimum-service statute or rule exists in Article 12-A or Part 175 (the full Part 175 table of contents in DOS's compilation contains none), but exclusive listings must carry the §175.24 explanation, auto-renewal and net listings are banned, the §443 form and SOP posting apply, and every entry-only listing is the broker's own advertisement that must identify the broker and be owner-authorized.

### Cited Findings
- [PRIMARY] RPL §442: "nothing in this section shall prohibit a real estate broker from offering any part of a fee, commission, or other compensation received by the broker to the seller, buyer, landlord or tenant who is buying, selling, exchanging, leasing, renting or negotiating a loan upon any real estate including the resale of a condominium or cooperative apartment. Such fee, commission, or other compensation must not be made to the seller, buyer, landlord or tenant for performing any activity requiring a license under this article." — [RPL §442](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442)
- [PRIMARY] DOS FAQ: "The payment of cash or offering an incentive to encourage a consumer to do business with a broker is permitted." and "The commission or compensation of a real estate broker is not regulated by statute or regulation, therefore the amount and terms are negotiable." — [DOS Real Estate Broker FAQ](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-frequently-asked-questions)
- [PRIMARY] 19 NYCRR Part 175 section list (DOS compilation, Jan. 2024): §§175.1–175.29 cover commingling, accounting, property management, broker self-dealing disclosures, compensation (175.7), exclusive-listing conduct (175.8, 175.9, 175.15), authorization to offer (175.10), signs, delivery of instruments (175.12), another broker's salesperson (175.13), termination of association, solicitation/cease-and-desist (175.17), trade names, net listings (175.19), branch offices, supervision, voting stock, records, exclusive listings (175.24), advertising (175.25), signs, disclaimer, fair-housing notices (175.28, 175.29). No section imposes minimum services for a listing. — [DOS Real Estate License Law (Jan. 2024) PDF](https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2024/07/re-law.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] 19 NYCRR §175.10 (title: "Broker's offering property for sale must be authorized") and §175.25(b)(2)(a): "No property shall be advertised unless the real estate broker has obtained authorization for such advertisement from the owner of the property". §175.25(d)(1): "Classified and multi-property advertisements shall indicate that the advertiser is a real estate broker or brokerage; or provide the name of the real estate broker or real estate brokerage." — same source
- [PRIMARY] §175.24(b) explanation and §175.24(c) offer-routing option (quoted in KQ6); §175.15 (no automatic continuation); §175.19 (no net listings); §175.7 (disclose which party you act for; no dual compensation without client's knowledge and consent). — same source
- [SECONDARY] NAR/NYSAR: the settlement permits "Limited service" and "One property, one weekend" buyer agreements and "$0, X flat fee, X percent, X hourly rate" compensation. — [NYSAR Legal Update PDF](https://nysar.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Legal-Update-NYSAR-LM-DESKTOP.pdf)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] A rebate must go to a party to the transaction and must not be pay for licensed activity (e.g., a "rebate" to a seller for showing the house himself is fine because an owner selling his own property is not acting "for another"; a payment to a buyer for finding another customer is not). Rebates must also be disclosed where a lender or the contract requires; that is a lending/underwriting matter outside this research.
- [INFERENCE] An "entry-only" MLS listing is lawful under state law but is an exclusive listing of residential property in most MLS forms, so the §175.24 explanation applies; the broker remains the advertiser and remains responsible for accuracy (§175.25(c)(9)) and fair-housing compliance. Whether OneKey's rules impose minimum services or special "limited service" flags is an MLS-rule question (Gap).

- Any DOS decision disciplining a flat-fee or entry-only broker (for misleading advertising or failure to supervise) was not found (search unavailable).

## KQ8. Penalties and precedent: what DOS can do, and what it has actually done to online or discount brokerages and unlicensed platforms

### Takeaway
Unlicensed brokerage is a misdemeanor prosecuted by the Attorney General, exposes the offender to a civil penalty of one to four times the compensation received at the suit of any aggrieved person, and bars any suit for the fee; licensees face revocation, suspension, a fine up to $2,000 per determination or reprimand for violations, misleading advertising, "untrustworthiness" (which the Court of Appeals reads broadly) and Human Rights Law or SOP violations. No DOS or Attorney General action against an online or discount brokerage or an unlicensed platform could be retrieved in this session; the only DOS statement about platforms found is its 2020 advertising rules aimed at Zillow/StreetEasy-style lead generation.

### Cited Findings
- [PRIMARY] RPL §442-e(1): "Any person who violates any provision of this article shall be guilty of a misdemeanor. The commission of a single act prohibited by this article shall constitute a violation hereof." §442-e(2): "Criminal actions for violations of this article shall be prosecuted by the attorney general". §442-e(3): "In case the offender shall have received any sum of money as commission, compensation or profit by or in consequence of his violation of any provision of this article, he shall also be liable to a penalty of not less than the amount of the sum of money received by him as such commission, compensation or profit and not more than four times the sum so received by him, as may be determined by the court, which penalty may be sued for and recovered by any person aggrieved". §442-e(5): the secretary of state may "investigate the business, business practices and business methods of any person, firm or corporation applying for or holding a license"; §442-e(6): subpoena power; §442-e(8): DOS may fine "persons not licensed" for violating §442-h (solicitation/SOP rules) "not exceeding one hundred fifty dollars for the first violation, not exceeding five hundred dollars for a second violation, and not exceeding one thousand dollars for a third and each subsequent violation." — [RPL §442-e](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442-E)
- [PRIMARY] RPL §441-c(1)(a): DOS "may revoke the license of a real estate broker or salesperson or suspend the same, for such period as the department may deem proper, or in lieu thereof may impose a fine not exceeding two thousand dollars payable to the department of state ... or a reprimand upon conviction of the licensee of a violation of any provision of this article, or for a violation of subdivision four of section four hundred forty-two-h of this article, or for a material misstatement in the application for such license, or if such licensee has been guilty of fraud or fraudulent practices, or for dishonest or misleading advertising, or has demonstrated untrustworthiness or incompetency to act as a real estate broker or salesperson, or for a violation of article fifteen of the executive law committed in their capacity as a real estate broker or salesperson"; §441-c(4): after revocation, ineligible for relicensing for one year. — [RPL §441-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/441-C)
- [PRIMARY] RPL §442-d bars any action for compensation without a licence (quoted in KQ1); RPL §442-c makes a broker "guilty of a misdemeanor for having any salesperson associated with their firm who has not secured the required license". — [RPL §442-d](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442-D); [RPL §442-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442-C)
- [PRIMARY] DOS Legal Memorandum LI01 (updated Feb. 2020): quoting Matter of Gold v. Lomenzo, 29 NY2d 468 (1972), "the Legislature intended the Secretary of State to be vested with a wide discretion in determining what should be deemed untrustworthy conduct"; conduct outside brokerage (Eich v. Shaffer; Dovale v. Patterson; Fogel v. Department of State) can ground discipline; but per Stowell v. Cuomo, 52 NY2d 208 (1981), DOS lacks jurisdiction over a pure contractual dispute with no "adverse effect upon the public," while "business practices ... so devious as to indicate clearly that he would not deal fairly with the public" can be "untrustworthiness." — [DOS LI01](https://dos.ny.gov/legal-memorandum-li01-discipline-real-estate-brokers-and-salespersons-untrustworthy-conduct)
- [PRIMARY] DOS v. Metalios, 1105 DOS 09: DOS charged a corporate broker with failure to supervise over cease-and-desist solicitation by his salespersons; charge dismissed on proof of policies, training and reminders (quoted in KQ5). — [1105 DOS 09](https://docs.dos.ny.gov/ooah/decisions/Real_estate/2009/Metalios_Menalaos.htm)
- [PRIMARY] DOS: 2020 internet-advertising regulations "require brokers using lead generation programs such as Zillow, StreetEasy, Trulia, and others to ensure that the listing agent is clearly disclosed and that the buyer's broker includes the word 'advertisement' in the advertising." — [DOS Real Estate Advertising](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-advertising)
- [SECONDARY] HGAR (Jan. 2022): DOS "commonly names the principal broker in the complaint for a 'failure to supervise'" and, after the Newsday investigation, filed complaints naming brokers for failure to supervise discriminatory conduct. — [HGAR Legal Corner](https://www.hgar.com/legal-corner-brokers-duty-to-supervise-changes-in-light-of-the-new-housing-discrimination-legislation)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The practical enforcement path against an unlicensed software operator would be (a) a §442-d defence by any client refusing to pay, (b) a §442-e(3) suit by an aggrieved consumer for up to four times the fee, (c) an Attorney General misdemeanor prosecution, and (d) for the licensed attorney-broker, §441-c discipline for "untrustworthiness" or for sharing commissions with the unlicensed party (a §442 violation is itself "a violation of any provision of this article"). DOS's direct fining power over unlicensed persons is limited to §442-h matters, which is why enforcement against unlicensed platforms would run through the Attorney General or private suits rather than DOS fines.
- [INFERENCE] Because "the commission of a single act" is a violation and the §442-e(4) presumption attaches to "repeated and successive acts," a scaled platform is a worse target than a one-off actor.

Leads: DOS OOAH real-estate decisions (docs.dos.ny.gov/ooah/decisions/Real_estate/, by year), Attorney General press releases on rental platforms and broker-fee practices (2023–2026), and NYSAR legal hotline summaries. ## KQ9. Is there any New York authority on an AI system performing negotiation or "procuring cause" acts under a broker's licence?

### Takeaway
None found: no New York statute, regulation, DOS memorandum, DOS decision or bill retrieved in this session mentions software, automation or artificial intelligence performing brokerage acts, and the only DOS guidance on non-licensees doing brokerage work is the human "unlicensed assistants" list, which reserves negotiation, offers and listing acceptance to licensees. The pending A8910/S9564 would, for the first time, define a buyer's agent by the acts of "present[ing] an offer ... and negotiat[ing] on behalf of the buyer," which underscores that negotiation is the core licensed act whoever or whatever executes it.

### Cited Findings
- [PRIMARY] The licensing definition turns on who "offers or attempts to negotiate a sale" "for another and for a fee" (RPL §440(1)) and on who "engage[s] in or follow[s] the business or occupation of, or hold[s] themselves or itself out or act[s] temporarily or otherwise as a real estate broker" (RPL §440-a). Neither mentions technology. — [RPL §440](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/440); [RPL §440-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/440-A)
- [PRIMARY] DOS's unlicensed-assistant list permits only clerical and preparatory tasks ("Type contract forms for approval by broker," "Write ads for approval of broker," "Schedule appointments for licensees to show listed property," "Gather information for a comparative market analysis") and makes "the licensed broker ... explicitly responsible for the supervision and control of activities conducted in the name of the licensed real estate business." — [DOS Unlicensed Real Estate Assistants](https://dos.ny.gov/unlicensed-real-estate-assistants)
- [PRIMARY] A8910/S9564 proposed definition: "'Buyer's agent' means an agent who contracts to locate residential real property for a buyer or who finds a buyer for a property and/or presents an offer to purchase to the seller or seller's agent and negotiates on behalf of the buyer." — [A8910 text](https://legislation.nysenate.gov/pdf/bills/2025/A8910)
- [PRIMARY] NAR's settlement FAQ keeps "procuring cause" as the arbitration test between brokers (Q57: "Does the prohibition of offers of compensation on the MLS mean that procuring cause will no longer be relevant to arbitration panels determining arbitration awards pursuant to Article 17 of the Code of Ethics or local MLS rules?"). — [NAR Settlement FAQs](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/nar-settlement-faqs)
- [PRIMARY] RPL §443(1)(a): "'Agent' means a person who is licensed as a real estate broker, associate real estate broker or real estate salesperson"; the disclosure form and acknowledgment duties in §443(3) are placed on the "listing agent," "seller's agent" and "buyer's agent." — [RPL §443](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/443)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Under current law an AI system has no legal personality; its acts are the acts of the licensee that deploys it. A licensed broker that uses software to solicit, present and negotiate is itself the "person" negotiating, so no separate licence or exemption is needed for the tool, and "procuring cause" (a compensation/arbitration doctrine between brokers) would be assessed by looking at what the brokerage did through its tools. The unresolved legal risk is the second actor: if the unlicensed co-founder (or his separate company) designs, operates and is paid in step with the negotiating system, DOS or a court could treat him as "act[ing] ... as a real estate broker" for the brokerage's clients (§440-a) and the payments as §442 splits; keeping him (and any software vendor) on fixed fees and keeping the attorney-broker as the person who approves offers, counteroffers and listing acceptance is the most defensible posture on the texts found.
- [INFERENCE] The §443 "agent" duties (first substantive contact; signed acknowledgments; refusal declarations under oath) can be executed electronically on the broker's behalf, but the record must show the licensee as the disclosing agent; the three-year retention rules (§443(3)(d)-(e), §175.23, §175.28(d)) require the system to store signed/duplicate disclosures and declarations.
- [INFERENCE] The fair-housing regime (SOP under §442-h(4), §175.28 notices, Executive Law art. 15 liability under §441-c) is the area where automated decisioning is most exposed; an AI that screens, steers or prioritises consumers by any protected characteristic (directly or by proxy) creates direct disciplinary liability for the attorney-broker.
