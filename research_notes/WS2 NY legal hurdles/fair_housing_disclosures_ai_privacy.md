## Key question 1 — Fair housing: what the software must never say or do, what notices it must deliver, and what New York enforcement looks like

### Takeaway
New York's fair-housing regime for licensees is stricter and more actively enforced than the federal baseline: Executive Law §296(5) protects 15 characteristics (including lawful source of income, citizenship/immigration status, military status, marital status and domestic-violence-victim status), DOS regulations require a specific state notice at first substantive contact plus website and open-house posting, RPL §442-h requires published, notarized standardized operating procedures applied to every buyer, and since 2020 DOS has revoked or suspended dozens of licences for steering, "school talk" and unequal pre-conditions, with fines capped at $2,000 but revocation the real penalty. Algorithmic ad targeting is governed federally by §3604(c) and the 2022 DOJ–Meta settlement; HUD's April 2024 AI-advertising guidance is no longer at its HUD URL (status unverified).

### Cited Findings

Federal baseline
- 42 U.S.C. §3604(c) makes it unlawful "To make, print, or publish, or cause to be made, printed, or published any notice, statement, or advertisement, with respect to the sale or rental of a dwelling that indicates any preference, limitation, or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin, or an intention to make any such preference, limitation, or discrimination." §3604(d) separately bars representing "that any dwelling is not available for inspection, sale, or rental when such dwelling is in fact so available." [PRIMARY] — [LII, 42 U.S.C. 3604](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/3604)
- HUD's two April 29, 2024 guidance documents ("Guidance on Application of the Fair Housing Act to the Advertising of Housing, Credit, and Other Real Estate-Related Transactions through Digital Platforms" and the companion tenant-screening guidance) were at hud.gov/sites/dfiles/FHEO/documents/; on 2026-10-23 both the original URL and the HUD press release URL (hud-no-24-098) returned HTTP 404, and no replacement page was found on HUD's site. [PRIMARY observation of dead links] — [HUD former URL](https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/FHEO/documents/FHEO_Guidance_on_Advertising_through_Digital_Platforms.pdf)
- From memory of the April 2024 advertising guidance (not re-verifiable today): HUD said advertisers (not only platforms) can violate §3604(c) by (a) targeting on protected characteristics or close proxies (e.g., interest categories correlated with race, ZIP codes, "lookalike"/custom audiences built from a skewed source list), and (b) using ad-delivery systems that skew who sees a housing ad; recommended practices were to use platform tools built for housing ads, avoid proxy targeting, run ads broadly, monitor audience outcomes and keep records. The tenant-screening guidance warned that AI/algorithmic screening does not shift responsibility from the housing provider, criticised overbroad criminal, eviction and credit criteria, and urged transparency to applicants. [CLAIMED — verify against an archived copy before encoding]
- DOJ sued Meta on June 21, 2022 over housing-ad delivery and settled the same week; Meta discontinued its "Special Ad Audience" (lookalike) tool for housing ads and built a "Variance Reduction System"; Meta remained under court oversight and compliance review through June 27, 2026; the civil penalty was $115,054, the FHA maximum. [SECONDARY] — [Consumer Financial Services Law Monitor, Jan 2023](https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2023/01/doj-provides-settlement-update-with-meta-over-allegedly-discriminatory-housing-advertising-practices/); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/meta-rolls-out-new-system-to-address-housing-ad-discrimination/)
- Meta's own account: the VRS "uses machine learning" so the audience that sees a housing ad "more closely reflects the eligible target audience"; race/ethnicity is estimated by Bayesian Improved Surname Geocoding with differential privacy; Special Ad Audiences were discontinued; advertisers targeting the US "cannot target housing, employment, or credit ads by age, gender, or ZIP code"; a third-party reviewer verified compliance in July 2023 and December 2023. [PRIMARY (Meta newsroom)] — [Meta, Jan 2023, updated Dec 2023](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/01/an-update-on-our-ads-fairness-efforts/)
- A 2025 peer-reviewed external evaluation found VRS "reduces variance according to the legal compliance metrics" but reaches fewer unique users and passes compliance cost to advertisers, and argues the settlement terms "allow for an implementation that does not meaningfully improve access to opportunities." New York State Human Rights Law (Executive Law Article 15)
- §296(5)(a)(1) protected characteristics, quoted from the current text (page revision 2026-06-05): "race, creed, color, national origin, citizenship or immigration status, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, military status, sex, age, disability, marital status, status as a victim of domestic violence, lawful source of income or familial status". The same list governs the advertising prohibition for brokers in §296(5)(c)(2) ("To print or circulate or cause to be printed or circulated any statement, advertisement or publication ... which expresses, directly or indirectly, any limitation, specification or discrimination as to ..."), and §296(5)(a)(3) applies the same prohibition to owners/agents generally. §296(5)(c)(1) bars a broker from refusing to sell, rent or lease or "representing that a property is not available" on a protected basis. The owner-occupied two-family rental exemption in §296(5)(a)(4) applies only to subparagraphs (1) and (2), not to the advertising subparagraph (3). The age/familial-status carve-out for 55+/62+ housing is §296(5)(c)(3). [PRIMARY] — [nysenate.gov, Exec. Law §296](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/296)
- Blockbusting/inducement provision is in §296(3-b), not §296(5)(c) (noted by the statute page; text not quoted). [PRIMARY, location only] — [same page](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/296)
- "Lawful source of income" (§292(36), page revision 2025-10-17) "shall include, but not be limited to, child support, alimony, foster care subsidies, income derived from social security, or any form of federal, state, or local public assistance or housing assistance including, but not limited to, section 8 vouchers, or any other form of housing assistance payment or credit whether or not such income or credit is paid or attributed directly to a landlord, and any other forms of lawful income." "Citizenship or immigration status" (§292(41)) means "the citizenship of any person or the immigration status of any person who is not a citizen of the United States." "Military status" (§292(28)) covers participation in US or state military service including the national guards and NY naval militia. "Familial status" (§292(26)) covers pregnancy, having a child, or securing custody of anyone under 18. [PRIMARY] — [nysenate.gov, Exec. Law §292](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/292)
- "Lawful occupation" is NOT a NYS Human Rights Law class; it is a New York City Human Rights Law class (NYC Admin. Code §8-107(5), with partnership status, alienage/citizenship status, presence of children and, since 2023, height and weight). NYC also has the FARE Act (Local Law 119 of 2024, effective June 11, 2025: landlord-hired rental brokers cannot charge tenants) and the Fair Chance for Housing Act (Local Law 24 of 2024, effective Jan. 1, 2025, restricting criminal-history screening). All NYC-only and [CLAIMED — nyc.gov pages returned 404 today; verify].

DOS regulations binding every licensee (19 NYCRR Part 175)
- 19 NYCRR 175.17(a)(1): "No broker or salesperson shall induce or attempt to induce an owner to sell, lease or list" residential property by representations about the entry into the neighbourhood of persons of a protected class; the regulation's own list is "race, color, religion, national origin, age, sex, sexual orientation, disability, gender identity, military status, and familial status" plus any other class protected by federal, state or local law. 175.17(a)(2): no solicitation after written notice the owner does not wish to sell/list. 175.17(a)(3): cease-and-desist zones. 175.17(b): "No real estate broker or salesperson shall engage in an unlawful discriminatory practice, as proscribed by any Federal, State or local law applicable to the activities of real estate licensees in New York State. A finding by any Federal, State or local agency or court of competent jurisdiction that a real estate broker or salesperson has engaged in unlawful discriminatory practice in the performance of licensed real estate activities shall be presumptive evidence of untrustworthiness and will subject such licensee to discipline, including a proceeding for revocation." Amendment history on the page: eff. 5/18/2016, 10/1/2017, 7/1/2018, 7/1/2023. [PRIMARY] — [LII, 19 NYCRR 175.17](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/19-NYCRR-175.17); 175.17(b) text also quoted verbatim in DOS decision 160 DOS 21 — [DOS OAH, Abarca](https://docs.dos.ny.gov/ooah/decisions/2021/Abarca,%20Aminta.pdf)

- 19 NYCRR 175.28 (adopted State Register May 6, 2020, eff. June 20, 2020): (a) every broker must ensure that associated licensees give "a prospective purchaser, tenant, seller, or landlord" a Department-furnished notice "upon first substantive contact" covering the Human Rights Law and how to file a complaint; (b) the notice may be delivered by email, text, electronic messaging, fax or hard copy; a link is acceptable "if the message also says the link contains Human Rights Law information"; oral disclosure does not satisfy the rule; (c) applies to all real property including condos, co-ops, vacant land and commercial; (d) hard-copy delivery requires a signed acknowledgment, electronic delivery requires keeping a duplicate, records kept at least three years, and if the recipient declines to sign the licensee keeps a sworn or affirmed written declaration of when it was provided, also three years. June 20, 2020) is the POSTING rule, not the training rule: (a) the DOS notice must be displayed at every office and branch; (b) in the window if the office has window postings, visible from the sidewalk; (c) "Websites maintained by brokers, associate brokers, salespersons, and real estate teams must prominently display a link to the Department's notice on the homepage"; (d) the notice must be displayed at all open houses and the §175.28(a) notice made available at open houses and showings. [PRIMARY] — [LII, 19 NYCRR 175.29](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/19-NYCRR-175.29). (Training requirements live in RPL §441(3), below.)

Standardized operating procedures (RPL §442-h(4))
- Statute text (page revision 2025-09-12): "Each real estate broker shall institute standardized operating procedures for the prerequisites prospective homebuyers must meet prior to receiving any services," including "(i) whether prospective clients shall show identification; (ii) whether an exclusive broker agreement is required; (iii) whether pre-approval for a mortgage loan is required; and (iv) any other such standardized operating procedures as the secretary of state shall determine by regulation." Brokers "shall date stamp, notarize and post such standardized operating procedures on any publicly available website and mobile device application they maintain, shall make a copy of such procedures available to the public upon request at their office locations, and shall maintain a file of such standardized operating procedures while the broker's license is active"; a team's or salesperson's website or app "should also have the brokers' policies posted or have a direct link"; changes must be re-posted "within thirty days" and archived; "Any broker or salesperson operating under a brokerage license that fails to adhere to such operating procedures shall be subject to the penalties imposed by section four hundred forty-one-c"; at every renewal the broker "must affirm to the department of state that they are in compliance." Licence discipline and training (RPL §§441, 441-c)
- RPL §441-c(1)(a) (page revision 2022-08-19): DOS may revoke or suspend, "or in lieu thereof may impose a fine not exceeding two thousand dollars payable to the department of state, or a reprimand" where the licensee "has been guilty of fraud or fraudulent practices, or for dishonest or misleading advertising, or has demonstrated untrustworthiness or incompetency ... or for a violation of article fifteen of the executive law" (the Human Rights Law), or for specified violations of §442-h(4); "fifty percent of all moneys received by the department of state for such fines ... shall be payable to the anti-discrimination in housing fund." [PRIMARY] — [nysenate.gov, RPL §441-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/441-C). Before 2022 the cap was $1,000 (quoted in the 2021 Abarca decision). [PRIMARY] — [DOS OAH, Abarca](https://docs.dos.ny.gov/ooah/decisions/2021/Abarca,%20Aminta.pdf)
- RPL §441(3)(a) (page revision 2022-12-23): each two-year renewal requires "at least twenty-two and one-half hours" of continuing education including "at least three hours of instruction pertaining to fair housing and/or discrimination", "at least two hours of instruction pertaining to implicit bias awareness and understanding", "at least two hours of cultural competency training", at least one hour on the law of agency (two for first-term salespersons), two and one-half hours on ethical business practices and one hour on recent legal matters; the salesperson qualifying course is 77 hours and the broker course 152 hours. Advertising content rules for licensees (19 NYCRR 175.25, amended eff. Jan. 4, 2017 and Nov. 2, 2020)
- "Advertising" covers promotion and solicitation by mail, phone, websites, email, signs, flyers and similar media. Only a broker may place ads; associates/teams need broker approval; no advertising a property without the owner's authorization; no advertising another broker's exclusive without permission; photos only with the copyright holder's written permission. Every ad must show the advertiser is a broker or give the broker/brokerage name plus full address or phone; licensees advertise under their licensed name (nicknames only with the full licensed name "clearly and conspicuously"); licence type must be stated accurately (titles like "sales associate" or bare "broker" prohibited); property descriptions "must be honest and accurate" and area names must be the actual location; no guarantees of future profit. Websites: "Every page, including search results pages, must include the required information," and an associate's homepage must link to the broker's site. Email: "The first email to a client or prospect must include the required information." Signs must identify the broker. Another broker's listing may be shown only with authorization and "Listing Courtesy of" the listing broker; paid third-party ads must carry the word "advertisement". Team names must include the members' full licensed names or be followed by "at/of" the brokerage, use the word "team", not "realty/group/associates", and unlicensed people's names cannot appear in a team name. [PRIMARY] — [LII, 19 NYCRR 175.25](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/19-NYCRR-175.25)

What actually triggered discipline in New York (the "risky words" evidence)
- Newsday's Feb. 15, 2024 analysis of 400+ pages of DOS rulings: of the 36 agents named in Long Island Divided, DOS penalised 15 — "revoked three of the agents' licenses, suspended the licenses of seven and fined five between $500 and $2,000, the most allowed under law"; DOS did not prosecute 10, dropped 2, judges dismissed 4, 5 were pending. Statewide, DOS disciplined four agents for fair-housing violations in 2016–2019 (fines up to $1,000 and a reprimand) versus "more than 40 agents statewide" in 2020–2023 "with six license revocations and 21 suspensions". Conduct-to-penalty mapping: unequal pre-conditions ("requiring a minority tester to get preapproved for a mortgage but serving a white tester without preconditions") drew "$2,000 fine to license suspension or revocation"; explicit demographic talk ("advising a white tester to avoid a 'mixed neighborhood'") drew six-month suspension to revocation; opinions about school districts, "positive or negative", drew "$500 fine to a three-month suspension" because judges called them a "proxy" for racial steering. Himanshoo (Raj) Sanghvi's licence was revoked on appeal in 2022 for telling a white tester Huntington is "a mixed neighborhood ... a mini, mini United Nations"; the appeals officer called the comments "egregious" and a revocation "a deterrent". Joy Tuxson's dismissal was reversed in part: telling a buyer to "look at the bus, who gets off the bus" was "a road map to a buyer who wishes to make a racially based purchase"; her licence was suspended until she completed a fair-housing course (June 9–10, 2022). Dismissals (e.g., Ann Pizaro) turned on DOS failing to prove the testers' race or discriminatory intent and over-relying on the Newsday article as hearsay. The AG launched a $3 million undercover testing programme in November 2023 funded by the new licence surcharges. [SECONDARY, detailed reporting] — [Newsday, Feb. 15, 2024 (PDF copy hosted by AGMB Law)](https://agmblaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/NYS-punishes-Long-Island-real-estate-agents-after-Newsdays-three-year-investigation-on-fair-housing-Newsday.pdf)
- DOS decision 160 DOS 21, Division of Licensing Services v. Aminta Abarca (ALJ John E. Kenny, Nov. 22, 2021): charges under RPL §441-c and 19 NYCRR 175.17(b); findings that the agent told a Black tester Hempstead schools "rank ... at the bottom" and that "Wikipedia will show you how many Haitians, how many whites, blacks ... The decision also holds hearsay (news articles, videos) can support a finding if "reliable, relevant, and probative" and corroborated. ### Inferences
- A chatbot or email that answers a buyer's "how are the schools?" or "what's the neighbourhood like?" with school rankings, demographic data, crime generalisations or "look at who gets off the bus" would reproduce the exact conduct DOS has punished since 2020; the software must refuse opinions on schools/neighbourhood composition and point users to public data sources without curating them differently per user (Abarca was penalised partly because the two testers got different specificity). [INFERENCE from Newsday/Abarca]
- RPL §442-h is a gift to a software design: encode the SOP (ID? exclusive agreement? pre-approval?) once, apply it identically to every buyer, log it, and publish it notarized and date-stamped on the site and app; any per-user deviation (e.g., the model asking only some users for pre-approval) is both a §441-c violation and evidence of disparate treatment. [INFERENCE]
- "First substantive contact" will typically occur inside the chat/email channel, so the DOS-2156 form (and the RPL §443 agency form, see Q2) must be delivered automatically at that moment, by a method the rule allows (email/text/link with the required descriptive sentence), with the duplicate stored for three years and a declaration generated if the user declines to sign. [INFERENCE from 175.28 and RPL §443]

## Key question 2 — Disclosures the software must generate, time and record (PCDS, lead paint, agency, smoke/CO, utility costs, agricultural districts, local water tests)

### Takeaway
Since March 20, 2024 every seller of a 1–4 family home (not condo/co-op/vacant land) must deliver the signed 56-question Property Condition Disclosure Statement, including 7 flood questions, before the buyer signs a binding contract, with no $500 opt-out and liability limited to actual damages for willful failure; the agency form under RPL §443 must be presented and signed at first substantive contact and kept three years; lead-paint (pre-1978), smoke-detector affidavit (Executive Law §378(5-b)(e)), truth-in-heating (Energy Law §17-103), agricultural-district notice (AML §310) and Westchester/Rockland well-test rules each have fixed trigger points the software can encode, but none of the NY statutes addresses electronic delivery, and the licensee's job under RPL §466 is to inform, not to fill in, the PCDS.

### Cited Findings

Property Condition Disclosure Statement (RPL Article 14, §§460–467)
- §462(1) (page revision 2024-03-29): "every seller of residential real property pursuant to a real estate purchase contract shall complete and sign" the statement and deliver it "prior to the signing by the buyer of a binding contract of sale"; a copy signed by seller and buyer "shall be attached to the real estate purchase contract"; parties may still agree on other condition terms including "as is". §462(3): the seller need not investigate or search public records. The form instructs "ANSWER ALL QUESTIONS BASED UPON YOUR ACTUAL KNOWLEDGE" (UNKN / NA allowed) and the seller "CERTIFIES THAT THE INFORMATION IN THIS PROPERTY CONDITION DISCLOSURE STATEMENT IS TRUE AND COMPLETE"; a revised statement is required if the seller learns the prior one is materially inaccurate, but not after title transfer or buyer occupancy. ## Key question 3 — Marketing and communications: CAN-SPAM, TCPA, NY telemarketing law, FTC impersonation and review rules, NY synthetic-performer and algorithmic-pricing laws, and whether any law requires telling a consumer they are talking to an AI

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 no New York or federal statute in force requires a brokerage chatbot to disclose that it is an AI, provided it is a customer-service/commercial-information system (GBL Article 47's AI-companion disclosure and $15,000/day penalty apply only to systems that simulate a sustained personal relationship); two disclosure bills (A3411-B generative-AI inaccuracy notice, passed both houses March 2026; S7263-A chatbot professional-impersonation bill) were pending at last check. What is in force: TCPA prior-express-written-consent and 10-business-day revocation rules for texts and robocalls ($500–$1,500 per message in private suits), GBL §399-z's 8am–9pm window and 30-second disclosure script for calls and texts ($20,000 per violation), CAN-SPAM ($53,088 per email), the FTC impersonation rule (16 CFR 461), the FTC fake-review rule (16 CFR 465), the NY synthetic-performer ad disclosure (GBL §396-b, in force June 9, 2026) and the algorithmic-pricing disclosure (GBL §349-a, in force Nov. 10, 2025).

### Cited Findings

Email — CAN-SPAM (15 U.S.C. §7701 et seq.)
- FTC compliance guide (page dated Aug. 2023, penalty figure updated Jan. 2024): "Don't use false or misleading header information"; "Don't use deceptive subject lines"; "Identify the message as an ad"; "Tell recipients where you're located"; "Tell recipients how to opt out"; "Honor opt-out requests promptly" — "within 10 business days"; "Monitor what others are doing on your behalf"; "Each separate email in violation of the CAN-SPAM Act is subject to penalties of up to $53,088." [PRIMARY (FTC)] — [FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)

Calls and texts — TCPA (47 U.S.C. §227; 47 CFR 64.1200)
- §227(b)(1)(A)(iii) bars calls using an automatic telephone dialing system or "an artificial or prerecorded voice" to "cellular telephone service" numbers without "prior express consent of the called party"; §227(b)(3) gives a private action for the greater of actual loss or "$500 in damages for each such violation", up to "3 times the amount" for willful or knowing violations; §227(c)(5) gives a private action to anyone receiving "more than one telephone call within any 12-month period by or on behalf of the same entity" in violation of the do-not-call rules. [PRIMARY] — [LII, 47 U.S.C. 227](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/227)
- 47 CFR 64.1200 (history ends with 90 FR 42138, Aug. 29, 2025): (a)(2) autodialed/prerecorded telemarketing calls and texts require "prior express written consent"; (f)(9) that consent is a signed written agreement that "clearly authorizes the seller" to deliver such messages to the number given, with disclosure that consent is not a condition of purchase; (a)(10) consumers may revoke "by any reasonable method to clearly express a desire not to receive further calls or text messages" and the caller must honor it "within a reasonable time not to exceed ten business days"; (a)(12) allows a one-time confirmation text within five minutes with no marketing; (c)(1) no telephone solicitation "before the hour of 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m." local time; (c)(2) national do-not-call registry, with exceptions for a signed "prior express invitation or permission" and a "personal relationship"; (d) a written do-not-call policy, training, and honoring company-specific requests within ten business days, records kept five years. The "one-to-one"/"logically and topically associated" consent language is not present in the current text. [PRIMARY] — [LII, 47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/64.1200)
- Insurance Marketing Coalition Ltd. v. FCC, No. 24-10277 (11th Cir. Jan. 24, 2025): "we agree with IMC that the FCC exceeded its statutory authority under the TCPA because the 2023 Order's" one-to-one-consent and logically-and-topically-related restrictions conflict with the ordinary meaning of "prior express consent"; the court granted the petition and "vacate[d] Part III.D of the 2023 Order". [PRIMARY] — [Eleventh Circuit opinion (PDF)](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202410277.pdf). The revocation rules in (a)(10)–(12) took effect April 11, 2025. [CLAIMED]

New York telemarketing — GBL §399-z (page revision 2024-11-29)
- "Telemarketing sales call" means "a telephone call or electronic messaging text made directly or indirectly by a telemarketer", and "electronic messaging text" means "real-time or near real-time non-voice messages in text form over communications networks" — so texts are covered; "customer" is "any natural person who is a resident of this state"; calls are allowed only "between 8:00 A.M. and 9:00 P.M. at the location of the customer unless the customer has given their express consent"; within the first 30 seconds the telemarketer must disclose, in order, the option to be added to the seller's entity-specific do-not-call list first, then the telemarketer's name, whether the call is recorded, its purpose and the goods/services that carry a fee; no unsolicited sales calls to numbers on the national do-not-call registry for 31 days; no unsolicited calls to anyone "in a county, city, town or village under a declared state of emergency"; after a hearing the Secretary of State may assess "a fine not to exceed twenty thousand dollars for each violation". [PRIMARY] — [nysenate.gov, GBL §399-z](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/399-Z)

### Inferences
- A brokerage assistant that answers listing questions, schedules showings and explains forms fits GBL §1700(4)(c)(i) ("solely for customer service" / "information about available commercial services"), so Article 47's disclosure and 988 protocol do not apply — but the exclusion is lost if the product is designed to retain personal preferences across sessions, ask unprompted emotion-based questions and sustain personal dialogue (all three elements of the definition). Designing the bot to avoid unsolicited emotional questions is cheap insurance; adding a voluntary "you are chatting with an automated assistant; a licensed broker reviews ..." line costs nothing and pre-empts A3411-B/S8874 if enacted. [INFERENCE]
- S7263-A does not currently list real estate brokerage as a "covered profession", but RPL Article 12-A makes brokerage a licensed activity; if the bill is amended or enacted with a broader list, a chatbot presenting itself as "your agent" could be treated as impersonating a licensee. The safer framing is that the human broker of record is the agent and the software is a tool the broker supervises. [INFERENCE]
- Texting buyers and sellers from the platform is the largest dollar exposure in this section: each autodialed marketing text without prior express written consent is a $500–$1,500 private-suit event and a $20,000 DOS event under §399-z. Transactional messages requested by the consumer (showing confirmations, document delivery) are lower-risk than promotional blasts, but the 8am–9pm window, the 30-second opening disclosures on calls, DNC scrubbing (national list plus the company list) and 10-business-day revocation handling should be hard-coded. [INFERENCE]
- AI-generated listing videos with a synthetic presenter or voiceover avatar shown in New York require the §396-b "synthetic performer" disclosure from June 9, 2026; audio-only ads and pure text copy do not. AI-written testimonials or "reviews" of the service are a §465.2 violation. Using a real listing agent's or competitor brokerage's name/branding in AI-generated outreach is a §461.3 issue. [INFERENCE]
- Charging different fees to different consumers based on their data (e.g., an algorithm that quotes a lower flat fee to some users) would trigger the §349-a disclosure; a uniform published fee avoids it. [INFERENCE]

## Key question 4 — Data: SHIELD Act safeguards and breach notice, New York Privacy Act status, DOS record-keeping, FTC Act §5, SSNs, financial pre-approvals and identity documents, and wire-fraud warnings

### Takeaway
The software will hold "private information" under GBL §899-aa (SSNs, driver's licence numbers, account numbers, and since March 2025 medical and health-insurance data), so it must run a written security programme meeting §899-bb's administrative/technical/physical safeguards (enforced by the AG at up to $5,000 per violation, no private action) and notify affected residents and the AG, DOS and State Police within 30 days of discovering a breach (since Dec. 21, 2024); New York still has no comprehensive privacy statute (NY Privacy Act S3044/A4947 not passed; NYHIPA vetoed Dec. 2025); DOS requires brokers to keep transaction records three years (19 NYCRR 175.23) plus three-year retention of agency and fair-housing acknowledgments; GBL §399-ddd restricts how SSNs may be collected and transmitted; and real-estate wire fraud is a documented, FBI-tracked loss category (9,359 real-estate complaints and $173.6 million in 2024; New York ranked fourth nationally with $904 million in all internet-crime losses), so wire-instruction warnings are a standard-of-care matter even though no New York statute mandates them.

### Cited Findings

SHIELD Act — breach notification (GBL §899-aa; page revision 2025-03-28)
- "Private information" is personal information plus one or more of: Social Security number; driver's licence or non-driver ID number; account, credit or debit card number with any required code; biometric information; medical information ("any information regarding an individual's medical history, mental or physical condition, or medical treatment or diagnosis"); health insurance information (policy/subscriber number, unique identifier, application and claims history); or a username/email plus password or security answer. Notice to affected residents must be "in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay" and "within thirty days after the breach has been discovered"; the business must also notify "the attorney general, the department of state and the division of state police" (and DFS if it is a 23 NYCRR Part 500 covered entity) of the timing, content and distribution of notices, and consumer reporting agencies if more than 5,000 residents are notified. AG may sue; for knowing or reckless violations "a civil penalty of the greater of five thousand dollars or up to twenty dollars per instance of failed notification", not to exceed $250,000; actions within three years, and "In no event shall an action be brought after six years from the date of discovery" unless concealed. [PRIMARY] — [nysenate.gov, GBL §899-aa](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/899-AA)

SHIELD Act — reasonable safeguards (GBL §899-bb)
- Any person or business that owns or licenses computerized data including private information of a NY resident "shall develop, implement and maintain reasonable safeguards"; deemed compliant if a "compliant regulated entity" under GLBA Title V, HIPAA/HITECH or 23 NYCRR Part 500; otherwise the programme must include "reasonable administrative safeguards such as the following" (designating coordinating employees, identifying reasonably foreseeable internal and external risks, assessing safeguards, training employees, selecting service providers capable of safeguards and requiring them by contract, adjusting the programme), "reasonable technical safeguards such as the following" (assessing risks in network and software design and in information processing/transmission/storage, detecting, preventing and responding to attacks, regular testing), and "reasonable physical safeguards such as the following" (assessing storage and disposal risks, protecting against unauthorized access during collection, transport and destruction, disposing of private information within a reasonable time). ### Inferences
- A brokerage platform holding pre-approval letters, bank statements, ID scans and SSNs (e.g., for co-op board packages or lender hand-offs) is squarely inside §899-aa; the cheapest compliant posture is to collect the minimum (never require an SSN through the chat; accept pre-approval letters without account numbers), encrypt at rest and in transit so that encrypted data falls outside "private information", and keep an incident-response runbook that can meet the 30-day clock with simultaneous AG/DOS/State Police notice. [INFERENCE]
- Because §899-bb deems a GLBA-compliant programme sufficient and the attorney-broker may also handle escrow and closing funds, modelling the written programme on the FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314) is a defensible template even though a brokerage is not itself a GLBA financial institution. [INFERENCE]
- DOS's three-year rule is a floor; PCDS/lead-paint/agency acknowledgment retention also runs three years, and the FHA/§296 limitation periods (two years federal private action; one year DHR complaint; three years state court) mean the fair-housing audit trail (what the model said to whom, SOP application logs, ad-targeting parameters) should be retained at least three years and arguably longer. [INFERENCE; limitation periods are CLAIMED]
- Wire-instruction handling is the highest-severity data risk: the system should never transmit or change wire instructions through the chat/email channel, should insert a standing warning that it will never send wiring instructions, and should log the attorney's verbal verification — a standard-of-care expectation rather than a statutory one in New York. [INFERENCE]
- Marketing claims such as "AI that replaces your agent" or "lawyer-grade" invite the DoNotPay theory (unsubstantiated performance claims) under FTC Act §5 and GBL §§349–350; claims should be limited to what the attorney-broker actually reviews. [INFERENCE]

### Inferences
Suggested risk ranking for the plan (observed NY enforcement × severity × how directly the software's own outputs create the violation):
1. Fair-housing steering/disparate treatment in buyer conversations and unequal application of SOPs — proven licence revocations and AG settlements; the model's words are the evidence; a finding is "presumptive evidence of untrustworthiness" (175.17(b)). Highest.
2. Discriminatory or proxy-targeted advertising and listing copy (§296(5)(c)(2), §3604(c), Meta precedent) — federal enforcement exists, NY testing programmes are now funded; AI-generated copy at scale multiplies exposure. High.
3. Missing or late statutory notices that DOS can audit on a complaint (DOS-2156 at first contact, website notice link, SOP posting, §443 agency form, 175.25 broker identification on every page/first email) — cheap to encode, routinely checked, each a §441-c ground. High frequency, low-to-medium severity.
4. Texting/calling compliance (TCPA, §399-z) — no NY brokerage case retrieved but per-message statutory damages make a single bad campaign expensive. Medium-high.
5. Data security and breach handling (SHIELD, §399-ddd, wire fraud) — AG enforcement exists in other sectors; a wire-fraud loss is the single most damaging event for a small brokerage. Medium-high severity, lower frequency.
6. Transaction disclosures (PCDS, lead paint, smoke affidavit, §17-103, AML §310, county well tests) — mostly civil/contractual consequences; lead paint is the exception with federal penalties and treble damages. Medium.
7. AI-specific statutes (Art. 47, §396-b, §349-a) and pending bills — low observed enforcement, easily satisfied by design choices (customer-service scope, uniform pricing, synthetic-performer label). Low today, rising.
8. Attorney-broker structure: RPL Article 12-A licensing (§440-a, §442 limits on sharing compensation with unlicensed persons, §442-f attorney provisions) is outside this note's scope but governs who may "replace the agent" at all. [CLAIMED pointer]

### New York Human Rights Law, county laws and notices (2025–2026 changes)

#### Takeaway
New York codified housing disparate impact on 2025-12-19 (§296(5-a), Ch. 649 of 2025, effective immediately). A broker's neutral-looking practice (an AI lead-scoring rule, an ad audience, a screening threshold) is now unlawful in NY if it has an unjustified discriminatory effect on any of the 15 classes, with a statutory burden-shift. The DHR filing window is three years for acts since 2024-02-15. Westchester adds classes and protects source of income at county level.

#### Cited Findings
- §296(5-a)(a): "For any case alleging housing discrimination under this article, an unlawful discriminatory practice may be established by a practice's discriminatory effect, even if such practice was not motivated by a discriminatory intent. The practice may still be lawful if supported by a legally sufficient justification". (b): a discriminatory effect exists where a practice "actually or predictably results in a disparate impact on a group of persons or creates, increases, reinforces, or perpetuates segregated housing patterns because of" the 15 classes. (c): justification = "necessary to achieve one or more substantial, legitimate, nondiscriminatory interests of the respondent" and those interests "could not be served by another practice that has a less discriminatory effect", "supported by evidence and may not be hypothetical or speculative". (d): burden-shift (complainant proves effect; respondent proves necessity; complainant may show a less discriminatory alternative). (f): no narrowing of broader interpretations. — [PRIMARY] — [nysenate.gov, Exec. Law §296](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/296), accessed 2026-10-23.

### Marketing and communications updates (TCPA, AI voices, §399-z, FTC)

#### Takeaway
AI-generated voices are "artificial" voices under the TCPA (FCC 24-17, Feb. 2024). An AI voice agent placing calls therefore needs the same consent as a robocall: prior express consent, and prior express written consent for marketing. The TCPA "revoke-all" provision is postponed to 2027-01-31, but the other revocation duties are in force. NY §399-z was tightened in Nov. 2024 (Ch. 539). FTC rule dates and penalty caps are confirmed and unchanged for 2026.

#### Cited Findings
- FCC Declaratory Ruling FCC 24-17 (CG Docket 23-362, adopted unanimously 2024-02-08): the TCPA's restrictions on "artificial or prerecorded voice" encompass "current AI technologies that generate human voices", so such calls require the called party's prior express consent. Per WSGR, the ruling says the TCPA allows no carve-out for technologies that "purport to provide the equivalent of a live agent". — [SECONDARY; fcc.gov returned 403] — [Mayer Brown, 2024-02-27](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/02/fcc-declares-authority-and-intent-to-regulate-ai-generated-calls-under-the-tcpa); [WSGR](https://wsgr.com/en/insights/fcc-rules-ai-generated-voices-are-artificial-under-the-tcpa.html); [FCC document page](https://www.fcc.gov/node/261899), accessed 2026-10-23.

### Recording and transcribing calls

#### Takeaway
New York and federal law are one-party consent, so the brokerage, as a party, may record its own calls. Calls to people in all-party-consent states (Pennsylvania, Massachusetts, California, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Michigan, Montana, Nevada, New Hampshire, Washington, arguably Delaware) need everyone's consent. Connecticut's civil statute also requires consent or a recorded warning for telephone recording. An AI vendor that can reuse call content may be treated as a separate eavesdropper (Ambriz v. Google). A recording/AI announcement at the start of every call is the only design that works everywhere.

#### Cited Findings
- NY Penal Law §250.00 (rev. 2014-09-22). "'Wiretapping' means the intentional overhearing or recording of a telephonic or telegraphic communication by a person other than a sender or receiver thereof, without the consent of either the sender or receiver, by means of any instrument, device or equipment." "'Mechanical overhearing of a conversation' means the intentional overhearing or recording of a conversation or discussion, without the consent of at least one party thereto, by a person not present thereat, by means of any instrument, device or equipment." — [PRIMARY] — [nysenate.gov, Penal Law §250.00](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/250.00), accessed 2026-10-23. §250.05 makes eavesdropping (unlawful wiretapping or mechanical overhearing) a class E felony. — [CLAIMED; page not opened].
- 18 U.S.C. §2511(2)(d): not unlawful for a non-government person to intercept "where such person is a party to the communication or where one of the parties to the communication has given prior consent to such interception unless such communication is intercepted for the purpose of committing any criminal or tortious act in violation of the Constitution or laws of the United States or of any State". — [PRIMARY] — [LII, 18 U.S.C. 2511](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2511), accessed 2026-10-23.
- All-party states for telephone recording, per the Reporters Committee: California, Delaware (conflicting laws), Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts ("a felony to use a device to hear or record any telephone call unless all parties give their consent"), Michigan, Montana, Nevada, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania, Washington. New Jersey, Vermont and New York are one-party. Connecticut's criminal law is one-party, but "The state also imposes civil penalties for recording telephone calls without first obtaining all parties' consent". — [SECONDARY, undated] — [RCFP, telephone recording](https://www.rcfp.org/reporters-recording-sections/telephone-and-electronic-communications/), accessed 2026-10-23.
- Conn. Gen. Stat. §52-570d: the three ways to comply are written consent of all parties, verbal notification recorded at the start, or an automatic tone warning (described as every 15 seconds). Remedies include actual damages, costs and attorney's fees. — [SECONDARY; cga.ct.gov refused connection and Justia returned 403] — [RCFP Connecticut](https://www.rcfp.org/reporters-recording-guide/connecticut/); [CT AG opinion 1991](https://portal.ct.gov/ag/opinions/1991-formal-opinions/honorable-larry-r-meachum-department-of-correction-1991039-formal-opinion-attorney-general-of-connec), accessed 2026-10-23.

#### Inferences
- Requirements:
  - (1) Every call, inbound or outbound, opens with "this call is recorded and transcribed by an automated assistant for [Brokerage]". This satisfies §399-z for telemarketing, the CT civil rule (recorded verbal notice), all-party states and Ambriz-type consent.
  - (2) Vendor contracts bar the voice/transcription provider from using call content for its own purposes, including training, so it stays the brokerage's tool and is not a "capable" third party.
  - (3) Look up the callee's area code or state and refuse to record without consent when it is unknown.
