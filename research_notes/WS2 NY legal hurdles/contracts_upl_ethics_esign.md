## Key question 1 — Unauthorized practice of law: may a brokerage, a company, or software draft and present a contract of sale?

### Takeaway
New York lets a non-lawyer broker fill in the blanks of a simple, bar-approved form, or any simple form that is expressly made subject to each party's attorney's approval, as an incident of brokerage and with no separate charge; anything beyond that (devising terms, tailoring contingencies, explaining legal effect, discouraging the parties from consulting a lawyer, or charging for the document) is unauthorized practice of law and, for a licensee, grounds for revocation as "untrustworthiness." A company that is "in the business of preparing" instruments affecting real estate for compensation falls under Judiciary Law §495(3) (corporations) and §484 (individuals), and New York's courts treat document preparation for others by a non-lawyer business as the practice of law; no New York authority found in this research carves out software.

### Cited Findings
- [PRIMARY] Judiciary Law §484 (text as displayed 2026-10-23, revision shown 2014-09-22): "No natural person shall ask or receive, directly or indirectly, compensation for ... preparing deeds, mortgages, assignments, discharges, leases or any other instruments affecting real estate ... or make it a business to practice for another as an attorney ... unless he has been regularly admitted to practice, as an attorney or counselor, in the courts of record in the state." — [nysenate.gov JUD §484](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/484)
- [PRIMARY] Judiciary Law §478 (revision 2014-09-22): "It shall be unlawful for any natural person to practice or appear as an attorney-at-law ... to hold himself or herself out to the public as being entitled to practice law as aforesaid ... to assume, use, or advertise the title of lawyer, or attorney and counselor-at-law ... to furnish attorneys or counsel or an attorney and counsel to render legal services ... without having first been duly and regularly licensed and admitted." — [nysenate.gov JUD §478](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/478)
- [PRIMARY] Judiciary Law §495 (corporations and voluntary associations), subd. 1: "No corporation or voluntary association shall ... (c) hold itself out to the public as being entitled to practice law, or to render legal services or advice, nor (d) furnish attorneys or counsel, nor (e) render legal services of any kind ... in any other way or manner ..." Subd. 3: "No voluntary association or corporation shall ask or receive directly or indirectly, compensation for preparing deeds, mortgages, assignments, discharges, leases, or any other instruments affecting real estate ... Any association or corporation violating the provisions of this subdivision is guilty of a misdemeanor." Subd. 2 penalties: fine up to $5,000 for the entity, and "every officer, trustee, director, agent or employee ... who directly or indirectly engages in any of the acts prohibited ... or assists such corporation ... is guilty of a misdemeanor. The fact that such officer ... shall be a duly and regularly admitted attorney-at-law, shall not be held to permit or allow any such corporation ... to do the acts so prohibited nor shall such fact be a defense." Subd. 5 exempts a corporation "lawfully engaged in the examination and insuring of titles to real property, in the preparation of any deeds, mortgages ... insofar as such instruments are necessary to the examination and insuring of titles," but adds: "no corporation shall be permitted to render any services which cannot lawfully be rendered by a person not admitted to practice law in this state nor to solicit directly or indirectly professional employment for a lawyer." — [nysenate.gov JUD §495](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/495)
- [PRIMARY] Judiciary Law §485-a makes a violation of §§478, 484, 486 or 495 a class E felony when the person "falsely holds himself or herself out as a person licensed to practice law in this state" (or as able to provide attorney-only services) and "causes another person to suffer monetary loss or damages exceeding one thousand dollars" or other material damage to a legal right (page revision 2014-09-22). — [nysenate.gov JUD §485-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/485-A)
- [PRIMARY] NY Department of State, Legal Memorandum LI04, "Real Estate Brokers and Salespersons and the Unauthorized Practice of Law" (dated February 14, 2020): brokers "have long been permitted" to draft "simple" contracts as part of brokerage, but may not insert "any provision which requires the exercise of legal expertise," may not devise purchase-money mortgage or other legal terms "beyond the general description of the property, the price, and the mortgage to be assumed or given," must "refrain, even with respect to these approved contracts, from providing legal advice to their clients," may not "discourage the parties from seeking advice from their attorneys," may not add provisions to approved forms "unless the entire contract is conditioned on each party's attorney's review and approval," may prepare contracts "only as an incident of the purchase and sale of real estate," and may not "charge a separate fee for preparing a contract, or share in attorneys' fees for preparing or reviewing contracts." It cites Judiciary Law §§478, 484, 485 (misdemeanor), 476-a (prosecution by the Attorney General or a bar association with leave of court), Real Property Law §441-c, Jemzura v. McCue, 45 A.D.2d 797 (3d Dept 1974), and AG Op. 96-F11. — [dos.ny.gov LI04](https://dos.ny.gov/node/23931)
- [PRIMARY] NY Attorney General Formal Opinion 96-F11 (November 14, 1996; pre-2024 but still the standard DOS says it applies, per LI04 2020), headnote: "Real estate brokers are not engaged in the unauthorized practice of law if they prepare purchase-and-sale contracts that expressly state the documents are subject to review by the parties' attorneys, or if they use forms approved by the appropriate organizations and do not insert any material requiring legal expertise. This limited privilege must be narrowly circumscribed." Operative rules: a broker or realtors' association "that prepares a simple fill-in-the-blanks purchase and sale contract can avoid the unlawful practice of law by including in the contract a condition making it subject to approval by each party's attorney. Alternatively, brokers can utilize a fill-in-the-blanks form that has been approved by a recognized bar association in conjunction with a recognized realtors' association. Such an approved form would only require that the real estate brokers fill in non-legal provisions such as the names of the parties, the date and location of the closing, a description of the property, the consideration for sale and any other relevant facts." The contract "should clearly and prominently indicate on its face that it is a legally binding document and clearly and prominently recommend that the parties seek advice and counsel from their lawyers prior to affixing their signature." "Brokers may not add provisions to the standard fill-in-the-blanks contracts unless they make the entire contract subject to and conditioned upon the review and approval of each party's attorney," and "may not charge a separate fee for preparation of the contract or share in the fees of attorneys." — [ag.ny.gov 96-F11 (PDF)](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/96-F11_pw.pdf)
- [PRIMARY, quoted in AG 96-F11] Matter of Duncan & Hill Realty, Inc. v. Department of State, 62 A.D.2d 690 (4th Dept), appeal dismissed 45 N.Y.2d 821 (1978): the court upheld revocation-level discipline (untrustworthiness and incompetence under RPL §441-c) of a non-lawyer broker who "prepared documents that included detailed mortgage terms he had devised." Quoted language (62 A.D.2d at 696-697): "the so-called 'simple' contract is in reality not simple. It is often the most important legal transaction that the average person will ever undertake--the purchase of a home ... The argument that the need for expediting such transactions justifies their consummation without reference to an attorney is specious ... The personal interest of the broker in the transaction and the fact that he is employed by one of the opposing parties are further reasons to require that, insofar as the contract entails legal advice and draftsmanship, only a lawyer or lawyers be permitted to prepare the document." At 701: "real estate brokers and agents must refrain from inserting in a real estate purchase offer or counteroffer any provision which requires the exercise of legal expertise ... A real estate broker may readily protect himself from a charge of unlawful practice of law by inserting in the document that it is subject to the approval of the respective attorneys for the parties. Moreover, a real estate broker or agent who uses one of the recommended purchase offer forms ... or one recommended by a joint committee of the bar association and realtors association of his local county, who refrains from inserting provisions requiring legal expertise ... has no need to worry about the propriety of his conduct." — [ag.ny.gov 96-F11](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/96-F11_pw.pdf); the DOS memo gives the Appellate Division date as 1976 while the AG and the reporter volume (62 A.D.2d) indicate 1978 — [dos.ny.gov LI04](https://dos.ny.gov/node/23931) vs [ag.ny.gov 96-F11](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/96-F11_pw.pdf)
- [PRIMARY, via AG 96-F11 fn 4] Later Appellate Division cases consistent with Duncan & Hill (all pre-2024; no later contrary authority found): Matter of Mulford v. Shaffer, 124 A.D.2d 876 (3d Dept 1986) (untrustworthiness where broker "inserted broad, legally significant contingency clause, failed to suggest agreement be reviewed by attorney, gave advice re legal effect of provisions and charged a fee for preparing documents"); Matter of Sorrentino v. Shaffer, 125 A.D.2d 956 (4th Dept 1986) (sustained where broker "discouraged purchaser from seeking legal advice, failed to use a form advising parties to seek legal advice, and failed to insert a contingency clause essential to protection of buyer's interest"); Matter of Tucci v. Dept. of State, 63 A.D.2d 835 (4th Dept 1978) (broker "drafted legal documents including a revised purchase offer and gave advice as to their legal effect"). — [ag.ny.gov 96-F11](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/96-F11_pw.pdf)
- [PRIMARY, via AG 96-F11] People v. Title Guarantee & Trust Co., 227 N.Y. 366 (1919): a corporation whose employees prepared a chattel mortgage and bill of sale "by filling in blanks in forms at the customer's direction as an incident to its regular business" did not practice law; the Court took judicial notice of "a widespread custom which has prevailed from time out of memory in this state" that laymen may draw simple instruments, while noting (dictum) that what counts as a simple instrument must be decided case by case; a 4-3 decision with Cardozo dissenting. — [ag.ny.gov 96-F11](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/96-F11_pw.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] Sussman v. Grado, 192 Misc. 2d 628, 746 N.Y.S.2d 548 (Dist. Ct. Nassau Co. 2002) (pre-2024; trial-level): an independent paralegal who prepared enforcement papers for $45 "crossed the line between filling out forms and engaging in the practice of law by rendering legal services, Judiciary Law 478"; the court quoted Matter of Rowe, 80 N.Y.2d 336 (1992) ("The practice of law involves the rendering of legal advice and opinions directed to particular clients"), In re Roel, 3 N.Y.2d 224 (1957) ("[W]hen legal documents are prepared for a layman by a person in the business of preparing such documents, that person is practicing law whether the documents be prepared in conformity with the law of New York or any other law"), and El Gemayel v. Seaman, 72 N.Y.2d 701 (1988) (§484 protects the public from "the dangers of legal representation and advice given by persons not trained, examined and licensed for such work"); the court also held the conduct a deceptive practice under General Business Law §349 and awarded treble damages, and referred the matter to the District Attorney and Attorney General. — [Sussman v. Grado (course copy, PDF)](https://openlab.citytech.cuny.edu/coughlinlaw1101f22/files/2022/11/Sussman-v-Grado-edited-NY-2002-UPL-serious-conseqs-for-para.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] NYSBA Ethics Op. 1082 (January 8, 2016): a lawyer "may not provide legal services to individuals through a for-profit corporation" in which a nonlawyer has an ownership or controlling interest; the Committee relied on Judiciary Law §495, Rule 5.5(b) (aiding unauthorized practice), Rule 5.4(a) (fee sharing occurs when the customer pays the company more than the company pays the lawyer, citing N.Y. State 942 (2012)) and Rule 5.4(b); ghost-writing without the lawyer's name does not cure the problem. — [nysba.org Op. 1082](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1082/)
- [PRIMARY] NY Rules of Professional Conduct (NYSBA edition "as amended through July 1, 2026"), Rule 5.5: "A lawyer shall not practice law in a jurisdiction in violation of the regulation of the legal profession in that jurisdiction, or assist another in doing so." Comment [1]: the Rule "also prohibits a lawyer from aiding a nonlawyer in the unauthorized practice of law"; Comment [2]: "The definition of the 'practice of law' is established by law." — [NYSBA Rules PDF (2026)](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/NYSBA-NY-Rules-of-Professional-Conduct-as-amended-through-July-1-2026-1.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] Insurance Law §2113(c): nothing in the Insurance Law "authorizes conduct prohibited by Judiciary Law sections 484 or 495," including the unauthorized practice of law (page revision 2014-10-03). — [nysenate.gov ISC §2113](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ISC/2113)
- [SECONDARY] The Attorney General has used UPL theories against a non-lawyer document-preparation business ("Legal Docs By Me, LLC," Watertown): a notice of proposed litigation, cease-and-desist letter and subpoena were reported; the article page returned HTTP 503 on 2026-10-23 so date and outcome are unverified. — [cnybj.com](https://www.cnybj.com/ny-ag-seeks-to-shut-down-watertown-legal-document-firm-for-unauthorized-practice-of-law)
- [SECONDARY] Pennsylvania (not NY) bar guidance treats online document preparation as UPL unless by a licensed lawyer, and the Reynoso bankruptcy-software case found UPL by a software provider; cited only to show other jurisdictions' direction. — [Communications of the ACM, 2013](https://cacm.acm.org/research/are-we-free-to-code-the-law)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The "attorney approval contingency" is a genuine, agency-recognized safe harbor, but it only works if the entire contract is conditioned on each party's attorney's approval; it therefore presupposes that both parties will have attorneys, which is the opposite of "the lawyer is out of the loop." A product built on this safe harbor cannot also tell parties they need no lawyer.
- [INFERENCE] A brokerage-operated system that fills in names, price, closing date, property description and deposit on a bar/realtor-approved form, with the mandated legends, and with no separate fee, is within the Duncan & Hill/AG/DOS privilege. A system that selects or tailors contingencies (financing, inspection, sale-of-home, mortgage terms), writes riders, or explains the legal effect of clauses does what Mulford, Tucci and Sorrentino were disciplined for.
- [INFERENCE] Because §495(3) targets compensation for preparing "any other instruments affecting real estate" and §495(2) makes attorney status no defense, the attorney-founder's membership in the company does not launder the company's drafting; the drafting must sit in a law practice (then it is legal services, with all the ethics constraints in Key question 3) or be limited to the lay fill-in privilege.
- [INFERENCE] Pricing matters: the privilege exists only for documents prepared "as an incident of the purchase and sale" with "no additional charge." A line item or subscription tier for "contract generation" converts the activity into compensated document preparation under §484/§495(3).
- [INFERENCE] No New York court, agency or NYSBA opinion found in this research addresses AI or form-generation software as such; a court would most likely apply the existing functional test (filling in blanks at the user's direction vs. selecting and drafting terms, giving advice on legal effect, and charging), under which the medium is irrelevant.

## Key question 2 — The New York custom: binder/offer, seller's attorney drafts, the bar form, riders, "attorney review," the 10% deposit; Hudson Valley counties

### Takeaway
Downstate (and, on the only county-level evidence found, in Ulster County) the broker's role ends at a binder or offer; the seller's attorney drafts the contract on the bar-association Residential Contract of Sale with a rider, the buyer's attorney negotiates, and the buyer's down payment (customarily 10%) is held in the seller's attorney's escrow. New York has no statutory attorney-review period; the bar form instead carries a "CONSULT YOUR LAWYER BEFORE SIGNING" legend. The downstate form is a bar-association document (NYSBA Real Property Law Section with the NYC Bar), not a NYSAR form; its licensing terms for use in software could not be found and must be asked of NYSBA.

### Cited Findings
- [PRIMARY] NYSBA consumer guide "5 Steps for Buying a Home in New York State" (page live 2026-10-23, undated): "After the inspection is finished and the buyer has decided to purchase the home, the buyer will be asked to sign a binder"; "Once the attorneys receive the binder, the seller's attorney will typically draft the contract of sale (downstate New York) and contract negotiation commences"; "After the contract has been signed, the buyer will deliver the down payment to the seller's attorney to be held in escrow"; "After the closing, the title company will typically bring the deed and mortgage to the county clerk's office for recording." — [nysba.org 5 Steps](https://nysba.org/5-steps-for-buying-a-home-in-new-york-state/)
- [SECONDARY] NYSBA Real Property Law Section county-practice sheet for Ulster County (uploaded February 2020; the PDF URL returned 404 on 2026-10-23, so this is from the search snippet): "Contracts drafted by Attorney or Realtor: Seller's attorney; Who holds deposit? Seller's attorney; What is customary deposit amount? 10% of the sales price unless mortgage amount great[er] than 90%"; buyer usually pays for survey, required only for new construction. — [nysba.org Ulster-2.pdf](https://nysba.org/app/uploads/2020/02/Ulster-2.pdf)
- [SECONDARY] A Dutchess County decision (farm sale in Pleasant Valley), 2011 NY Slip Op 51420(U), enforced a binder as a contract where it "identifies the parties, describes the subject property, recites the essential terms and is signed by the party to be charged," even though a "more formal contract" was anticipated (nycourts.gov page blocked; from search snippet). — [nycourts.gov 2011_51420](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/3dseries/2011/2011_51420.htm)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The Hudson Valley is a split market: the NYSBA guide ties the seller's-attorney-drafts custom to "downstate," the Ulster sheet shows that custom reaching at least into the mid-Hudson, and the broker-prepared "purchase offer with attorney-approval contingency" model that Duncan & Hill (a Rochester-area case) and AG 96-F11 describe is the upstate pattern. Which of Columbia, Greene, Sullivan, Orange, Putnam, Rockland and Dutchess follow which pattern was not established by any source opened (see Gaps). Rockland and Putnam, being in the Hudson Gateway/Westchester orbit, almost certainly follow the downstate attorney-drafted model; Columbia and Greene are the likeliest to see broker-prepared contracts with attorney-approval clauses.
- [INFERENCE] "Attorney review" in New York means two things: (a) the bar form's legend and the practice of each side's lawyer negotiating the rider before signing (no statutory cooling-off period), and (b) in broker-drafted contracts, an express attorney-approval contingency. A product must choose one of those models; it cannot assume a New Jersey-style three-day review right.
- [INFERENCE] Because the downstate form was written by bar committees, is sold by NYSBA under per-user terms, and bears no general licence, embedding it in a commercial product needs NYSBA's (and the NYC Bar's) written permission; the "approved form" safe harbor for brokers also depends on using a form that a bar/realtor joint committee actually approved for broker use, which the NYSBA/NYC Bar contract is not (its legend presupposes lawyers on both sides).

## Key question 3 — A lawyer who is also a broker: Rules 1.7, 1.8, 5.4, 5.7, 7.1–7.3 and the NYSBA opinions

### Takeaway
New York's ethics committee has said for more than fifty years, and repeated in 2015, 2017, 2020, 2022 and 2024, that a lawyer may not be both lawyer and broker in the same real estate transaction, "with or without client consent, and whether or not the lawyer charges for legal services," because a commission earned only at closing is a per se non-waivable personal-interest conflict; the single relaxation (Op. 1015, 2014) applies only where the broker fee is fixed, prepaid and non-refundable and still requires informed written consent. Splitting legal fees with a non-lawyer co-founder, or delivering legal services through a company he co-owns, is barred by Rule 5.4 and Op. 1082; a lawyer may co-own a non-legal business such as a brokerage with non-lawyers, but Rule 5.7 then presumes the Rules apply to that business unless each customer gets a written "these are not legal services" notice.

### Cited Findings
- [PRIMARY] Rule 1.7(a)(2) (2026 text): a concurrent conflict exists if "there is a significant risk that (i) the lawyer's independent professional judgment on behalf of a client will be adversely affected by ... the lawyer's own financial, business, property or other personal interests"; Rule 1.7(b) permits representation only if the lawyer "reasonably believes" competent and diligent representation is possible, the representation "is not prohibited by law," and "each affected client gives informed consent, confirmed in writing." — [NYSBA Rules PDF (2026)](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/NYSBA-NY-Rules-of-Professional-Conduct-as-amended-through-July-1-2026-1.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] Rule 5.4 (2026 text): "(a) A lawyer or law firm shall not share legal fees with a nonlawyer," with exceptions only for deceased lawyers' estates, nonlawyer-employee profit-sharing/retirement plans, and fee divisions with out-of-state firms that lawfully have nonlawyer owners; "(b) A lawyer shall not form a partnership with a nonlawyer if any of the activities of the partnership consist of the practice of law"; "(c) ... a lawyer shall not permit a person who recommends, employs or pays the lawyer to render legal service for another to direct or regulate the lawyer's professional judgment"; "(d) A lawyer shall not practice with or in the form of an entity authorized to practice law for profit, if: (1) a nonlawyer owns any interest therein ... (2) a nonlawyer is a member, corporate director or officer thereof ... or (3) a nonlawyer has the right to direct or control the professional judgment of a lawyer." — [NYSBA Rules PDF (2026)](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/NYSBA-NY-Rules-of-Professional-Conduct-as-amended-through-July-1-2026-1.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] Rule 5.7 (2026 text): (a)(1) nonlegal services "not distinct from legal services" to the same person make the lawyer "subject to these Rules with respect to the provision of both legal and nonlegal services"; (a)(3) a lawyer "that is an owner, controlling party or agent of, or that is otherwise affiliated with, an entity that the lawyer ... knows to be providing nonlegal services" is subject to the Rules as to those services if the recipient "could reasonably believe that the nonlegal services are the subject of a client-lawyer relationship"; (a)(4) that belief "will be presumed ... unless the lawyer or law firm has advised the person receiving the services in writing that the services are not legal services and that the protection of a client-lawyer relationship does not exist," or the lawyer's interest in the entity is de minimis; (b) the lawyer "shall not permit any nonlawyer providing such services or affiliated with that entity to direct or regulate the professional judgment of the lawyer"; (c) "nonlegal services" are services "not prohibited as an unauthorized practice of law when provided by a nonlawyer." Comment [1]: "The risk of confusion is especially acute when the lawyer renders both legal and nonlegal services with respect to the same matter"; Comment [3]: the written disclaimer "should be made before entering into an agreement" and "a more detailed explanation may be required for someone unaccustomed to making distinctions between legal services and nonlegal services." — [NYSBA Rules PDF (2026)](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/NYSBA-NY-Rules-of-Professional-Conduct-as-amended-through-July-1-2026-1.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] Rule 7.1(a): "A lawyer shall not make a false or misleading communication about the lawyer or the lawyer's services," including one that "omits a fact necessary to make the statement considered as a whole not materially misleading." Rule 7.2(a): "A lawyer shall not compensate or give anything of value to a person or organization to recommend or obtain employment by a client, or as a reward for having made a recommendation." Rule 7.3(a) defines solicitation as a communication "initiated by or on behalf of a lawyer ... directed to a specific person the lawyer knows or reasonably should know needs legal services in a particular matter." — [NYSBA Rules PDF (2026)](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/NYSBA-NY-Rules-of-Professional-Conduct-as-amended-through-July-1-2026-1.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] NYSBA Op. 752 (February 22, 2002): after adoption of DR 1-106 (now Rule 5.7), the earlier bars on a lawyer acting where "the lawyer or his or her spouse acted as a real estate broker" in the same transaction "continue to apply"; digest: a lawyer who owns or operates an ancillary business "may still be barred from providing legal and nonlegal services in the same transaction, even with client consent." — [nysba.org Op. 752](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-752/)
- [PRIMARY] NYSBA Op. 845 (October 14, 2010): restates that N.Y. State 752 (2002), 493 (1978), 340 (1974), 291 (1973) and 208 (1971) hold "a lawyer cannot act as lawyer and broker in the same real estate transaction" and that under Rule 1.7 this is a nonconsentable conflict ("the broker's interest in closing the transaction interferes with the lawyer's ability to render independent advice," quoting 752; "a lawyer should not have a personal stake in the advice rendered," quoting 753). A lawyer-broker may share her commission with a referring lawyer only if that lawyer does not represent the buyer or seller, or remits/credits the fee to the client with informed consent; she "may not share her brokerage commission" with a referring attorney who represents the client and keeps the fee (Rule 8.4(a)). Communications to other lawyers made in a broker capacity are not "advertisements" under Rule 1.0(a). — [nysba.org Op. 845](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-845/)
- [PRIMARY] NYSBA Op. 1015 (August 4, 2014): a lawyer-broker "may represent the seller of real property and act as broker in same transaction if the lawyer's fee as a broker is fixed and non-refundable and any conflict is properly waived"; the Committee reasoned that prior opinions "assumed contingent commissions," and where the broker fee "is not contingent on the transaction being closed and is non-refundable" the earlier reasoning does not apply, but a personal-interest conflict under Rule 5.7 Comment [5A] remains and is consentable only with informed consent confirmed in writing; because the services were offered from the same office in the same matter they were "not distinct," so the Rules (including Rule 1.5's bar on excessive combined fees) govern both; footnote 5: separating the services (separate office, name, staff and written disclosure) could change the analysis. — [nysba.org Op. 1015](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1015/)
- [PRIMARY] NYSBA Op. 1043 (January 2015): "a lawyer may not act as a lawyer and a broker in the same real estate transaction ... with or without client consent, and whether or not the lawyer charges for legal services"; the rationale applies "as long as the lawyer has a financial interest in the real estate broker's commission, whether or not the lawyer is acting as a broker"; a lawyer may not take 25% of a broker's commission as a referral fee in lieu of legal fees even with informed consent. Op. 1015 is not mentioned. — [nysba.org Op. 1043](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1043/)
- [SECONDARY] NYSBA Op. 1177 (2017): a lawyer receiving a broker's commission paid by buyers "may not also serve as the lawyer for the buyers, even if the buyers are long-time clients and friends and have requested both kinds of services" (from search summary; opinion not opened). — [nysba.org Op. 1177 (search result)](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1117/)
- [PRIMARY] NYSBA Op. 1208 (November 16, 2020): a lawyer with "no financial interest" in a commission may accept referrals from her paralegal who is also a broker, subject to a fact-specific Rule 1.7(a)(2) risk assessment (volume of referrals, relationship), informed written consent under 1.7(b) if a significant risk exists, Rule 5.4(c) (the paralegal may not direct advice), and Rule 7.3 (unprompted recommendations by the paralegal are solicitation attributable to the lawyer). Taking a share of the commission "would create a per se conflict that cannot be waived." — [nysba.org Op. 1208](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1208/)
- [PRIMARY] NYSBA Op. 1237 (March 3, 2022): inquirer is "a lawyer and broker who co-owns a brokerage separate from his law practice" (other brokers: his mother and a friend). He may not accept closing referrals from an agent in his brokerage where the agency and agent split the commission, "even if the lawyer waives, for the clients, the share of commission owed to the lawyer's agency": sharing in the commission "would therefore constitute a per se non-waivable conflict," and waiving the agency's share gives clients a discount that is "something of value" given for referrals in violation of Rule 7.2(a). Cites N.Y. State 916, 933, 1043, 1177, 1208, 845 and 1015. — [nysba.org Op. 1237](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1237/)
- [PRIMARY] NYSBA Op. 1252 (January 13, 2023): a New York lawyer who works only as a real estate broker and provides no legal services is outside Rules 1.7/1.8 for referral fees, but under Rule 5.7(a)(4) "must inform the client in writing that the services are not legal services and that client-lawyer protections do not apply"; Rules 1.9, 8.3 and 8.4 apply regardless. — [nysba.org Op. 1252](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1252/)
- [PRIMARY] NYSBA Op. 1273 (August 28, 2024): a lawyer may accept real estate clients referred by his firm's associate who is also a licensed broker only if the lawyer "does not share legal fees or commissions with the broker/associate and obtains the client's informed consent, confirmed in writing"; it restates Op. 1043 ("with or without client consent") and Op. 1237 ("per se non-waivable"), and cites 1015, 916 and 933 "in support of the same prohibition." It does not address whether a lawyer may own a brokerage. — [nysba.org Op. 1273](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1273-referral-of-real-estate-matters-to-lawyer-from-real-estate-broker-who-is-also-employed-by-lawyer-as-an-associate/)
- [PRIMARY] Op. 1082 (2016): see Key question 1; legal services may not be delivered through a nonlawyer-owned for-profit company (Rules 5.4(a), 5.4(b), 5.5(b); Judiciary Law §495). — [nysba.org Op. 1082](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1082/)
- [PRIMARY] Real Property Law §442 (revision 2022-08-19): "No real estate broker shall pay any part of a fee, commission or other compensation received by the broker" to anyone other than a licensed salesperson associated with the broker, another licensed broker, an out-of-state broker, or an unlicensed corporation/LLC "if each shareholder or member is individually associated with the broker as a licensed associate broker or salesperson"; a broker may credit part of the compensation to the buyer, seller, landlord or tenant, but not for performing licensed activity. — [nysenate.gov RPP §442](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442)
- [SECONDARY] NYCLA Op. 685 (1991) addressed dual-practice conflicts (not opened). — [nycla.org](https://www.nycla.org/resource/ethics-opinion/ethics-opinion-685-1991-dual-practice-conflict-of-interest/)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Current status of the opinions: the flat prohibition (208, 291, 340, 493, 752, 753, 845, 916, 919, 933, 1043, 1177, 1237, 1273) is the governing line; Op. 1015 has not been withdrawn but every later opinion cites it only for the general rule and none applies its fixed-fee exception, so it should be treated as a narrow, consent-dependent exception that a disciplinary committee might not honour. A flat, prepaid, non-refundable brokerage fee is, however, exactly the pricing model this venture contemplates, so Op. 1015 is the one opinion that could matter; it still requires separation or full application of the Rules, written informed consent, and a reasonable combined fee.
- [INFERENCE] Op. 1043's phrase "as long as the lawyer has a financial interest in the real estate broker's commission, whether or not the lawyer is acting as a broker" means the attorney-founder, as a brokerage owner, is disqualified from being either party's lawyer in any deal on which the brokerage earns a contingent fee, even if someone else is the licensee of record and even if his legal work is free.
- [INFERENCE] Co-ownership of the brokerage with the non-lawyer son-in-law is not itself an ethics violation (Op. 1237's inquirer co-owned a brokerage with non-lawyers and the Committee did not object to ownership), but (a) Rule 5.4(a)/(d) and Op. 1082 bar routing any legal fee or legal service through that company, (b) Rule 5.7(a)(3)-(4) requires a written "not legal services" notice to every customer, and (c) Rule 5.7(b) bars the non-lawyer co-owner from directing the lawyer's professional judgment.
- [INFERENCE] Revenue sharing with the non-lawyer co-founder has two independent constraints: Rule 5.4(a) for any legal fees, and RPL §442 for brokerage compensation. Profit distributions to an equity owner of a licensed corporate brokerage are not literally a "payment of part of a commission," but whether DOS accepts an unlicensed equity owner in a licensed brokerage entity was not verified here (see Gaps).
- [INFERENCE] Advertising: no opinion found squarely addresses a brokerage advertising its principal's attorney status. Rule 7.1(a) (misleading omissions), Rule 5.7(a)(4) (the presumption that customers think they are getting legal services) and Op. 1015 fn 5 (separate name, office and staff) together point one way: marketing the brokerage as "run by a lawyer" invites the presumption that legal protections apply and undermines any claim that the services are "distinct."

- No NYSBA opinion was found on brokerage advertising that mentions the owner's law licence, or on a lawyer co-owning a brokerage with a non-lawyer as such (Op. 1237 assumes it without discussion). Rule 1.8 text was not extracted; Op. 845 applies Rule 1.8(f) to third-party referral payments.

## Key question 4 — If the brokerage's software drafts and the attorney-founder reviews: whose lawyer is he, may the buyer be told "you don't need a lawyer," and what exposure follows?

### Takeaway
He can be the seller's attorney only in a deal on which neither he nor his brokerage earns a commission; he can be the broker (or brokerage owner) only if he gives no legal advice to anyone in that deal and the company's drafting stays inside the lay fill-in privilege; he cannot be "neither" while reviewing contracts for the parties, because review for a party is legal services. Telling a buyer they need no lawyer is specifically the conduct DOS treats as untrustworthiness, and a seller's lawyer may give an unrepresented buyer no advice except to get counsel (Rule 4.3). Exposure runs from licence revocation and misdemeanour/felony liability to GBL §349 treble damages and malpractice claims.

### Cited Findings
- [PRIMARY] Rule 4.3 (2026 text): "In communicating on behalf of a client with a person who is not represented by counsel, a lawyer shall not state or imply that the lawyer is disinterested ... The lawyer shall not give legal advice to an unrepresented person other than the advice to secure counsel if the lawyer knows or reasonably should know that the interests of such person are or have a reasonable possibility of being in conflict with the interests of the client." Comment [1]: an unrepresented person "might assume that a lawyer is disinterested in loyalties or is a disinterested authority on the law even when the lawyer represents a client." — [NYSBA Rules PDF (2026)](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/NYSBA-NY-Rules-of-Professional-Conduct-as-amended-through-July-1-2026-1.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] DOS LI04 (2020): brokers may not "discourage the parties from seeking advice from their attorneys" and may not provide legal advice even when using approved contracts. — [dos.ny.gov LI04](https://dos.ny.gov/node/23931)
- [PRIMARY, via AG 96-F11] Sorrentino v. Shaffer, 125 A.D.2d 956 (4th Dept 1986): untrustworthiness sustained where the broker "discouraged purchaser from seeking legal advice." Tucci, 63 A.D.2d 835: UPL where the broker "gave advice as to their legal effect." — [ag.ny.gov 96-F11](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/96-F11_pw.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] Op. 1043 (2015): the prohibition applies "whether or not the lawyer charges for legal services" and "as long as the lawyer has a financial interest in the real estate broker's commission, whether or not the lawyer is acting as a broker." — [nysba.org Op. 1043](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1043/)
- [PRIMARY] Rule 5.7(a)(1) and Comment [1]: where legal and nonlegal services in the same matter "are so closely entwined that they cannot be distinguished," all the Rules apply to both. — [NYSBA Rules PDF (2026)](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/NYSBA-NY-Rules-of-Professional-Conduct-as-amended-through-July-1-2026-1.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] Judiciary Law §495(2): officers and employees who engage in or assist corporate UPL commit a misdemeanour and attorney status "shall not be ... a defense"; §495(3): corporate compensation for preparing instruments affecting real estate is a misdemeanour. — [nysenate.gov JUD §495](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/495)
- [PRIMARY] Judiciary Law §485-a: class E felony where a false holding-out as licensed causes loss over $1,000 or other material damage. — [nysenate.gov JUD §485-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/485-A)
- [PRIMARY] DOS LI04: Judiciary Law §485 makes §§478/484 violations a misdemeanour; §476-a allows the Attorney General, or a bar association with leave of the Supreme Court, to sue; RPL §441-c is the licence-discipline hook. — [dos.ny.gov LI04](https://dos.ny.gov/node/23931)
- [PRIMARY] Sussman v. Grado (2002): non-lawyer document preparation held a deceptive practice under GBL §349 with treble damages, and referred to prosecutors. — [Sussman v. Grado (PDF)](https://openlab.citytech.cuny.edu/coughlinlaw1101f22/files/2022/11/Sussman-v-Grado-edited-NY-2002-UPL-serious-conseqs-for-para.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] Op. 1082 (2016): a company-employed lawyer's unnamed drafting for the company's customers does not avoid the UPL/fee-sharing problem; failing to disclose the lawyer's participation may also violate Rule 8.4(c). — [nysba.org Op. 1082](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1082/)
- [PRIMARY] Duncan & Hill (via AG): "The personal interest of the broker in the transaction and the fact that he is employed by one of the opposing parties are further reasons to require that ... only a lawyer or lawyers be permitted to prepare the document." — [ag.ny.gov 96-F11](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/96-F11_pw.pdf)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The three possible roles resolve as follows. (1) Seller's attorney: permitted only if neither he nor any entity he has a financial interest in earns a commission on that sale (Op. 1043/1237); then his review is legal services, billed by his law practice, with no fee-sharing to the company (Rule 5.4) and Rule 4.3 limits toward the unrepresented buyer. (2) Broker/owner: he may not give legal advice to either side in that deal (DOS LI04), the company's document may not go beyond the fill-in privilege, and each customer must receive the Rule 5.7(a)(4) written notice that no legal services are being provided; his "review" can be a quality check of filled-in facts, not legal review. (3) "Neither" is not available: reviewing a contract for a party's benefit is legal advice "directed to particular clients" (Matter of Rowe, quoted in Sussman), and doing so for a company customer would be legal services delivered through a non-lawyer-owned company (Op. 1082).
- [INFERENCE] "You don't need a lawyer" is independently fatal in each role: as broker it is the Sorrentino conduct; as seller's lawyer it is advice to an unrepresented adverse party contrary to Rule 4.3; and as a company statement it contradicts the legend the AG says the contract must carry.
- [INFERENCE] Malpractice: a lawyer who reviews the contract "for the deal" invites both parties to rely on him; New York generally limits legal-malpractice claims to clients and near-privity relationships, but a buyer told to rely on the company's review has a plausible argument that a relationship was created, and the GBL §349 route in Sussman does not need privity at all. The malpractice-insurance position of a lawyer acting through a brokerage is a practical, not legal, unknown (see Gaps).

- No New York decision on malpractice or negligent-misrepresentation liability of a lawyer-broker to the counter-party was located in this session; the near-privity doctrine (Prudential v. Dewey; Credit Alliance line) is [CLAIMED] from general knowledge.
- Malpractice-insurer underwriting of lawyers who also broker (exclusions for "business enterprise" activities) was not researched.

## Key question 5 — Title and closing roles: can the brokerage or its principals act as title agent or closing attorney?

### Takeaway
A lawyer may represent a client and act as that client's title agent in the same matter (Insurance Law §2113(e)) provided the client is told they need not use him; a broker or any "affiliated person" who owns or controls a title agency may refer customers only with the written affiliated-business disclosures of §2113(d) and 11 NYCRR 35.5 (and RESPA's affiliated-business rules), may never require use, and may receive only a return on ownership; rebates and inducements are banned by §6409(d) with penalties up to ten times the amount. Regulation 208 (11 NYCRR 228) is in force except for the closer-fee and ancillary-search-cap provisions the First Department annulled in January 2019; whether the Court of Appeals later ruled was not verified.

### Cited Findings
- [PRIMARY] Insurance Law §2113 (page revision 2014-10-03): (a) commissions for acting as title agent may be paid "except to a licensed title insurance agent" only to licensees; (b) a written good-faith estimate of premium, fees and the agent's commission must be given to the applicant (or the applicant's attorney); (c) nothing authorizes conduct prohibited by Judiciary Law §§484 or 495; (d) a person acting for an owner, lessee or mortgagee (or prospective one) who, with a spouse, is a member, employee, director, owner, subsidiary or affiliate of a title agent may refer the applicant only in compliance with §6409 and with a written disclosure of the relationship, that use is not required, that payments reflect ownership not referral volume, that no referral quota exists, the expected compensation, and any RESPA disclosures; (e) "An attorney or law firm may represent a client and also act as the title agent in that matter, subject to applicable law"; (f) such an attorney is exempt from the (d) disclosure if the attorney advises the client that "the client is not required to use the attorney as the title insurance agent"; (h) the superintendent issues regulations and considers RESPA standards. The section does not mention real estate brokers by name. — [nysenate.gov ISC §2113](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ISC/2113)
- [PRIMARY] Insurance Law §6409(d): "No title insurance corporation, title insurance agent, or any other person acting for or on behalf of" one "shall offer or make, directly or indirectly, any rebate of any portion of the fee, premium or charge ... [or] pay or give to any applicant, or to any person, firm, or corporation acting as agent, representative, attorney, or employee ... any commission, any part of its fees or charges, or any other consideration or valuable thing ... as an inducement for, or as compensation for, any title insurance business"; recipients may not "knowingly receive" such things; penalties: $5,000, "up to ten times the amount of any compensation or rebate" for insurers/agents, five times for others, and for a residential (1-4 unit) applicant an amount up to the rebate received when the applicant knew it was a violation. — [nysenate.gov ISC §6409](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ISC/6409)
- [PRIMARY] 11 NYCRR 35.1 (Part 35, "Title Insurance Agents, Affiliated Relationships, and Required Disclosures"): "Affiliated person means an applicant for insurance or a person who acts as an agent, representative, attorney, or employee of the owner, lessee, or mortgagee, or of the prospective owner ... or the applicant's or other person's spouse, when the person or spouse directly or indirectly: (1) owns or controls a title insurance agent; (2) owns at least five percent of a title insurance corporation's securities ...; or (3) is under common control with or is controlled by a title insurance agent." — [LII 11 NYCRR 35.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/11-NYCRR-35.1)
- [PRIMARY] 11 NYCRR 35.5: an affiliated person who refers an applicant "shall, at the time of making the referral, provide the following disclosures pursuant to Insurance Law section 2113(d) in a separate writing to the applicant, and shall obtain written acknowledgement of receipt" (interest and benefit; "that the applicant is not required to use the services of the title insurance agent ... and that the applicant may shop around"; that compensation "is not related to the amount of title insurance business that person refers"; no quota; the amount or a range; whether the agent has non-affiliated business); (b) the receiving agent must ensure the disclosures are made; (c) "A title insurance agent that represents an applicant in another capacity, including as an attorney, shall not be subject to the disclosure requirements ... but shall advise the applicant that the applicant is not required to use the person as a title insurance agent." — [LII 11 NYCRR 35.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/11-NYCRR-35.5)
- [SECONDARY] 11 NYCRR 35.4 bars rebates or inducements for title business, including to attorneys acting for a party, and treats reciprocal referral arrangements as intended to evade §6409(d) (search summary; section not opened). — [Justia 11 NYCRR 35.4](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-york/title-11/chapter-ii/part-35/section-35-4)
- [PRIMARY] 11 NYCRR 228.2 (Regulation 208): (a) no title insurer or agent, "including any employee or independent contractor thereof, shall offer or make any rebate ... or pay or give any consideration or valuable thing" to an applicant or to any "agent, representative, attorney or employee of the actual or prospective owner ... as an inducement for, or as compensation for, any title insurance business ... regardless of whether provided as a quid pro quo for specific business"; (b) specifically bars meals, entertainment, gifts, outings, parties, "providing assistance with business expenses of another person, including ... rent, employee salaries, advertising ... computers," premises below market rent, "paying the fees or charges of any professional representing an insured as part of a real estate transaction, such as an attorney, engineer, appraiser, or surveyor," and "non-title services, without a charge that is commensurate with the actual cost"; (c) permits market-rate advertising, de minimis logo items, public promotional events and CLE open to the profession, all "reasonable and customary, and not lavish or excessive." — [LII 11 NYCRR 228.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/11-NYCRR-228.2)
- [PRIMARY] 11 NYCRR 228.5 (as published): (a) caps on ancillary charges (Patriot, bankruptcy and municipal searches at 200% of cost; recording $25 per document plus cost; survey inspection $75 plus cost; escrow $50); (d)(1) the insurer or agent "shall be responsible for payment of the title insurance closer and shall prohibit the closer from receiving any compensation directly or indirectly from the applicant." — [LII 11 NYCRR 228.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/11-NYCRR-228.5)

- [SECONDARY] A further First Department order in the same matter is dated December 26, 2019 (178 A.D.3d 611); its content was not retrieved. — [nycourts.gov 2019_09366](https://nycourts.gov/REPORTER/3dseries/2019/2019_09366.htm)
- [PRIMARY] RESPA Regulation X, 12 CFR 1024.14: "(b) No referral fees ... (c) No split of charges except for actual services performed"; (g)(1)(ii) permits "a payment by a title company to its duly appointed agent for services actually performed in the issuance of a policy of title insurance"; (g)(3): when "an attorney, mortgage lender, real estate broker or agent" receives payment for additional settlement services, "such payment must be for services that are actual, necessary and distinct from the primary services provided by such person. For example, for an attorney of the buyer or seller to receive compensation as a title agent" the attorney must perform core title services. — [LII 12 CFR 1024.14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1024.14)
- [PRIMARY] 12 CFR 1024.15 (affiliated business arrangements): safe only if the referrer gives the Appendix D written disclosure, "no person making a referral has required ... any person to use any particular provider" (an attorney may require a particular title agent only with disclosure at engagement, per (b)(1)(ii)/(b)(2)), and "the only thing of value ... is a return on an ownership interest," which excludes payments that vary with referrals. — [LII 12 CFR 1024.15](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1024.15)
- [PRIMARY] Judiciary Law §495(5): title companies may prepare instruments "necessary to the examination and insuring of titles" but may not render services a non-lawyer cannot or "solicit directly or indirectly professional employment for a lawyer." — [nysenate.gov JUD §495](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/495)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The brokerage (or its principals) can own a licensed title agency, but as an "affiliated person" every referral needs the §2113(d)/35.5 separate written disclosure with signed acknowledgement plus the RESPA Appendix D form, the customer must remain free to choose, and the owners may take only a return on equity; the attorney-founder, if acting as a client's attorney and title agent, has the lighter §2113(f)/35.5(c) duty but must actually perform core title work to be paid (RESPA 1024.14(g)(3)) and is again barred by Op. 1043 from being that client's attorney if his brokerage earns the commission.
- [INFERENCE] Bundling "free" or below-cost title, closing or legal services with a brokerage package would collide with 11 NYCRR 228.2(b)(8)-(9) (paying a professional's fees; non-title services below cost) and §6409(d) if any title-affiliated money flows in, and with RESPA §8 if a federally related mortgage is involved.
- [INFERENCE] New York has no statutory "closing attorney" role: title closers represent the insurer/agent (11 NYCRR 35.1(h), 228.5(d)), parties' lawyers attend for their clients, and lenders set their own settlement-agent requirements; the company cannot "be the closing attorney" without an attorney representing a party, which brings back Key question 3.

## Key question 6 — E-signature and remote notarization: what can be signed electronically, what needs a notary, and what needs paper or presence

### Takeaway
Under New York's ESRA an electronic signature "shall have the same validity and effect as the use of a signature affixed by hand" for everything except wills, trusts, powers of attorney and similar fiduciary documents, so listing agreements, agency disclosures, offers/binders, contracts of sale and riders can be e-signed, while deeds and mortgages must still be acknowledged before a notary to be recordable. Since January 31, 2023 that acknowledgment can be done remotely by a New York-registered electronic notary who is physically in New York (the signer can be anywhere), the session must be recorded and kept ten years, and county clerks "shall accept for recording" a notary-certified paper copy; remote ink notarization no longer exists. A power of attorney still needs paper, a notary and two witnesses. All eight Hudson Valley counties appear in CSC's e-recording network, with Dutchess, Ulster and Putnam confirmed by their own notices; lender and title-insurer acceptance of remote notarization in New York is set by 2023 underwriter bulletins that could not be read in full.

## Key question 7 — Can software "present" the contract (send, explain, negotiate) without an attorney or licensee, and what would New York authorities expect?

### Takeaway
Software may transmit a contract and collect factual inputs; the moment it selects or drafts terms for a party, explains legal effect, or steers a party away from counsel, it is doing what New York's cases and agencies define as the practice of law, and if a licensee or a lawyer-affiliated company operates it the conduct is attributed to them. The expected safeguards are the ones already written into the Duncan & Hill/AG/DOS standard and the Rules: a bar/realtor-approved fill-in form or an all-encompassing attorney-approval contingency, a prominent "legally binding — consult your lawyer" legend, no legal advice, no discouragement of counsel, no separate charge, a Rule 5.7(a)(4) written "not legal services" notice where a lawyer is involved, and a real lawyer on each side for anything beyond fill-in.

### Cited Findings
- [PRIMARY] AG 96-F11 and DOS LI04: the contract "should clearly and prominently indicate on its face that it is a legally binding document and clearly and prominently recommend that the parties seek advice and counsel from their lawyers prior to affixing their signature"; brokers "must refrain ... from providing legal advice"; "Nor may they discourage the parties from seeking advice from their attorneys"; no provisions may be added unless "the entire contract [is] subject to and conditioned upon the review and approval of each party's attorney"; no separate fee. — [ag.ny.gov 96-F11](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/96-F11_pw.pdf); [dos.ny.gov LI04](https://dos.ny.gov/node/23931)
- [PRIMARY, via AG] Mulford (gave "advice re legal effect of provisions") and Tucci ("gave advice as to their legal effect") were disciplined; Sorrentino ("discouraged purchaser from seeking legal advice") likewise. — [ag.ny.gov 96-F11](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/96-F11_pw.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] Matter of Rowe (quoted in Sussman): "The practice of law involves the rendering of legal advice and opinions directed to particular clients"; In re Roel: preparing legal documents for a layman "by a person in the business of preparing such documents" is practicing law. — [Sussman v. Grado (PDF)](https://openlab.citytech.cuny.edu/coughlinlaw1101f22/files/2022/11/Sussman-v-Grado-edited-NY-2002-UPL-serious-conseqs-for-para.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] People v. Title Guarantee (via AG): lay preparation is tolerated where the business "only filled in the blanks ... as an incident of its business" and "did not represent to the public that it practiced law." — [ag.ny.gov 96-F11](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/96-F11_pw.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] Rule 5.7(a)(4) and Comment [3]: written notice that services "are not legal services and that the protection of a client-lawyer relationship does not exist," given before the engagement, with "a more detailed explanation" for unsophisticated users. — [NYSBA Rules PDF (2026)](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/NYSBA-NY-Rules-of-Professional-Conduct-as-amended-through-July-1-2026-1.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] Rule 4.3: a lawyer communicating for a client may not imply disinterest and may give an unrepresented adverse party no advice "other than the advice to secure counsel." — [NYSBA Rules PDF (2026)](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/NYSBA-NY-Rules-of-Professional-Conduct-as-amended-through-July-1-2026-1.pdf)
- [PRIMARY] Rule 7.3(a) and Op. 1208: outreach by a non-lawyer affiliate recommending the lawyer is solicitation attributable to the lawyer ("stands in the shoes of the inquirer," N.Y. State 1150 (2018), quoted in 1208). — [nysba.org Op. 1208](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1208/)
- [PRIMARY] Judiciary Law §478 and §495(1)(c): holding out "as being entitled to practice law, or to render legal services or advice" is itself unlawful, independent of any particular document. — [nysenate.gov JUD §478](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/478); [JUD §495](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/495)
- [PRIMARY] Executive Law §135-c(9) and (8): a notary may refuse to proceed where the signer's competence or voluntariness is doubtful, and no business may require electronic-only notarization — i.e., a human judgment step remains at the acknowledgment stage. — [nysenate.gov EXC §135-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/135-C)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Permissible without a lawyer or licensee: hosting the form, collecting names, price, deposit, dates and property data, routing signatures (ESRA), tracking deadlines, transmitting the executed contract and deposit instructions. Permissible only through a licensed broker acting within the Duncan & Hill privilege: filling in a bar/realtor-approved purchase offer, with the mandatory legend and (for any added term) an attorney-approval contingency, at no separate charge. Requires a lawyer for the party concerned: choosing or drafting contingencies and riders, explaining what a clause means for that party, advising whether to sign, negotiating legal terms, and "reviewing" the other side's draft.
- [INFERENCE] Negotiation by software between the parties is the clearest line: Duncan & Hill's stated reason for the rule is that the broker "is employed by one of the opposing parties"; software owned by the brokerage that proposes compromise language to both sides is advising two adverse parties on legal terms at once, which neither a broker nor a single lawyer may do.
- [INFERENCE] Expected minimum design, if the venture proceeds: (1) the bar/realtor-approved fill-in form with the AG's legend; (2) a hard-coded statement that each party should retain their own attorney, with no copy anywhere implying a lawyer is unnecessary; (3) contract generation bundled, never priced; (4) any "explanations" limited to neutral, generic descriptions reviewed by counsel and framed as information, with a visible route to a lawyer; (5) Rule 5.7 written notice on every account the attorney-founder's company touches; (6) the attorney-founder never acting as a party's lawyer on a brokerage-fee deal (Op. 1043/1237) and never sharing legal fees with the company (Rule 5.4).

### Gaps
- There is no New York authority on whether neutral, non-party-specific plain-language explanations generated by software constitute "advice directed to particular clients"; the inference above rests on the broker cases and the Rowe definition.
- The NAR Code of Ethics (Article 13) and NYSAR guidance on UPL were not retrieved and are not relied on.
- Whether DOS would treat an automated system operated by a licensed brokerage as the broker's own act for RPL §441-c purposes has not been decided in any source found; the inference that it would follows from LI04's framing of UPL as "untrustworthiness and incompetence" of the licensee.
