## Key question 1 — Zillow and Trulia: FSBO posting, Listing Access Standards, broker and MLS feeds, costs, lead routing, Compass litigation

### Takeaway
Zillow takes for-sale listings in three ways: MLS IDX feeds (Zillow is itself a licensed brokerage and MLS participant since 2020–2021), broker "direct" feeds built on the broker's own MLS data (the MLS Grid "PDAP" / Broker Only Export route, which still requires MLS participation), and free owner-posted FSBO listings, which sit in a separate "Owner Listings" category. The Listing Access Standards (April 2025) banned publicly marketed listings not in the MLS within one business day; on 17 March 2026 Zillow dropped the timeline and the MLS requirement and replaced them with a "broadly accessible to the general public" test, launched "Zillow Preview" (brokerages send pre-market listings straight to Zillow), and Compass dropped its suit the next day. Zillow then sued Compass and the Chicago MLS (MRED) in May 2026; that case and MLS feed fights (MRED, Realtracs) ran through summer 2026. Buyer inquiries on an ordinary Zillow listing go to paying Premier Agents in the ZIP code, not to the listing agent; the listing agent gets the lead only in Preview (Contact Agent button), in Showcase (paid, roughly $300–$750 per listing at retail) or by buying Premier Agent share.

LAS revision, 17 March 2026
- The revised standards contain no timeline and do not require MLS submission. A listing qualifies if it is "broadly accessible to the general public in a manner that provides open access" — via the MLS, a public-facing website or a home-search portal. It fails if consumers must register with, or contact, the listing brokerage to see it (private networks, registration walls). Listings kept within a brokerage, or private at the seller's request, remain allowed; "pseudo-private" exclusive inventory marketed through a private network is not. The old list of public-marketing tactics (yard signs, social media, open houses) was removed. Zillow spokesperson: the revised standards "do not replace MLSs or change agents' obligations to the MLS in any way" and "do not encourage bypassing MLSs." [SECONDARY, quoting Zillow] — [Real Estate News, 19 Mar 2026 (updated 20 Mar)](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/03/19/zillows-simplified-listing-standards-make-the-mls-optional)
- Zillow's stated reasons: brokerage acquisitions that aim to "proliferate hidden listings", MLSs relaxing rules "to the detriment of consumers", and a more consolidated market. [SECONDARY] — same article.
- Zillow said in its newsroom that Compass abandoned its suit and that the standards remain in effect: https://www.zillow.com/news/compass-abandons-lawsuit-affirming-zillow-pro-consumer-listing-standards/ [PRIMARY; URL only, blocked]. Zillow disputes that it ever "banned" anything and objects to Compass's private listing network. [SECONDARY, search summary of that release and of CNN/Bloomberg coverage] — [CNN, 18 Mar 2026](https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/18/business/compass-zillow-lawsuit-listing-ban)
- Zillow said in August 2026 the LAS "remain in full effect across the Nashville region" after its Realtracs deal, i.e. the revised standards are still being enforced. [SECONDARY] — [Real Estate News, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/08/05/new-deal-gives-zillow-uninterrupted-access-to-realtracs-feed)

Zillow Preview (pre-market listings sent directly to Zillow)
- Launched 17 March 2026. Participating brokerages send pre-market listings directly to Zillow (not through MLS feeds); they appear in Zillow and Trulia search results labelled pre-market with brokerage branding. Launch partners: Keller Williams, HomeServices of America, RE/MAX, Side, United Real Estate; Zillow said other brokerages "that support broad listing access" can join; eligibility criteria and costs not published. Must fit local MLS rules (commonly MLS submission within one business day of public marketing). Buyers can save or "Take a Tour", which connects them to Zillow Preferred Agents; tours start when the listing goes active. While in Preview, "Contact Agent" inquiries go directly to the listing agent, unlike Zillow's usual lead distribution. If a buyer who booked a Preview tour closes with a Zillow Preferred Agent, Zillow pays the listing agent 10% of the buy-side commission from Zillow's own share. [SECONDARY] — [Real Estate News, 17 Mar 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/03/17/zillow-launches-preview-to-highlight-pre-market-listings)
- Access is "gated at the brokerage level"; 24 more brokerages were added by 25 March 2026. "Request a Tour" on a Preview listing routes to a Zillow Preferred buyer agent in the ZIP rotation, not the listing agent. [SECONDARY/CLAIMED; agent-marketing site citing Zillow's 17 Mar 2026 press release, HousingWire and Inman] — [EffectiveAgents, 22 Apr 2026](https://www.effectiveagents.com/resources/zillow-preview-explained-how-listing-agents-now-earn-10)

### Inferences
- For a licensed New York brokerage, the normal route onto Zillow and Trulia is the MLS IDX feed, with the MLS Grid PDAP "direct" feed as a backup; both presuppose MLS participation. The only non-MLS routes are owner FSBO posting (owners only, segregated in "Owner Listings") and Zillow Preview (direct to Zillow, but for pre-market listings, brokerage-gated, and still subject to MLS rules). I found no evidence of a general non-MLS agent-posting path since Zillow's 2020–2021 move to IDX feeds. [INFERENCE from the Metro MLS notice, Canopy/Real Estate News feed descriptions and the Clever FSBO guide]
- The March 2026 revision means a listing on the brokerage's own public website would satisfy the LAS test even before MLS entry, but satisfying the LAS is not the same as being displayed: Zillow still needs a feed. [INFERENCE]
- A one-broker brokerage appears eligible for the PDAP direct feed because the right attaches to any MLS participant and Canopy's page describes a broker-in-charge self-service process with no size test; the historical 200-listing threshold applied to Zillow's older XML direct syndication, which Zillow's IDX move largely replaced. Zillow's own page should be read to confirm. [INFERENCE from CLAIMED and MLS sources]
- On a standard listing the brokerage will not receive Zillow buyer inquiries unless it buys Premier Agent share, uses Showcase, or (for the pre-market window) Preview. Budget for Showcase at roughly $300–$750 per listing at retail if Zillow lead capture matters. [INFERENCE]

## Key question 2 — Realtor.com: MLS-only ingestion, non-MLS paths, costs, lead routing, FSBO

### Takeaway
Realtor.com ingests listings from MLS feeds (direct MLS-to-Move feeds, and ListHub, which Move owns) and has no owner-upload path; flat-fee brokers and FSBO guides agree a home must be in an MLS to appear. Its paid products (Connections Plus, Listing Toolkit, ReadyConnect Concierge, Market VIP, Spotlight Listings, Listing Manager) exist, but their current lead-routing rules and prices could not be read from its JavaScript-only pro site in this session.

- "Listing Manager" and "Spotlight Listings" are the products through which an agent would claim, enhance or promote their own listings on Realtor.com; their mechanics need to be checked on the site. [INFERENCE from product names]

## Key question 6 — Policy changes affecting visibility, 2025–2026: Clear Cooperation, delayed marketing, private-listing networks, state law

[CLAIMED/SECONDARY] — [Brokerless](https://brokerless.com/how-to-list-on-realtor-com); [Clever, Sep 2026](https://listwithclever.com/real-estate-blog/zillow-listing-fees/)
- Lead handling: Zillow routes ordinary buyer inquiries to Premier Agents (Clever, Sep 2026); Homes.com routes them to the listing agent (Homes.com Membership page); ListHub publishers must email leads to the broker-designated address (ListHub protections page). [SECONDARY/PRIMARY] — links as above.

### Inferences
- Realistic architecture for a licensed NY brokerage: (1) broker joins HGAR (or another REALTOR association) and OneKey; (2) listings are entered once in OneKey (manually in Matrix/Keystone, via a vendor listing-input tool, or via RESO Add/Edit if OneKey offers it — unconfirmed); (3) IDX/syndication carries them to Zillow/Trulia, Realtor.com, Redfin and Homes.com, with ListHub and OneKey's Keystone distribution covering the long tail; (4) a one-time MLS Grid PDAP broker feed to Zillow as insurance against feed disputes. [INFERENCE]
- Steps that remain human or semi-manual: REALTOR association and MLS applications (identity, licence checks); MLS listing input where no Add/Edit API exists, including photo order and open-house records; ListHub and MLS Grid broker account creation; claiming and verifying agent/broker profiles on Zillow, Realtor.com and Homes.com; purchasing Showcase, Premier Agent, Homes.com Membership/Boost or Realtor.com products (sales conversations, contracts); answering portal leads (phone and email, often within minutes to be competitive); correcting portal display errors through each portal's support; and seller certifications for any delayed-marketing or office-exclusive listing. [INFERENCE from the findings above]
- Where coverage straddles MLSs (Ulster, Columbia, Greene, northern Dutchess), dual MLS membership may be needed for full portal and cooperating-broker reach. [INFERENCE from the Redfin attributions and CGNDMLS description]

## Key question 8 — Secondary channels: Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, Nextdoor, Google Business Profile — is automated posting permitted?

### Takeaway
None of the four allows an unattended bot to post property listings: Facebook business Pages lost the ability to create real-estate Marketplace listings on 30 Jan 2023 (personal profiles can still post, and paid Housing-category ads, including feed-driven dynamic ads, are the sanctioned automation); Craigslist's terms forbid any software other than a browser or its app for posting; Nextdoor expects manual Business Posts (ads are the automated route); Google Business Profile's API does allow programmatic marketing posts (not listings), with a reported block for accounts with more than 10 locations.

### Cited Findings
- Meta announced on 29 Dec 2022 that from 30 Jan 2023 it "will no longer support the ability for all sellers to create vehicle and real estate/rentals listings" through business Pages; still allowed: person-to-person real-estate listings from personal profiles, promoting listings on a business Page, and Facebook ads (Special Ad Category: Housing). [SECONDARY, quoting Meta's Help Center notice] — [BAM, Jan 2023](https://nowbam.com/facebooks-new-rules-for-real-estate-listings/); [Inman, 4 Jan 2023](https://www.inman.com/2023/01/04/agents-baffled-by-confusing-new-facebook-property-listing-policy/)
- Meta Commerce Policies: "Facebook Marketplace is intended for consumer-to-consumer sales"; commerce content must comply with anti-discrimination law "including ... housing listings"; no real-estate-specific or automation clause on the page (no revision date shown). [PRIMARY] — [Meta Commerce Policies](https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/commerce/)
- Meta's "Dynamic Ads for Real Estate" run from a "Home Listings" catalog, an XML property feed registered in Commerce Manager. [SECONDARY; vendor integration docs] — [WP-ImmoMakler docs](https://docs.wp-immomakler.de/en/integrations/facebook/)
- Craigslist Terms of Use (last updated 16 Aug 2019): users agree "not to use or provide software (except our App and general purpose web browsers and email clients)" for "downloading, uploading, creating/accessing/using an account, posting"; "not to copy/collect CL content via robots, spiders, scripts, scrapers, crawlers"; misleading or unsolicited postings and account buying/selling prohibited; fees are non-refundable. [PRIMARY; pre-2024 but current text] — [Craigslist Terms of Use](https://www.craigslist.org/about/terms.of.use/en)
- Craigslist obtained a $1.3 million default judgment against an auto-posting software seller (craigslist v. Naturemarket, 2010). [SECONDARY; pre-2024] — [Eric Goldman blog, Mar 2010](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2010/03/craiglist_wins.htm)
- Nextdoor: agents claim and verify a Business Page, then share Business Posts ("Share an update" / "Share a local event") to the neighborhood newsfeed; paid Nextdoor Ads via Ads Manager. (Article dated 21 Oct 2019, updated 7 Apr 2022; Nextdoor's business site, © 2026, still hosts it.) [PRIMARY; dated] — [Nextdoor for Business](https://business.nextdoor.com/local/resources/tips-for-real-estate-agents-how-to-promote-your-listings-on-nextdoor-with-posts). Nextdoor's dedicated real-estate listings section (with MLS IDs) launched in 2017–2018 in ten metros. [SECONDARY; pre-2024, may have changed] — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/40678-neighborhood-social-network-nextdoor-launches-real-estate-listing-service)
- Google Business Profile API: third-party software can create, edit and delete local posts (Event, Call to Action, Offer) via `accounts.locations.localPosts` (v4) after OAuth registration; "Small businesses and large chains are eligible to create Posts"; product posts cannot be created via the API; page last updated 28 Aug 2026 and does not mark the endpoint deprecated. [PRIMARY] — [Google Business Profile APIs, Posts](https://developers.google.com/my-business/content/posts-data). A posting vendor reports the API error "This location belongs to a chain. Local Post are disabled for this location" for brands with more than 10 locations. [SECONDARY/CLAIMED] — [StoryChief help](https://help.storychief.io/en/articles/3832865-google-business-profile-local-post-disabled). Google requires the location to have passed verification before posting. [CLAIMED; vendor page] — [Ayrshare](https://www.ayrshare.com/solutions/google-business-api-error-403-location-not-verified-how-to-fix-it.md)

### Inferences
- Compliant automation on these channels is limited to: Meta Housing-category ads fed from a listing catalog; Nextdoor Ads; and Google Business Profile posts via the API. Organic Marketplace, Craigslist and Nextdoor newsfeed posts must be made by a person from their own account, and Craigslist expressly forbids tooling. Building scripts to post listings to Craigslist or Facebook Marketplace would breach their terms and risk account bans. [INFERENCE]
- Google Business Profile posts promote the brokerage; they are not a property-listing channel. [INFERENCE from the API's post types]
