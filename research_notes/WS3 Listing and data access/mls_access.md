## Key question 1: Which MLS(s) does a brokerage in Dutchess, Ulster, Orange, Columbia, Greene, Putnam, Sullivan and Rockland need after the 2021-2025 consolidations, and do listings in one appear in the others?

### Takeaway
Two MLSs cover the eight target counties in October 2026: OneKey MLS (service area by rule includes Dutchess, Orange, Putnam, Rockland and Sullivan, plus Westchester, NYC boroughs and Long Island; Mid-Hudson MLS was absorbed in January 2024) and the Hudson Valley Catskill Region MLS run by Hudson Valley Catskill Region REALTORS (the November 2024 merger of the Columbia-Greene and Ulster County boards; MLS still hosted at cgndmls.com on Rapattoni) for Columbia, Greene and Ulster. No OneKey-HVCRMLS data share was found; a OneKey participant may voluntarily enter out-of-area (e.g., Ulster) listings into OneKey. Elsewhere in the state, Global MLS (Capital Region, Flexmls; Southern Adirondack members now subscribe to it), the NYSAMLS statewide database (CNYIS Syracuse, WNYREIS Buffalo, UNYREIS/GENRIS Rochester and others, one shared Matrix database) and the REBNY RLS (NYC, not a Realtor MLS) are the main systems.

## Key question 2: What are the complete 2026 costs for a broker to join each MLS (association dues, MLS fees, setup, lockbox, ShowingTime), and per additional licensee?

### Takeaway
OneKey via HGAR is the only fully published 2026 price list: a one-broker firm pays about $1,356 up front in January (HGAR $606 dues + $100 processing + OneKey $650 start-up) plus $190/month per office, with each added licensee costing $606 HGAR dues (or $285 secondary) + $200 OneKey start-up + $225/yr, and SentriLock an optional $130/yr; LIBOR's 2027 local dues are $596 per Designated REALTOR and $596 per sponsored licensee. Upstate, BNAR/WNYREIS publishes $1,322 (Designated REALTOR, Jan 2026, incl. $500 application) plus MLS fees of roughly $53/month, GSAR/CNYIS charges a $1,500 participant fee plus $684/yr access, and Global MLS, HVCRMLS, GRAR and REBNY do not publish amounts.

## Key question 3: Can listings be entered and updated programmatically (vendor APIs), has any NY MLS enabled it, and what is the manual workflow?

## Key question 4: Who may enter and sign off a listing, what are the accuracy, photo, remarks and fair-housing duties, and which fields and documents are required?

## Key question 5: Do any NY MLSs admit non-Realtor brokerages, and can an attorney acting under RPL §442-f without a broker's licence join?

### Takeaway
No New York REALTOR MLS admits non-members: OneKey calls itself "a REALTOR-only service" and its Rule 202.1 requires the participant to be "a REALTOR of any Board and hold a valid state-issued Real Estate Broker's license", and NYSAMLS explicitly did not adopt NAR's non-member participation sections (rules effective Sept 2026); only the REBNY RLS (not an MLS) admits non-member firms at a separate fee. RPL §442-f exempts "attorneys at law" from the licensing article, but every MLS and association eligibility rule found is written around a state-issued broker's licence, so an unlicensed attorney cannot be a Participant; the DOS pages describing the attorney path to a broker's licence could not be retrieved.

## Key question 6: What do the rules say about limited-service, entry-only and flat-fee listings, and about offering compensation to buyer brokers after August 2024?

### Takeaway
NYSAMLS (upstate) has adopted NAR's optional limited-service (1.2.1) and MLS-entry-only (1.2.2) rules with a "Service Type" field, while OneKey's 2026 rule book has no limited-service or entry-only category at all; instead OneKey accepts exclusive-agency listings (flagged in a field), allows the listing agreement to authorise other brokers to "negotiate directly with the Principal" when the "Negotiate Through" field is set to "Seller", requires every listing to be showable with scheduling within 24 hours, forbids calling services "free", and bars denying participation "based on the level of service". Since 17 Aug 2024 no NY REALTOR MLS may carry any offer of compensation anywhere in a listing (OneKey fines $250 immediately and strips the field), compensation may still be offered off-MLS and on the brokerage's own website for its own listings, optional seller-concession fields exist, and any MLS participant working with a buyer must sign a written buyer agreement before a tour.

**Gap theme M2: Who may join in 2026, and the attorney route**

### Takeaway
NAR stopped requiring MLSs to demand association membership (Policy Statement 7.7 repealed at NAR NXT on 17 Nov 2025, effective 1 Jan 2026), making it a local choice. OneKey has kept the requirement: its 2026C rules and join page still require REALTOR membership plus a state-issued broker licence. An attorney acting under RPL 442-f without a licence therefore cannot be a Participant; but DOS issues an admitted attorney a broker licence on a paper application and $185, with no course, experience or exam. **Gap theme M4: Photo and listing-copy rights**

### Takeaway
Uploading photos and remarks to OneKey grants OneKey a licence and warrants that the broker holds the rights and the written consents; the photographer normally keeps copyright, so the firm needs a written licence from each photographer that covers MLS and portal use. Another broker's photos may not be used for marketing without that broker's prior written consent (OneKey 612.2) and, in New York law, without the copyright holder's written permission (175.25(b)(2)(iii)). Reusing photos outside the licence is costly: VHT v. Zillow ended in statutory damages per photo. OneKey has no virtual-staging rule beyond "accurately reflect"; NYSAMLS requires a "virtually staged" banner; DOS warned in November 2025 that AI-generated listing images may be misleading advertising; a New York disclosure bill (S9584) is in committee.

**Gap theme M5: Data output and AI rules (buyer side)**

### Takeaway
OneKey sends all data through MLS Grid, and MLS Grid's licence now carries a binding AI Use Addendum (file dated 12 Aug 2026). It allows AI only for (i) search, matching, comparisons and "conversational guidance" on IDX or VOW sites and (ii) generating the firm's own marketing content; everything else needs the MLS's written approval. It forbids "AI Training" without authorisation, and defines training to include vector embeddings or retrieval indices "that persist beyond a single user session"; data may not be retained by an AI tool beyond a single query. The licence lets the firm use its own listings "in any manner", treats an automated valuation as a "Comparative Market Analysis" for one specific client, bans derivative works without consent, and bans using MLS data for unsolicited contact. NAR itself has no Handbook AI rule. Costs: a free Matrix IDX widget; $20 a month OneKey data share through an IDX vendor; or $250 a month plus $20 per licence as the firm's own MLS Grid vendor.
