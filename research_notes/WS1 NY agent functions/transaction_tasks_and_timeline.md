# WS1 — NY agent functions: transaction tasks and timeline (Hudson Valley, New York State outside NYC)

Research date: 2026-10-23. Scope: what a listing agent and a buyer's agent actually do, step by step, in a New York residential sale outside New York City, who does each task today, with what tools, how long it takes, and a first-pass judgement on whether software could do it alone.

Labels used on every claim: [PRIMARY] = statute, regulation, court, MLS/association/vendor's own document, or official data; [SECONDARY] = practitioner explainer, trade press, brokerage page, or a search-result summary of a page I could not open ("snippet"); [CLAIMED] = a party's own assertion or an undated/marketing claim; [INFERENCE] = my reasoning from the cited material. "FLAG pre-2024" marks sources older than 2024 whose content may have changed.

## KQ0 — The New York (outside NYC) sequence and the legal frame every task sits in

### Takeaway
Outside NYC, the Hudson Valley follows the "downstate" custom: a non-binding accepted offer, a broker-prepared memorandum/binder sent to two attorneys, inspection before contract, a seller's-attorney-drafted contract with a customary 10% deposit held in the seller's attorney's escrow, lender/title work, and an attorney-run closing. Three statutes bound who may do what: Article 12-A of the Real Property Law (RPL §§440–443: licensing, agency disclosure, no commission suit without a license, attorneys exempt), the amended Property Condition Disclosure Act (mandatory 56-question PCDS since 20 March 2024), and the Department of State's broker regulations (19 NYCRR Part 175: records, listing-agreement disclosures, offer routing).

### Cited Findings
- Sequence, consumer explainer: inspection is "one of the most important steps"; after inspection the buyer signs a binder listing "the price, down payment, anticipated mortgage, realtor and attorney information, and expected closing date"; the seller's attorney "typically drafts the contract" (the article says this applies in "downstate New York"); after both sign, "the buyer typically delivers the down payment to the seller's attorney to be held in escrow"; the contract is "usually" contingent on the buyer getting a mortgage; a title report ordered for buyer, lender and insurer discloses liens, judgments, property-line issues, violations, permits and unpaid taxes; title insurance is bought for buyer and lender "at the buyer's cost"; at closing title passes by deed and the title company then records deed and mortgage with the county clerk. [SECONDARY; NYSBA Lawyer Referral Service, 19 Dec 2022; FLAG pre-2024] — [NYSBA, "5 Steps for Buying a Home in New York State"](https://nysba.org/?p=132946)
- Sequence, Westchester listing-agent explainer (HGAR territory, same customs as the Hudson Valley counties on OneKey MLS): after acceptance "the brokers prepare a memorandum" of the agreed terms and send it to buyer, buyer's attorney, seller and seller's attorney; the seller's attorney drafts the contract from the memorandum and sends it to the buyer's attorney "only after engineering inspections are complete"; the buyer signs and sends the contract plus the "customarily 10%" deposit to the seller's attorney, "usually by overnight courier", who holds it in escrow; the seller then signs; "only after receiving the executed contract can the buyer apply for a mortgage"; verbal acceptance to executed contracts "is typically one to two weeks"; until executed and delivered the acceptance is non-binding on either side. [SECONDARY; Bill Boeckelman, Coldwell Banker Realty, Dobbs Ferry; page undated, footer "© 2007"; FLAG pre-2024] — [Accepted offer, seller version](https://boeckelman.realgeeks.com/accepted-offer-seller-version/)
- Sequence and durations, Westchester buyer-side explainer (Howard Hanna | Rand Realty, © 2026): accepted offer is "not legally binding yet"; attorney review and contract prep 3–7 days; inspection "usually within the first week after acceptance"; contract signing with ~10% earnest money about 7–10 days after acceptance; mortgage application/underwriting 30–45 days; walkthrough 24–48 hours before closing; total "about 45–60 days" from accepted offer to closing. [SECONDARY, 2026] — [Rand Realty, "From Offer to Closing"](https://jeselle.randrealty.com/?p=79)
- NYC-only customs for comparison (mark NYC-only): about 10 business days from accepted offer to signed contract; deposit "at least 10%" held by seller's attorney; closing "another 30–75 days"; either side can walk until contract. [SECONDARY, NYC-only, © 2009–2024] — [Brick Underground](https://www.brickunderground.com/guides/how-to-buy/going-from-accepted-offer-to-contract); a NYC brokerage says 5–7 business days to signed contract and that all written and verbal offers are non-binding. [SECONDARY snippet, NYC-only] — [Hauseit](https://hauseit.com/are-real-estate-offers-binding-in-nyc)
- Upstate (non-Hudson-Valley) custom differs: an HGAR trade-press legal column states that brokers commonly prepare the contract upstate while in southern New York attorneys draft it. [SECONDARY, search snippet only; page returned a certificate error] — [Real Estate In-Depth, Legal Corner](https://realestateindepth.com/legal-corner-what-documents-are-brokers-and-agents-required-to-maintain). The DOS record-keeping rule itself contemplates both models: brokers must keep "the purchase contract or binder prepared by the broker" or, if the broker did not prepare the contract, the price and deposit amount. [PRIMARY] — [19 NYCRR 175.23](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/19-NYCRR-175.23)
- Binders: a 2005 appellate decision found a binder "subject to attorney approval" not enforceable and returned the down payment; a 2016 Kings County decision analysed a signed "Binder of Sale" (price, 10% deposit, closing date, formal contract to follow) with a different outcome; a 1979 NYS Bar Journal article calls binders a source of confusion and litigation. [SECONDARY snippets; FLAG old] — [2005 decision](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/3dseries/2005/2005_50616.htm); [2016 decision](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/3dseries/2016/2016_26055.htm); [Bar Journal 1979 PDF](https://www.bhpp.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Real-Estate-Binders-Lawyers-Beware.-51-NYS-Bar-Journal-550-November-1979.pdf)
- Licensing frame: Article 12-A "shall not apply to receivers, referees, administrators, executors, guardians or other persons appointed by or acting under the judgment or order of any court; or public officers while performing their official duties, or attorneys at law." [PRIMARY] — [RPL §442-f](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442-F). No person or entity may sue in New York to collect compensation for brokerage services unless it was a "duly licensed real estate broker or real estate salesperson" when the cause of action arose. [PRIMARY, revision shown 19 Aug 2022] — [RPL §442-d](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442-D)
- Agency disclosure (RPL §443, residential 1–4 family, condo, co-op): the listing agent must give the seller the NYS agency disclosure form and get a signed acknowledgment "prior to entering into a listing agreement"; a seller's agent must give it to the buyer at the first substantive contact; a buyer's agent must give it to the buyer before agreeing to act and to the seller at first substantive contact; signed acknowledgments kept at least three years, with a sworn declaration kept if a party refuses to sign. The statute uses but does not define "first substantive contact". Definitions: buyer's agent = "an agent who contracts to locate residential real property for a buyer" (or who finds a buyer and presents an offer); seller's agent = "a listing agent who acts alone, or an agent who acts in cooperation with a listing agent"; dual agent and designated sales agent also defined. [PRIMARY, revision shown 19 Aug 2022] — [RPL §443](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/443)
- DOS broker regulations: records of each residential transaction kept three years (names/addresses of seller and buyer; contract or binder prepared by the broker; commission paid; any Article 12-A document; the listing, commission or buyer-broker agreement). [PRIMARY] — [19 NYCRR 175.23](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/19-NYCRR-175.23). An exclusive residential listing must carry (≥6-point type, signed or initialled by the owner) the DOS explanation of "exclusive right to sell" versus "exclusive agency", and if the listing broker belongs to an MLS the listing agreement must give the homeowner the option of having all negotiated offers submitted either through the listing broker or through the selling broker. [PRIMARY] — [19 NYCRR 175.24](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/19-NYCRR-175.24)
- Property Condition Disclosure Act as amended (Chapter 484, Laws of 2023; effective 20 March 2024): the seller must deliver the PCDS to the buyer or buyer's agent before the buyer signs a binding contract, and a signed copy is attached to the contract; the listing broker must tell the seller of the obligation in time; the buyer's broker (or the seller's broker if the buyer has none) must tell the buyer of the right to receive one; the former $500 credit in lieu of the form is deleted; the form grew from 49 to 56 questions with seven new flood questions (FEMA 100/500-year floodplain, federal flood-insurance requirement, FEMA assistance, current flood insurance, elevation certificate, flood claims); liability is for actual damages where the seller wilfully gives untruthful answers; the statute no longer states a remedy for simply failing to deliver a complete PCDS; whether a buyer can waive delivery is unresolved. [PRIMARY-adjacent: NYSBA Real Property Law Journal article by Karl Holtzschue, dated 28 Feb 2024 (first published Dec 2023)] — [NYSBA](https://nysba.org/pcda-amended-500-seller-credit-deleted-and-additional-questions-added-to-pcds/); corroborated by [NYSBA news note](https://nysba.org/?p=163648) and [LI Land Use & Zoning blog, 5 Feb 2024](https://www.lilanduseandzoning.com/2024/02/05/disclosure-revolution-legislation-makes-property-condition-disclosure-statements-mandatory-adding-flood-risks-and-waving-farewell-to-the-500-credit/) [SECONDARY]
- Private well testing at sale is NOT law as of October 2026: S3038 (Hoylman-Sigal) "In Senate Committee Environmental Conservation Committee", referred 23 Jan 2025; S9622 (Rachel May) referred to Environmental Conservation 30 Mar 2026; both would add RPL §468 requiring every contract for property served by a private well to include water testing "as a condition of sale", with closing barred until both parties certify they reviewed the report. [PRIMARY] — [S3038](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S3038); [S9622](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S9622)
- Appraisal independence (federal): real estate agents are "covered persons" under Regulation Z §1026.42 (comment 42(b)(1)-1) and may not coerce or influence an appraiser's value; an agent's own market-value estimate is itself a "valuation" (comment 42(b)(3)-1); expressly permitted: asking the appraiser "to consider additional, appropriate property information" (including comparable sales), requesting further detail, and asking for error corrections. [PRIMARY] — [12 CFR 1026.42](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/42/)
- Commission payment: the broker's commission "is typically paid by the seller at closing" under the listing contract; New York brokers cannot lien the property; under the Commission Escrow Act (RPL §294-b) a broker whose listing contains the statutory notice may file an "affidavit of entitlement" with the county clerk before the deed is delivered, after which the seller must deposit the lesser of net proceeds or the unpaid commission with the clerk, and the broker must sue within 60 days. [SECONDARY; Cuddy & Feder, 21 May 2021; FLAG pre-2024] — [Cuddy & Feder](https://www.cuddyfeder.com/the-commission-escrow-act/). NYC-only practice description: the seller's attorney issues certified checks (or wires) to both brokerages at closing and brokers usually don't attend the closing; all commission must be paid to the brokerage, not the agent. [SECONDARY/CLAIMED, NYC-only, brokerage marketing site] — [Hauseit](https://hauseit.com/how-does-a-realtor-get-paid-when-you-buy-a-house-nyc)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Westchester explainers (Boeckelman, Rand) are the best available proxies for Hudson Valley practice because Westchester, Putnam, Rockland, Orange, Sullivan, Dutchess and Ulster are all served by the same association (HGAR) and MLS (OneKey), and the NYSBA explainer describes the same "downstate" custom; I found no Hudson-Valley-specific attorney explainer in the retrievable sources (see Gaps).
- [INFERENCE] The non-binding accepted-offer stage is the single most important structural fact for software design: no legally enforceable act occurs until the attorneys exchange a signed contract and deposit, so everything an agent does before that point (pricing, marketing, showings, offer intake, memorandum) is informational and logistical rather than legal.
- [INFERENCE] Columbia and Greene counties sit outside OneKey (third-party listing sites credit the "Columbia Greene Northern Dutchess MLS" as their data source), so MLS rules, lockbox vendor and showing tools there may differ from the OneKey counties; this needs a WS3 check. [SECONDARY basis: site disclaimers at e.g. https://wp.wps.neutrinoinc.com/search/real-estate-sales/copake-ny/]

## KQ1 — Seller side: chronological task inventory (listing agent)

### Takeaway
Of roughly thirty listing-side tasks, the ones with a legal gate are few and specific (agency disclosure, listing agreement with the DOS explanation, PCDS delivery, MLS entry under a participant broker, offer presentation duties, record-keeping); the ones that need a body on site are also few (photography/measurement, sign and lockbox installation, open houses, in-person showings, walkthrough); most of the rest — pricing analysis, MLS data entry, syndication, showing scheduling and feedback, offer intake and comparison, the memorandum to attorneys, date tracking, status updates, commission arithmetic — is paperwork and coordination that software already performs for agents today.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Tasks S7–S8, S11, S13, S15, S20, S22, S27–S28 are already executed by software for agents (MLS, ShowingTime, Offer Manager, forms platforms); the agent's contribution there is data entry and judgement, so these are the natural "software alone" set.

## KQ4 — Physical-presence tasks and how existing players handle them without an agent

### Takeaway

## KQ5 — Relationship- and trust-based tasks

### Takeaway
The measurable trust component is large: two-thirds of sellers and over 60% of buyers chose their agent by referral or prior use, and sellers rank reputation as a deciding factor; by contrast, "appraiser relations" is largely a myth under federal valuation-independence rules, which bar agents from influencing value and limit them to supplying information.

### Cited Findings
- Buyers: 43% found their agent via referral, 18% used one they had used before; sellers: 66% referral or repeat; 35% of sellers cited reputation as deciding; 91% of buyers would use the agent again. [SECONDARY summary, 8 Dec 2025; PRIMARY for 91% via NAR release 4 Nov 2025] — [Virginia Realtors](https://virginiarealtors.org/2025/12/08/key-takeaways-from-nars-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers); [NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/first-time-home-buyer-share-falls-to-historic-low-of-21-median-age-rises-to-40)
- Appraisers: agents are "covered persons" and may not cause value to rest on anything but the appraiser's independent judgement; they may provide "additional, appropriate property information". [PRIMARY] — [12 CFR 1026.42](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/42/)
- Attorney referral is part of the listing agent's post-acceptance script ("retain an attorney … now is the time"). [SECONDARY, Boeckelman]
- Cooperating-agent access depends on association/MLS membership for lockboxes (SentriLock/Supra distribution rules above) and on OneKey rules for offer handling. [PRIMARY vendor pages; SECONDARY snippet for OneKey rule]
- NAR's own advice to agents on justifying fees is to list services and let clients pick which to do themselves, and to gather testimonials and niches — i.e., a reputation strategy. [SECONDARY, NAR Magazine, 17 Nov 2023] — [NAR Magazine](https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/sales-marketing/are-you-worth-it-how-to-justify-your-compensation)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The trust tasks that matter in the Hudson Valley are referrals to local attorneys, inspectors (septic/well/radon), and contractors, plus credibility with cooperating listing agents when presenting an offer; the attorney-broker's own standing covers the first, a vetted vendor directory covers the second, and the third is the hardest for software (a listing agent deciding between two offers may discount an unrepresented/software-represented buyer) — no source quantifies this.
- [INFERENCE] "Appraiser relations" should be dropped from any list of agent value; what remains is logistics (access) and information (comps), both software-friendly.

## KQ6 — How listing agents and buyers' agents communicate, and what flows between them

### Takeaway
The channels are standardised: ShowingTime for requests, confirmations and feedback; email/PDF (or Offer Manager) for offers; the NYS agency disclosure at first substantive contact; PCDS delivery to the buyer's agent; and a broker-prepared memorandum to both attorneys at acceptance — after which the attorneys, not the agents, carry the contract. Compensation offers can no longer be posted on the MLS, so buyer-agent pay is now negotiated in the offer or via seller concessions.

### Cited Findings
- Showing requests, confirmations, instructions and feedback flow through ShowingTime between buyer's agent and listing agent/seller; feedback can be auto-requested and shared with the seller; listing activity reports aggregate it. [PRIMARY/SECONDARY] — [ShowingTime](https://www.showingtime.com/); [GAAR 2016](https://www.gaar.com/images/uploads/blog/showingtime-quickstart.pdf); [beycome 2026](https://www.beycome.com/faq/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/ShowingTime-How-to-1.pdf)

### Gap theme G1: who does what in Hudson Valley closings (NYSBA county sheets)

### Gap theme G2: contract-to-close timing and the attorney-approval contingency

### Takeaway
Upstate attorney-approval clauses are enforced as written (Court of Appeals, Moran v Erk, 2008): either attorney may disapprove "for any reason or for no stated reason" within the approval period, commonly three business days. Downstate-style contracts typically give the buyer 45 days to obtain a mortgage commitment and set the closing "on or about" a date that in practice moves up to 30 days either way.

### Cited Findings
- Moran v Erk, 2008 NY Slip Op 09255 (Nov 25, 2008): "Attorney approval contingencies are routinely included in real estate contracts in upstate New York"; "an attorney for either party may timely disapprove the contract for any reason or for no stated reason"; "no vested rights are created by the contract prior to the expiration of the contingency period"; rider: "This Contract is contingent upon approval by attorneys for Seller and Purchaser by the third business day …"; the form was approved by the Greater Buffalo Association of Realtors and the Bar Association of Erie County [PRIMARY, accessed 2026-10-23] — [Moran v Erk at LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/nyctap/I08_0176.htm)
- Attorney guide (Angiuli & Gentile LLP, Staten Island, May 29, 2022): inspection follows the signed offer and the contract is drafted "if all goes well with the inspection"; "the seller's attorney will draft a proposed contract of sale"; "the purchaser will typically have 45 days to obtain a mortgage commitment"; the closing date "will typically vary up to 30 days before and 30 days after" [SECONDARY, accessed 2026-10-23] — [Angiuli & Gentile guide](https://industrym.com/point-of-sale/)
- Court decisions quote 45-day commitment clauses measured from delivery of the fully executed contract to the purchaser or the purchaser's attorney, with short cancellation-notice windows (e.g., five business days after the commitment date in Dazzo v Kilcullen, 2d Dept 2008) [SECONDARY, search-result summaries; nycourts pages returned HTTP 403, accessed 2026-10-23] — [Dazzo v Kilcullen](https://www.nycourts.gov/REPORTER/3dseries/2008/2008_08447.htm)
- National days-to-close: no primary 2025–2026 ICE figure was retrievable; lender and blog pages cite about 42–43 days for purchase loans in 2026, attributed to ICE but not checked against ICE [CLAIMED, accessed 2026-10-23] — [mortgage-info.com, July 2026](https://mortgage-info.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-close-on-a-house-2026)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Combining the verified pieces: accepted offer → inspection within about a week → executed contract and deposit at about one to two weeks → 45-day commitment window → closing "on or about" 60 days after acceptance, give or take 30 days. This matches the Rand 45–60-day practitioner figure in KQ3.
- [INFERENCE] For the product, the contingency dates (attorney approval where used, mortgage commitment, cancellation-notice windows) are the deadline engine that a transaction tracker must compute from the executed-contract date, not from acceptance.

### Gap theme G5: commission collection and post-close

### Takeaway
The Commission Escrow Act lets a licensed broker whose agreement carries the statutory bold notice file an affidavit of entitlement before the deed passes, forcing the seller to deposit the lesser of net proceeds or the unpaid commission with the county clerk; the broker then has six months to sue, but the seller can get the money released if nobody sues within 60 days. This corrects the notes' "must sue within 60 days".

### Cited Findings

### Gap theme G7: physical-presence professionals and valuation

### Takeaway
Paid home inspections require a New York home-inspector licence (architects, engineers and code officials excepted), and federal appraisal-independence rules let agents supply information but not influence value; an estimate produced purely by software is outside Reg Z's definition of a "valuation".

### Cited Findings
- RPL §444-d: "No person shall conduct or represent that he or she has the ability to conduct a home inspection for compensation" without a licence under the article; exceptions for architects, engineers, code-enforcement officials and licensing candidates [PRIMARY, accessed 2026-10-23] — [nysenate §444-d](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/444-D)
- 12 CFR 1026.42 comment 42(b)(1)-1: "'Covered persons' include creditors, mortgage brokers, appraisers, appraisal management companies, real estate agents"; 42(c)(3) permits asking the appraiser to consider "additional, appropriate property information", to explain, and to correct errors; comment 42(b)(3)-1: a valuation includes "an estimate of market value prepared by a real estate agent"; comment 42(b)(3)-2: an estimate produced exclusively by an automated model is not a valuation, but one a person develops partly from automated output is [PRIMARY, accessed 2026-10-23] — [12 CFR 1026.42 (CFPB)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/42/)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Pricing can be split cleanly: the automated estimate (outside "valuation") is software's; the licensee's pricing advice is a covered person's valuation and must never be used to pressure an appraiser. The product should keep the two labelled separately.
