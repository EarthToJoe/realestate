## Key Question 1: What is the current DOS-approved syllabus for the salesperson (77-hour) and broker (75-hour; 152 total) qualifying courses, topic by topic with hours, and did 2022–2026 updates change it?

### Takeaway
The current syllabi took effect **December 21, 2022** (19 NYCRR 176.3 and 176.4, not Part 177 — Part 177 governs continuing education): the salesperson course rose from 75 to 77 hours (fair housing 4→6 hours) and the broker course from 45 to 75 hours, with new mandatory broker subjects on supervision, onboarding, teams, standardized operating procedures, cultural competency, DEI, implicit bias, assistance animals and source of income. I found no further syllabus changes through October 2026; DOS curriculum pages are still labelled "eff 12/21/2022".

### Cited Findings

**Statutory hour requirements**
- Broker applicants "must also furnish proof that they have attended for at least one hundred fifty-two hours and has successfully completed a real estate course or courses approved by the secretary of state"; plus either "not less than two years" as a licensed salesperson under a broker or "the equivalent experience in general real estate business for a period of at least three years" **[PRIMARY, RPL §441(1)(b), booklet Jan 2024]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law); [nysenate §441](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/441).
- A licensed salesperson who completed "seventy-seven hours of an approved real estate course or courses, six hours of which have been focused on fair housing and discrimination … within eight years of the date of the application" may have it credited "against the one hundred fifty-two hours" **[PRIMARY, RPL §441(1)(c)]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- The 152 hours "shall include instruction on fair housing and discrimination … and instruction on the licensed real estate broker's responsibility for ensuring that each licensed real estate salesman under such broker's supervision is in compliance with his or her obligations under applicable federal, state, and local laws, rules, and regulations pertaining to fair housing" **[PRIMARY, RPL §441(1)(d)]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- No salesperson's license issues unless the applicant "has attended at least seventy-seven hours and successfully completed a real estate course or courses approved by the secretary of state" **[PRIMARY, RPL §441(1-A)(d)]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- "Computer-based and distance-learning courses may be approved by the department so long as providers demonstrate the ability to monitor and verify participation by the applicant … the department may prescribe that specified subjects or hours must be presented in a classroom setting" **[PRIMARY, RPL §441(1)(b) and (1-A)(d)]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- Statutory exam content, broker: "a fair understanding of the general purposes and general legal effect of deeds, mortgages, land contracts of sale, and leases, a general and fair understanding of the obligations between principal and agent, has taken a class on cultural competency training, a general and fair understanding of the laws, rules and regulations pertaining to fair housing" **[PRIMARY, RPL §441(1)(b)]**. Salesperson: "working knowledge of the basic concepts of law pertaining to contracts, real property, agency and this article … fair housing … mastery of basic skills … working knowledge of the ethical obligations of a real estate salesperson, and knowledge of the provisions of the general obligations law" **[PRIMARY, RPL §441(1-A)(b)]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- DOS exam logistics: salesperson exam is multiple choice "based on the 77-hour pre-licensing curriculum", 1.5 hours, pass/fail, passing result valid two years; broker exam "based on the 152-hour pre-licensing curriculum", 2.5 hours, pass/fail, valid two years. Fees: salesperson $65 application/renewal, $15 exam; broker $185 application/renewal/branch, $15 exam. Broker applicants must be 20; salespersons 18; broker needs two years as salesperson or three years general real estate experience with minimum points (19 NYCRR Part 179: 3,500 points = two years full-time) **[PRIMARY, DOS pages fetched 2026-10-23; Part 179 in booklet]** — [DOS salesperson](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-agent); [DOS broker](https://dos.ny.gov/become-real-estate-broker).
- A vendor summary of the DOS applications page says the qualifying course "is only valid for 8 years past the course completion date" **[SECONDARY]** — [RealEstateU](https://realestateu.com/real-estate/guides/new-york-real-estate-pre-licensing-course); consistent with the eight-year window in RPL §441(1)(c) [PRIMARY above].

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The syllabi are the State's own description of what a licensee is trained to do; they are also a map of the functions the State expects a *human licensee* to perform (negotiate a "meeting of the minds", explain the §443 form, present fair housing notices, orchestrate the closing, supervise). Any automation plan should be read against the syllabus line items, not only against the statute.
- [INFERENCE] Several syllabus lines explicitly acknowledge the attorney-centred NY practice ("Upstate Contracts / Downstate Contracts"; "Who prepares the contract? Seller's attorney…"; "advantages of lawyers holding down payments"; "Lawyer's unconditional approval"), which supports the view that in the Hudson Valley the contract and down payment ordinarily run through attorneys, not brokers.
- [INFERENCE] The 2022 broker-course additions (supervision, onboarding, teams, SOPs, unlicensed assistants) show the regulator's current emphasis: brokers are being trained as supervisors of people and processes. A software-run brokerage will be judged by a DOS that has just re-centred broker education on supervision.

## Key Question 2: What continuing education is required per renewal cycle and on which topics?

### Takeaway
22.5 hours every two-year term, with statutory minimums: 2 hours cultural competency, 3 hours fair housing/discrimination, 2 hours implicit bias, 2.5 hours ethical business practices, 1 hour recent legal matters, 1 hour law of agency (2 hours in a salesperson's initial term). Attorneys admitted to the NY bar are exempt; the old 15-year broker exemption ended July 1, 2021.

### Cited Findings
- RPL §441(3)(a) (text in the Jan 2024 booklet): no renewal "unless such licensee shall have within the two year period immediately preceding such renewal attended at least twenty-two and one-half hours which shall include at least two hours of cultural competency training, at least three hours of instruction pertaining to fair housing and/or discrimination in the sale or rental of real property or an interest in real property, at least two hours of instruction pertaining to implicit bias awareness and understanding, at least two and one-half hours of instruction pertaining to ethical business practices, at least one hour of instruction pertaining to recent legal matters governing the practice of real estate brokers and salespersons in New York which may include statutes, laws, regulations, rules, codes, department of state opinions and decisions, and court decisions and at least one hour of instruction pertaining to the law of agency except in the case of the initial two-year licensing term for real estate salespersons, two hours of agency related instruction must be completed" **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law); [nysenate §441](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/441).
- Same subdivision: licensees admitted under §442-g(6) (military households) must complete 11 of the 22.5 hours in the first year of the initial term, "three hours [of which] shall pertain to applicable New York state statutes and regulations"; licensees "shall provide an affidavit" of CE; "'implicit bias' shall mean the attitudes or stereotypes that affect an individual's understanding, actions and decisions in an unconscious manner" **[PRIMARY, RPL §441(3)(a)]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- CE course standards: "curriculum composed primarily of real estate practice and professional responsibility and ethics … taught by a qualified faculty with attorneys presenting legal subjects. Credit shall be awarded on the basis of one hour for each sixty minutes of actual attendance"; distance learning allowed if participation is monitored **[PRIMARY, RPL §441(3)(c)(i)]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- Fair-housing CE content is set by rule after public hearing and "shall include, but not be limited to, courses on: (1) the legacy of segregation, unequal treatment, and historic lack of access to opportunity in housing; (2) unequal access to amenities and resources on the basis of race, disability, and other protected characteristics; (3) federal, state, and local fair housing laws; and (4) anti-bias training" **[PRIMARY, RPL §441(3)(e)]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- No license continues past expiry without proof of CE, though DOS may issue a temporary renewal where the failure "is not due to the fault of the licensee" **[PRIMARY, RPL §441(3)(b)]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- 19 NYCRR 177.1(a): "No renewal license shall be issued to any real estate broker or salesperson for any license period unless such licensee shall provide evidence of completion of 22 1/2 hours of approved continuing education within the two-year period immediately preceding such renewal"; the 2022 rule deleted the former sentence exempting brokers "licensed prior to July 1, 2008 for at least 15 consecutive years" **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS adopted rule text](https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2023/01/adopted_educational-update-for-real-estate-professionals_rule-text-2-1.pdf).
- DOS FAQ: "Effective July 1, 2021, all real estate licensees, with the exception of Attorneys admitted to the New York State Bar, must complete 22.5 hours of continuing education"; "A recent legislative amendment REMOVED the 15-year real estate broker continuing education exemption"; "The law clearly states that continuing education must be completed within each license term" (no carry-over); "Extensions will only be granted in bona fide hardship cases"; "Schools are required by law to maintain course completion records for three years"; "You may request duplicate certificates from the school(s) in the event you are audited"; answering "no" to the renewal CE question means "your renewal application will not be approved" **[PRIMARY, DOS FAQ pages fetched 2026-10-23]** — [DOS broker FAQ](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-frequently-asked-questions); [DOS salesperson FAQ](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-salesperson-frequently-asked-questions).

The initial two-year term is excepted." ## Key Question 3: What fiduciary duties does NY recognise for agents and where are they stated?

### Takeaway
The statutory §443 disclosure form names six fiduciary duties — "reasonable care, undivided loyalty, confidentiality, full disclosure, obedience and duty to account" — plus three duties to the non-client (reasonable skill and care; honesty, fairness and good faith; disclosure of known material facts). The Court of Appeals (Dubbs, 2001) treats a broker as a fiduciary with a duty of loyalty whose disclosure must "lay bare the truth"; DOS (LI12/Moore) forbids dual agency without informed written consent. §443 expressly preserves the common law and expressly exempts a licensee who works with a party "in a capacity other than as an agent".

### Cited Findings
- RPL §443(1)(a): "'Agent' means a person who is licensed as a real estate broker, associate real estate broker or real estate salesperson under section four hundred forty-A of this article and is acting in a fiduciary capacity." §443(2): applies "only to transactions involving residential real property" (1–4 family, condo, co-op; not unimproved land) **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law); [nysenate §443](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/443).
- Statutory form text (buyer–seller version): "A seller's agent has, without limitation, the following fiduciary duties to the seller: reasonable care, undivided loyalty, confidentiality, full disclosure, obedience and duty to account. A seller's agent does not represent the interests of the buyer. … In dealings with the buyer, a seller's agent should (a) exercise reasonable skill and care in performance of the agent's duties; (b) deal honestly, fairly and in good faith; and (c) disclose all facts known to the agent materially affecting the value or desirability of property, except as otherwise provided by law." Mirror text for a buyer's agent, whose duty to the seller is to "disclose all facts known to the agent materially affecting the buyer's ability and/or willingness to perform a contract to acquire seller's property that are not inconsistent with the agent's fiduciary duties to the buyer" **[PRIMARY, RPL §443(4)(a)]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- Form text on dual agency: "A real estate broker may represent both the buyer and the seller if both the buyer and seller give their informed consent in writing. In such a dual agency situation, the agent will not be able to provide the full range of fiduciary duties … An agent acting as a dual agent must explain carefully to both the buyer and seller that the agent is acting for the other party as well … by consenting to the dual agency relationship the buyer and seller are giving up their right to undivided loyalty." Designated sales agents: "A designated sales agent cannot provide the full range of fiduciary duties … they cannot provide undivided loyalty." The form opens "THIS IS NOT A CONTRACT" and says "A real estate agent is a person qualified to advise about real estate. If you need legal, tax or other advice, consult with a professional in that field." **[PRIMARY, RPL §443(4)(a)]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- Timing/records (§443(3)): listing agent gives the form "prior to entering into a listing agreement" and obtains a signed acknowledgment; seller's agent gives it to a buyer/buyer's agent "at the time of the first substantive contact"; buyer's agent gives it to the buyer "prior to entering into an agreement to act as the buyer's agent" and to the seller/listing agent at first substantive contact; the agent "shall maintain a copy of the signed acknowledgment for not less than three years"; on refusal "the agent shall set forth under oath or affirmation a written declaration of the facts of the refusal" and keep it three years; advance consent to dual agency may be given on the form **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- §443(5): "This section shall not apply to a real estate licensee who works with a buyer or a seller in accordance with terms agreed to by the licensee and buyer or seller and in a capacity other than as an agent." §443(6): "Nothing in this section shall be construed to limit or alter the application of the common law of agency with respect to residential real estate transactions." **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- A DOS administrative decision held "the Legislature in enacting Real Property Law §443(3) did not require an element of intent" — failure to give the form is itself a violation **[SECONDARY — search-result summary of a DOS decision; decision not read]** — [DOS OAH appeal decision (Lemecke)](https://docs.dos.ny.gov/ooah/decisions/appeals/17%20DOS%20APP%2004%20Lemecke.htm).
- Dubbs v. Stribling & Associates, 96 NY2d 337 (2001): "a real estate broker is a fiduciary with a duty of loyalty and an obligation to act in the best interests of the principal"; where loyalties are divided (personal stake or multiple parties) the broker must disclose, and the disclosure must "lay bare the truth, without ambiguity or reservation, in all its stark significance" (quoting Wendt v Fischer, 243 NY 439); a broker who wishes to buy the principal's property must disclose that intent and information reasonably affecting the principal's decision; the relationship and its duties can end by agreement or the principal's unilateral act. Claim dismissed on the facts **[PRIMARY, opinion read at LII]** — [Dubbs (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/nyctap/I01_0072.htm).
- Sonnenschein v. - DOS Legal Memorandum LI12 "Be Wary of Dual Agency" (updated Feb 2020), relying on DOS v. - The salesperson syllabus lists the duties as "Loyalty, confidentiality, full disclosure, obedience, reasonable skill and care and duty to account"; the broker syllabus as "Obedience, loyalty, disclosure, confidentiality, accounting, and reasonable care" and adds "Vicarious liability as it relates to buyers, sub, and broker's agencies" **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS 77-hour curriculum](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-salesperson-77-hour-curriculum-eff-12212022); [DOS 75-hour curriculum](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-75-hour-curriculum-eff-12212022).
- Related statutory duties of an agent to disclose/inform:
  - RPL §466 (Property Condition Disclosure Act): "An agent representing a seller … as a listing broker shall have the duty to timely inform each seller … of the seller's obligations under this article. An agent representing a buyer …, or, if the buyer is not represented by an agent, the agent representing a seller … and dealing with a prospective buyer, shall have the duty to timely (in any event, before the buyer signs a binding contract of sale) inform such buyer of the buyer's rights and obligations under this article. If an agent performs the duties … the agent shall have no further duties under this article and shall not be liable" **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law). The booklet's PCDS form includes the FEMA flood questions (items 10–13) and §465 contains no $500-credit language, consistent with the 2023 amendments effective March 2024 **[PRIMARY, booklet Jan 2024]**.
  - RPL §443-a: HIV/AIDS status and homicide/suicide/felony history are "not a material defect or fact"; no cause of action or discipline for non-disclosure; buyer may submit a written inquiry; seller may choose whether to respond **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
  - 19 NYCRR 175.7: "A real estate broker shall make it clear for which party he is acting and he shall not receive compensation from more than one party except with the full knowledge and consent of the broker's client." **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
  - 19 NYCRR 175.4–175.6 (self-dealing): broker "shall not directly or indirectly buy for himself property listed with him … without first making his true position clearly known to the listing owner"; must disclose an ownership interest before buying for a client or selling to a purchaser **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The duties attach to a licensed *person* "acting in a fiduciary capacity". Software can prompt, document and log, but DOS and the courts will hold the licensee (and the broker) to the duties; there is no mechanism in NY law for a non-licensed system to be the fiduciary.
- [INFERENCE] A single-broker platform that "replaces both the seller's agent and the buyer's agent" is, by definition in §443(1)(i) and LI12, a **dual agent** for every in-house match. The statute itself warns that a dual agent "will not be able to provide the full range of fiduciary duties", requires informed written consent from both sides, and DOS titles its memo "Be Wary of Dual Agency". Designated sales agents (one for each side) would need at least two licensed salespersons or associate brokers under the broker — a lone broker cannot designate. This does not kill the model but it fixes its legal shape: either (a) dual agency with explicit consent and reduced duties, or (b) a non-agent "capacity other than as an agent" under §443(5), which the statute recognises but does not define and which the broker must document by agreement.
- [INFERENCE] §443(5) is the only statutory hook for a reduced-duty "transaction facilitator" model, but Dubbs's default rule (broker = fiduciary) and §443(6) (common law preserved) mean the non-agent capacity must be clearly agreed in writing and consistently practised; it does not remove the licence requirement (KQ4/KQ5).

- I did not read the Lemecke DOS appeal decision itself (only a search summary).
- Whether NY courts have treated a written "non-agent/facilitator" engagement under §443(5) as effective to negate fiduciary duties is untested in anything I found.

## Key Question 4: What do 19 NYCRR Part 175 and RPL Article 12-A require or forbid a licensee to do?

### Takeaway
Part 175 is a short, specific code of conduct: separate escrow account with three-business-day deposit; render accounts; disclose self-interest; make clear whom you represent; no net listings; no automatic listing renewals; a mandated six-point explanation on exclusive listings; broker-only advertising with detailed content and team rules; three-year transaction records; "regular, frequent and consistent personal guidance" of salespersons; a written fair-housing notice at first substantive contact and posted at offices, on website homepages and at open houses. Article 12-A adds the licence requirement, a definite NY place of business, compensation only through licensed brokers, SOPs for homebuyers, no after-the-fact referral fees, and the §443 form. The section the question calls "training (175.29)" is actually "Posting of Fair Housing Laws"; training requirements live in RPL §441.

"Advertisements shall include an honest and accurate description of the property"; geographic-area names must be accurate; "Advertisements shall not guarantee future profits"; websites of associates/teams "are permitted, provided that said associate real estate brokers, real estate salespersons and teams are duly authorized by their supervising real estate broker to create and maintain such websites and such websites remain subject to the supervision of the real estate broker … while the website is live", every page carrying the required information and a homepage link to the broker's site; initial e-mails must carry the required information; for-sale signs must identify the broker; team names must include licensees' names or "at/of [broker]", must use the word "team" (not "associate", "realty" or "group"), may not use an unlicensed person's name, and ads naming unlicensed team members "must clearly and conspicuously state which individuals are real estate licensees and which ones are not".
- 175.26 Business signs in apartment buildings (where lobby signs are barred).
- 175.27 Disclaimer: Part 175 does not indicate whether a salesperson is an independent contractor or employee.
- 175.28 Notification of Fair Housing Laws: "A real estate broker shall be responsible to ensure that each individual licensed … and associated with such broker provides to a prospective purchaser, tenant, seller, or landlord upon first substantive contact a disclosure notice furnished by the Department, containing substantive provisions of the New York State Human Rights Law"; delivery "by any of the following means: email, text, electronic messaging system, facsimile, or hardcopy" (a link is acceptable if the message says it contains Human Rights Law information); "Oral disclosure does not satisfy"; applies to "all real property … regardless of the number of units … condominiums; cooperative apartments; vacant lands …; or commercial properties"; hardcopy requires a signed acknowledgment; electronic delivery requires a retained duplicate; records kept "not less than three years"; refusal requires a sworn written declaration kept three years.
- 175.29 Posting of Fair Housing Laws: poster "at every office and branch office", window-visible where listings are posted, otherwise where the licence is posted; "(c) All websites created and maintained by real estate brokers, associate real estate brokers, real estate salespersons and any real estate team … shall prominently and conspicuously display on the homepage of such website a link to the Department's notice"; "(d) … shall have displayed at all open houses of all real property the notice required by paragraph (a) … [and] shall have available at all open houses and showings of all real property the notice required by paragraph (a) of section 175.28".
- Training: there is no "training" section in Part 175. ### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The rules are written for a human-run office: a sign on a building, a licence on a wall, a pocket card "shown on demand", a broker who "accepts" listings, "personal" guidance of salespersons. A brokerage whose operations are a website must still satisfy each of these literally; none has an online substitute in the text, though 175.28(b) (electronic fair-housing notices), 175.25(d)(3) (websites) and §442-h(4)(b) (posting SOPs on websites and apps) show DOS is comfortable regulating online channels.
- [INFERENCE] §442(1) is the pay-the-software problem: a success fee or commission share paid by the broker to an unlicensed software company "for any service, help or aid rendered … in buying, selling …" is on its face prohibited; a flat subscription or licence fee not measured by commissions is the structure the statute tolerates (as with MLS, CRM and advertising vendors). DOS's unlicensed-assistant page makes the same distinction for people ("If compensated on a completed transaction basis, the assistant must be licensed"). The founders' corporate structure (an unlicensed software owner and a licensed attorney-broker) needs to be built around this.
- [INFERENCE] 175.22 bars only *salespersons* from owning voting stock in their brokerage corporation; it does not address unlicensed owners. Whether an unlicensed person may hold equity in a licensed brokerage entity (with a licensed representative broker) is a structural question I could not settle from the sources (see Gaps).
- [INFERENCE] 175.19 (no net listings) and §442 (rebates to parties allowed) leave room for flat-fee or low-percentage pricing and for rebating part of a commission to a buyer, provided the rebate is not payment for licensed activity by the buyer.

## Key Question 5: What does DOS say unlicensed persons (assistants, software, third parties) may and may not do, and has DOS addressed software or online platforms?

### Takeaway
DOS's rule is simple: an unlicensed person may do "any office activities that are not specified in Section 440", and DOS lists 15 examples (answering phones, scheduling, following up loan commitments, assembling closing documents, writing/placing ads for broker approval, "Type contract forms for approval by broker", computing commissions, signs, ordering repairs, flyers, scheduling showings, gathering CMA/appraisal data, personnel files, clerical work), with the broker responsible for "training, supervision and control". Showing property, negotiating, listing and advising are not on the list. I found no formal DOS opinion on software or AI; the only DOS position on online platforms is a 2016 informal email that moving the connection online "does not mean that a license is not required".

### Cited Findings
- DOS "Unlicensed Real Estate Assistants" page (undated): unlicensed employees or assistants "may engage in any office activities that are not specified in Section 440 of Article 12A". The page's list of activities an unlicensed assistant may generally perform, verbatim: (1) "Answer the phone, forward calls, and take messages."; (2) "Arrange appointments, by telephone, for the licensee."; (3) "Follow up on loan commitments after a contract has been negotiated and generally secure status reports on loan progress."; (4) "Assemble documents for closing."; (5) "Write ads for approval of broker and place approved classified advertising."; (6) "Type contract forms for approval by broker."; (7) "Compute commission checks."; (8) "Place or remove signs from property."; (9) "Order items of repair as directed by the broker."; (10) "Prepare flyers and promotional information for approval by broker."; (11) "Schedule appointments for licensees to show listed property."; (12) "Gather information for a comparative market analysis."; (13) "Gather information for an appraisal."; (14) "Monitor licenses and personnel files."; (15) "Perform secretarial and clerical duties such as typing of letters and filing." **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS Unlicensed Real Estate Assistants](https://dos.ny.gov/node/64166).
- Same page: the broker is responsible for "the supervision and control of activities conducted in the name of the licensed real estate business" and "is required to extend and provide necessary training, supervision and control over licensed and non-licensed services being provided to consumers"; pay should be hourly, per-activity or salaried; "If compensated on a completed transaction basis, the assistant must be licensed as a real estate salesperson and must receive compensation directly from the licensed broker"; "experience in these activities may not be counted toward any qualifying experience requirements" **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS Unlicensed Real Estate Assistants](https://dos.ny.gov/node/64166).
- The page contains no list of prohibited activities; prohibition is by reference to §440 **[PRIMARY observation]** — [DOS Unlicensed Real Estate Assistants](https://dos.ny.gov/node/64166).
- DOS salesperson FAQ: without a licence you "cannot conduct any real estate activities that require a New York State real estate license"; "There are no grace periods"; collecting rent or placing tenants for a landlord requires a broker's licence; negotiating a residential mortgage requires DFS registration, not a real estate licence **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS salesperson FAQ](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-salesperson-frequently-asked-questions).
- The salesperson syllabus includes "Unlicensed assistants guidelines" (Subject 1) and the broker syllabus "How to assure that unlicensed assistants do not perform tasks requiring a license" **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS 77-hour curriculum](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-salesperson-77-hour-curriculum-eff-12212022); [DOS 75-hour curriculum](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-75-hour-curriculum-eff-12212022).
- 175.25(b)(1): "Only a real estate broker is permitted to place or cause to be published advertisements related to the sale or lease of property" **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- Showings: a Buffalo-area forum post reports that the Buffalo Niagara Association of REALTORS reads the RPL as barring showings and open houses by unlicensed staff **[CLAIMED, non-official]** — [Hauseit forum](https://forum.hauseit.com/forum/hauseit-customer-service-support/426-can-unlicensed-real-estate-assistants-or-neighbors-show-property-on-behalf-of-owners). The DOS list permits unlicensed staff only to "Schedule appointments for licensees to show listed property" **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS Unlicensed Real Estate Assistants](https://dos.ny.gov/node/64166).
- Online platforms: a March 28, 2016 column by an Olshan Frome Wolosky partner reports that "in informal advice via email, the Department of State has taken the position that … the fact that the connection between the parties is made online does not mean that a license is not required", that the statute and rules "don't address this issue", and that some sites take the opposite view; the author favours modernising the rules **[SECONDARY, reporting an informal DOS position]** — [Olshan, Real Estate Websites and Broker Regulation](https://www.olshanlaw.com/Real-Estate-Law-Blog/Real-Estate-Websites-Broker-Regulation).
- Related: Matter of Malul (3d Dept, Nov. 6, 2025) upheld revocation where a broker's "wholesale failure to supervise" let unlicensed activity proceed under his licence; the Deputy Secretary found this "enabled Malakov and Amrani to avail themselves of his status as a licensed broker to engage in unlicensed real estate activity" and so was fraud, citing DOS v. Flagship Marketing Group, 7 DOS 00 **[PRIMARY, decision read]** — [Malul, CV-24-1010 (3d Dept 2025)](https://decisions.courts.state.ny.us/ad3/Decisions/2025/CV-24-1010.pdf).
- Legal Memorandum LI04 (Feb 14, 2020) on the unauthorized practice of law: Judiciary Law §478 bars non-attorneys from practising law; §484 bars anyone "not regularly admitted" from receiving compensation "for preparing deeds, mortgages, leases, and other instruments affecting real estate"; violations are misdemeanors (§485). Under Duncan & Hill Realty v. Department of State (62 AD2d 690, 4th Dept), a non-attorney broker who drafted detailed purchase-money mortgage terms was untrustworthy/incompetent; brokers may prepare "simple" fill-in-the-blank contracts only "subject to approval by the parties' attorneys"; "it is not proper for such a broker to undertake to devise the detailed terms of a purchase-money mortgage"; brokers may complete non-legal blanks (names, closing date/place, description, price) on forms approved by a joint bar/realtor committee; brokers may not "develop any 'legal terms'", add provisions unless the whole contract is conditioned on attorney approval, give legal advice, discourage consulting attorneys, "charge a separate fee for preparing a contract, or share in attorneys' fees"; contracts should state prominently that they are legally binding and recommend consulting a lawyer; authorities also include Jemzura v. McCue and Attorney General Opinion 96-F11 **[PRIMARY, DOS memo]** — [DOS LI04](https://dos.ny.gov/node/23931).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Mapping the DOS list to software: scheduling, loan-commitment follow-up, closing-document assembly, ad drafting for broker approval, contract-form typing for broker approval, commission computation, flyer preparation and CMA data gathering are all things DOS already lets an unlicensed human do under broker supervision; a software system doing them under the same broker supervision is the strongest ground the model stands on. The DOS framing ("for approval by broker") implies a licensed approver remains in the loop for ads and contract forms.
- [INFERENCE] Everything *not* on the list and inside §440 — listing "for another and for a fee", offering or negotiating a sale, advising on price or terms as the party's agent, presenting the §443 form "as the agent", explaining dual agency, conducting showings and open houses (contested; not on the permitted list) — is licensed activity. Software can transmit an offer the way e-mail does, but the moment the system itself negotiates on a party's behalf for a fee, the firm is brokering through an unlicensed instrument; DOS's 2016 informal position suggests it would look through the technology to the function.
- [INFERENCE] "Draft the contract": in NY the licensed real estate activity and the practice of law are separate gates. A broker (or software) may populate a bar-approved form subject to attorney approval but may not devise terms or charge for preparing it (LI04; Judiciary Law §484). An attorney may draft, but a lawyer who drafts the contract for *both* buyer and seller while the same person's brokerage represents both is in a conflict-of-interest situation under the Rules of Professional Conduct, and NY ethics opinions have long barred acting as broker and attorney in the same transaction **[CLAIMED from background knowledge; not verified this session — the attorney-broker should check NYSBA ethics opinions on dual roles]**. The attorney's presence solves the unauthorized-practice problem only if the attorney is genuinely acting as counsel to a party, not as a drafting service for the brokerage.
- [INFERENCE] A purely self-help tool sold to a seller (the seller lists, advertises, shows and negotiates personally, using software as the seller's own instrument) is outside §440 because nothing is done "for another … for a fee" by a third person — this is the FSBO/forms-software model. The venture as described (software that *replaces* the agents and "charges far less" per side) is on the other side of that line: it performs the functions for the parties for a fee.

### Gaps
- No DOS opinion letter, memorandum or decision addressing software, automation or AI performing licensed functions was found; the 2016 informal e-mail position is the only trace. A written inquiry to DOS Division of Licensing Services would be the way to get a current position.
- The DOS unlicensed-assistants page carries no date; I could not establish when it was last revised.
- NYSAR legal-hotline guidance on unlicensed assistants and teams was not retrieved (no URL found before the search budget ended).

## Key Question 6: What are the grounds for discipline (RPL §441-c) most relevant to a software-run brokerage?

### Takeaway
§441-c lets DOS revoke, suspend, fine up to $2,000 per violation or reprimand for any Article 12-A violation, a §442-h(4) (SOP) violation, fraud or "fraudulent practices" (read broadly to include "dishonest and misleading" conduct), "dishonest or misleading advertising", "untrustworthiness or incompetency", or a Human Rights Law violation. For a software-run brokerage the live exposures are: failure to supervise (treated as untrustworthiness, incompetency and even fraud in Malul 2025), unauthorized practice of law (LI04), advertising violations, discrimination findings (presumptive untrustworthiness under 175.17(b)), and SOP non-compliance.

### Cited Findings
- RPL §441-c(1)(a): DOS "may revoke the license of a real estate broker or salesperson or suspend the same, for such period as the department may deem proper, or in lieu thereof may impose a fine not exceeding two thousand dollars payable to the department of state, provided that fifty percent of all moneys received … shall be payable to the anti-discrimination in housing fund …, or a reprimand upon conviction of the licensee of a violation of any provision of this article, or for a violation of subdivision four of section four hundred forty-two-h of this article, or for a material misstatement in the application for such license, or if such licensee has been guilty of fraud or fraudulent practices, or for dishonest or misleading advertising, or has demonstrated untrustworthiness or incompetency to act as a real estate broker or salesperson, or for a violation of article fifteen of the executive law committed in their capacity as a real estate broker or salesperson" **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law); [nysenate §441-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/441-C). (A secondary source's "$1,000 per violation" figure is out of date **[SECONDARY, contradicted by PRIMARY]**.)
- §441-c(2)–(4): written, signed determination served on licensee and complainant; licence and pocket card returned within five days; no display of a revoked/suspended licence; a revoked licensee is barred from relicensing "for one year from the date of such revocation" **[PRIMARY, per nysenate summary and booklet]** — [nysenate §441-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/441-C). A5169 would make the bar three years (pending) — [A5169](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A5169).
- §442-c: "No violation of a provision of this article by a real estate salesperson or employee of a real estate broker shall be deemed to be cause for the revocation or suspension of the license of the broker, unless it shall appear that the broker had actual knowledge of such violation or retains the benefits, profits or proceeds of a transaction wrongfully negotiated by their salesperson or employee after notice of the salesperson's or employee's misconduct. A broker shall be guilty of a misdemeanor for having any salesperson associated with their firm who has not secured the required license" **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law); [nysenate §442-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442-C).
- Matter of Roberts Real Estate v. NYS Dept. of State, 80 NY2d 116 (1992), as applied in Malul: §442-c limits revocation/suspension of a broker for a salesperson's violation absent actual knowledge or retained proceeds, but a broker may be disciplined for his own "untrustworthiness and incompetency [as a broker] and fail[ure] to meet [his] duty to supervise sales personnel" (quoting the Third Department's Roberts decision, 171 AD2d 217, 221, mod 80 NY2d 116) **[PRIMARY as quoted in Malul; Roberts itself not read]** — [Malul (3d Dept 2025)](https://decisions.courts.state.ny.us/ad3/Decisions/2025/CV-24-1010.pdf). A search summary adds that the Court of Appeals in Roberts remanded for a lesser sanction because "the absence of actual knowledge does not insulate real estate brokers from disciplinary measures less severe than license suspension or revocation" **[SECONDARY]** — search-result summary citing [2009 App. Div. decision](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/3dseries/2009/2009_01806.htm) (page returned 403).
- Malul (Nov. 6, 2025): a broker who "had no knowledge of any personnel hiring or working in the office", "had never even met" the salesperson, and left day-to-day operations to another salesperson was found untrustworthy/incompetent for "his wholesale failure to supervise", and also to have engaged in "fraudulent practices" because in the regulatory context fraud "goes beyond the traditional understanding of intentional fraud to 'include those acts which may be characterized as dishonest and misleading'" (Allstate Ins. Co. v Foschio, 93 AD2d 328); revocation of both his LLC broker's licence and his associate broker's licence was confirmed as not "so disproportionate to his misconduct as to shock the judicial conscience" **[PRIMARY]** — [Malul](https://decisions.courts.state.ny.us/ad3/Decisions/2025/CV-24-1010.pdf); [nycourts.gov cite 2025 NY Slip Op 06110](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/3dseries/2025/2025_06110.htm).
- DOS Legal Memorandum LI01 (updated Feb 2020): the Secretary has "wide discretion in determining what should be deemed untrustworthy conduct" (Matter of Gold v. Lomenzo, 29 NY2d 468); conduct outside real estate (insurance-broker misconduct, fraudulent loan applications, a criminal conviction) can support untrustworthiness; a pure contract dispute over commissions with no public harm is not enough (Stowell v. Cuomo, 52 NY2d 208), but practices "so devious as to indicate clearly that he would not deal fairly with the public" are; "the manifest purpose of Real Property Law §441-c is the protection of the general public" **[PRIMARY, DOS memo]** — [DOS LI01](https://dos.ny.gov/node/23871).
- LI04: DOS treats a licensee's unlawful practice of law "as grounds for discipline, including suspension or revocation" and acts independently of any prosecution **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS LI04](https://dos.ny.gov/node/23931).
- 175.17(b): an agency or court finding of unlawful discrimination "shall be presumptive evidence of untrustworthiness and will subject such licensee to discipline, including a proceeding for revocation" **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- §442-h(4)(b): "Any broker or salesperson operating under a brokerage license that fails to adhere to such [standardized] operating procedures shall be subject to the penalties imposed by section four hundred forty-one-c" **[PRIMARY]** — [nysenate §442-h](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442-H).
- Criminal and civil exposure for unlicensed activity: misdemeanor (§442-e(1)); penalty of one to four times the compensation received, recoverable by the aggrieved person (§442-e(3)); no suit for commission without a licence (§442-d) **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- DOS v. Metalios (1105 DOS 09, Nov. 9, 2009): charges of soliciting a cease-and-desist address and failing to supervise two salespersons were dismissed where the broker kept the cease-and-desist list at the front desk, reminded salespersons, held weekly meetings and barred full-time salespersons from working from home; the tribunal said the duty does not require the broker to "look over a salesperson's shoulder constantly" **[PRIMARY, DOS decision]** — [Metalios, 1105 DOS 09](https://docs.dos.ny.gov/ooah/decisions/Real_estate/2009/Metalios_Menalaos.htm).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The most probable DOS theory against a software-run brokerage is not "software is illegal" but "the broker demonstrated untrustworthiness or incompetency by not supervising what was done under his licence", with Malul's extension to "fraudulent practices" if unlicensed actors (or an unlicensed system acting for a fee) were enabled by the broker's licence. The broker's personal, documented involvement in each licensed decision is the defence, and Metalios shows DOS accepts written policies, training, regular meetings and reminders as evidence of supervision.
- [INFERENCE] Because fraud is read to include merely "dishonest and misleading" acts, automated consumer-facing statements (valuations, property descriptions, "no agent needed" claims) carry disciplinary as well as GBL §349 risk; 175.25(c)(9)–(10) and "dishonest or misleading advertising" in §441-c apply to every automated listing description.
- [INFERENCE] Fair-housing risk is amplified by automation: any algorithmic steering or disparate treatment that produces an agency/court finding is "presumptive evidence of untrustworthiness" against the broker personally (175.17(b)).

- The DOS decision Flagship Marketing (7 DOS 00), on unlicensed activity using a licensed broker's status, was cited but not read — [Flagship (DOS)](https://docs.dos.ny.gov/ooah/decisions/non_indexed/FLAGSHIP.htm).

## Key Question 7: What exactly must a broker do when "supervising", and could a single broker of record supervising software output satisfy it?

### Takeaway
The regulation defines supervision as "regular, frequent and consistent personal guidance, instruction, oversight and superintendence by the real estate broker with respect to the general real estate brokerage business"; DOS decisions accept written policies, training, regular meetings and reminders as proof, and reject absentee brokers who do not know what is done under their licence. The rule is written about supervising *salespersons*; a broker with no salespersons has no 175.21 duty as such, but then every licensed act is the broker's own act, DOS requires "training, supervision and control" over unlicensed staff, and Malul shows that enabling unlicensed activity through a licence is fraud. Whether one broker can personally "superintend" thousands of automated interactions is untested in any source I found.

### Cited Findings
- 19 NYCRR 175.21: "(a) The supervision of a real estate salesperson by a licensed real estate broker, required by subdivision l(d) of §441 of the Real Property Law, shall consist of regular, frequent and consistent personal guidance, instruction, oversight and superintendence by the real estate broker with respect to the general real estate brokerage business conducted by the broker, and all matters relating thereto. (b) The broker and salesperson shall keep written records of all real estate listings obtained by the salesperson, and of all sales and other transactions effected by, and with the aid and assistance of, the salesperson … (c) Participation in the general real estate brokerage business as a licensed real estate salesperson shall consist of active service under the supervision of a licensed real estate broker for at least 35 hours per week for 50 weeks in each year required for qualification" **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- RPL §440(3): a salesperson acts "for or in behalf of such real estate broker"; §440(6): an office manager (associate broker with two of the last four years active) "shall be required to exercise the same duty of supervision over salespersons and associate brokers as a licensed real estate broker"; §441-a(3): each branch office "shall be under the direct supervision of the broker …, or a representative broker …, or a duly appointed office manager"; 175.20(b): "Every branch office shall be under the direct supervision of the broker" **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law).
- DOS FAQ: "A real estate broker is responsible for the supervision and conduct of the real estate brokerage business"; "A real estate salesperson works for and is supervised by the representative broker"; "All listings, although perhaps negotiated by a salesperson, are accepted by the representative broker" **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS broker FAQ](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-frequently-asked-questions); [DOS salesperson FAQ](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-salesperson-frequently-asked-questions).
- DOS unlicensed-assistants page: the broker "is required to extend and provide necessary training, supervision and control over licensed and non-licensed services being provided to consumers" and is responsible for "the supervision and control of activities conducted in the name of the licensed real estate business" **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS Unlicensed Real Estate Assistants](https://dos.ny.gov/node/64166).
- Specific supervisory duties placed on the broker by rule: approve placement of all advertisements and authorize/supervise associate and team websites "while the website is live" (175.25(b)(1), (d)(3)); "ensure that each individual licensed … and associated with such broker provides" the fair-housing notice at first substantive contact (175.28(a)); institute, notarize, post and affirm SOPs (§442-h(4)); keep three-year transaction records (175.23); the broker course must teach "the licensed real estate broker's responsibility for ensuring that each licensed real estate salesman under such broker's supervision is in compliance with" fair-housing law (RPL §441(1)(d)) **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS booklet](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law); [nysenate §442-h](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442-H).
- Malul (2025): the ALJ "found that, although petitioner did not personally 'engage[ ] in any fraudulent acts,' his failure to supervise Malakov and Amrani as required constituted untrustworthiness or incompetency"; the court confirmed because "Petitioner … was not disciplined for the misconduct of Malakov but for what the undisputed proof showed was his own lack of involvement in the day-to-day operations of Wyckoff and his wholesale failure to supervise" (citing RPL §441-c(1)(a); 19 NYCRR 175.21(a)); the failure "was itself dishonest and misleading because it enabled them to engage in 'the deliberate, unlicensed practice of regulated, real estate activity … by misrepresenting either directly or indirectly that' they were either brokers themselves or were being adequately supervised by one" **[PRIMARY]** — [Malul](https://decisions.courts.state.ny.us/ad3/Decisions/2025/CV-24-1010.pdf).
- Metalios (1105 DOS 09): supervision was adequate where the broker kept the cease-and-desist list at the front desk, reminded salespersons of compliance, held weekly meetings and required full-time salespersons to work from the office; the duty does not require the broker to "look over a salesperson's shoulder constantly" **[PRIMARY]** — [Metalios](https://docs.dos.ny.gov/ooah/decisions/Real_estate/2009/Metalios_Menalaos.htm).
- Broker syllabus: supervision is taught as policy manuals, onboarding/continued training, office managers, IC-compliant oversight, enforcing advertising rules, overseeing BPOs, timely escrow handling, and "How to assure that unlicensed assistants do not perform tasks requiring a license" **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS 75-hour curriculum](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-75-hour-curriculum-eff-12212022).
- Pending A5164 would add to §442-c a duty of "reasonable and adequate supervision" judged by "The supervising broker's availability to review and approve all documents", "The distance between the main office and branch offices", and "The broker's ability to teach federal and state transaction law, including discrimination prohibitions", and would make failure to supervise a third ground for revoking a broker's licence for a salesperson's violation; not passed (prior versions 2021–22 and 2023–24 also failed) **[PRIMARY, bill page]** — [A5164](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A5164).
- Independent-contractor overlay taught in the salesperson syllabus: "Broker supervises but does not direct and control"; "Balancing the need to supervise" with the 1986 Labor Law/Workers' Compensation IC provisions **[PRIMARY]** — [DOS 77-hour curriculum](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-salesperson-77-hour-curriculum-eff-12212022).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] What "supervision" concretely means from the sources: (1) the broker knows who is doing what under the licence (Malul's broker failed this by never meeting a salesperson); (2) written office policies and procedures, training/onboarding and recurring reminders/meetings (Metalios, broker syllabus); (3) the broker personally accepts listings and approves advertising and websites (DOS FAQ, 175.25); (4) the broker ensures each required disclosure (§443, 175.28, PCDS §466) is delivered and recorded; (5) the broker controls escrow and records (175.1, 175.23); (6) the broker instructs on fair housing and anti-discrimination (§441(1)(d)). It does not require constant real-time observation.
- [INFERENCE] A single broker of record with **no salespersons**, where software performs clerical functions and the broker personally performs every §440 act (listing, offering, negotiating, advising), is the cleanest fit: 175.21 is not triggered because there is no salesperson, and the DOS unlicensed-assistant framework (tasks "for approval by broker") is the template. The constraint is capacity, not legality: each listing accepted, each ad approved, each §443 form presented "by" the licensee, each negotiation, each dual-agency explanation is a personal act of that one licensee. A broker who "approves" at a volume he cannot actually review is in Malul territory ("lack of involvement in the day-to-day operations").
- [INFERENCE] If salespersons or associate brokers are added to scale, 175.21's "personal guidance, instruction, oversight" applies to each of them, and the software becomes a supervision tool (policies, logs, checklists) rather than a substitute; office managers (§440(6)) and branch offices (§441-a(3)) are the statutory ways to add supervisory capacity.
- [INFERENCE] Nothing in the sources lets a broker delegate the *judgment* components of supervision to software; nothing forbids using software to execute and evidence supervision. DOS has not said whether automated review counts as the broker's "oversight"; a prudent reading is that it counts only where a named licensee actually reviews and can show it (the A5164 factor "availability to review and approve all documents" signals how the Legislature thinks about it).

### Gaps
- No DOS decision or court case addresses supervision of automated systems or of a brokerage whose consumer interactions are software-mediated.
- The HGAR "Real Estate In-Depth" legal column on the broker's duty to supervise after the 2021 housing-discrimination legislation could not be fetched (HTTP 503) — [Real Estate In-Depth](https://realestateindepth.com/legal-corner-brokers-duty-to-supervise-changes-in-light-of-the-new-housing-discrimination-legislation).
- Whether DOS would require a physical "definite place of business" to be staffed, and how it treats a broker working remotely from a licensed home office (175.25(c)(6) allows a licensed residence office), was not researched beyond §441-a(3).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Of 39 rows, the functions that NY law assigns to a licensed human and that a software product cannot take over are: being the broker with a NY office (1–3), accepting listings (17), negotiating for a party (24), presenting agency disclosure and dual-agency consent as the agent (10–11), supervising people and systems (33–35), holding fiduciary accountability (37–38); and to an attorney: drafting contract terms and giving legal advice (25, 27). Everything else is document, delivery, logging, scheduling, computation and compliance-checking work that DOS already allows unlicensed staff to do for broker approval.
- [INFERENCE] The economic claim "far less than 2.5–3% per side" is not itself regulated (no NY rate law; S9564 would even require the words "COMPENSATION IS NOT SET BY LAW AND IS FULLY NEGOTIABLE"); the regulated part is *who* performs the licensed acts and *who* gets paid from the commission (§442).
- [INFERENCE] The model that fits the sources is a licensed brokerage (attorney-broker as representative broker) using software for every A/P function, with the broker (and any sponsored licensees) personally performing the H functions, attorneys (independent of the brokerage for conflict reasons) drafting contracts, and no transaction-based payments to the unlicensed software owner. A model in which the software company itself contracts with buyers and sellers to sell or buy their homes for a fee, without a licensed person performing the §440 acts, is unlicensed brokerage under §440-a and §442-e on the text of the statute.

### Gap theme G1: is showing a home for a seller a licensed act?

### Takeaway
Yes on the best New York authority found: in 1993 the Department of State held that an unlicensed trainee who checked availability with the owners, discussed availability with a prospect and showed him other listings was acting as an unlicensed salesperson, and suspended the broker who let her. The case was a rental, but the statutory definition it applied ("offer for sale", "rent or place for rent") covers sales the same way, and DOS's own assistant list permits only "Schedule appointments for licensees to show listed property". No DOS ruling addresses a seller showing their own home, which the statute does not reach because it is not done "for another".

### Cited Findings
- DOS v. Ardelean, 96 DOS 93 (decision on a July 8, 1993 hearing): "Those activities clearly are referable to an attempt to rent real property, and, therefore, constitute working as an unlicensed salesperson"; conclusion of law: "By allowing Bugyi, who was not licensed … to ascertain from the owners of real property whether an apartment was available for rental, to discuss such availability with Mariani, and to show additional listings to Mariani, the respondent authorized, permitted and suffered Bugyi to act as an unlicensed real estate salesperson, thereby violating RPL § 442-c and demonstrating incompetency"; the broker's licence was suspended six months (with a separate fair-housing finding for failing to show apartments because of ancestry) [PRIMARY, accessed 2026-10-23] — [DOS decision 96 DOS 93 (PDF)](https://docs.dos.ny.gov/ooah/decisions/rebrokers/ardelean.pdf)
- RPL §440(3): a salesperson is a person associated with a broker "to list for sale, sell or offer for sale … or to negotiate the purchase or sale"; §440(1) broker acts are done "for another and for a fee" [PRIMARY, accessed 2026-10-23] — [nysenate §440](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/440)
- DOS assistant page lists "Schedule appointments for licensees to show listed property" and contains no line permitting unlicensed staff to show property or host open houses [PRIMARY, accessed 2026-10-23] — [DOS Unlicensed Real Estate Assistants](https://dos.ny.gov/node/64166)
- Searches for a NYSAR Legal Hotline answer or a DOS opinion on unlicensed showings of for-sale homes returned none; NYSAR's hotline is a member service at (518) 436-9727 [SECONDARY, search summary, accessed 2026-10-23] — [NYSAR legal update listing](https://nysar.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/2023_December_Legal_update.pdf)
- Other states write the rule down: Texas requires a licence to show a broker's listings (Rule 535.4(c)), Illinois bars unlicensed assistants from hosting open houses or showing property [SECONDARY, search summary, accessed 2026-10-23] — [TREC](https://www.trec.texas.gov/node/676); [Illinois Admin. Code 1450](https://witnessslips.ilga.gov/ftp/JCAR/AdminCode/068/068014500G07400R.html)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] For classification: accompanied showings and open houses run by the brokerage for a seller are "Human or licence required" on legal grounds (Ardelean, §440(3), the assistant list) as well as practical ones (presence, safety). The contradiction-free workaround is the owner: a seller who shows or opens their own home is not acting "for another". Scheduling, access codes, route planning and feedback stay fully automatable.
- [INFERENCE] This sharpens A6: a buyer-side service with "no human agent for tours" is not available inside a brokerage; buyer tours need a licensee (the broker or a sponsored salesperson paid only through the broker, §442-a), or the listing side's owner/agent.

### Gaps
- Ardelean is a 1993 rental decision; no sales-specific DOS ruling or court case on showings by unlicensed people was found. A written inquiry to DOS Division of Licensing Services would settle it.

### Gap theme G2: fiduciary case law beyond Dubbs

### Takeaway
The Court of Appeals in Rivkin (2008) held that an individual buyer's agent may not represent competing buyers on the same property without disclosure and consent, but the brokerage firm as a whole need not stop other affiliated agents from doing so; and a DOS appeal decision (Lemecke) holds that failing to give the §443 form is a violation without any intent. Both bear directly on a software brokerage that serves many buyers and both sides.

### Cited Findings
- Rivkin v Century 21 Teran Realty LLC, 2008 NY Slip Op 03517 (Apr 24, 2008): certified question answered "in the negative"; "[s]imultaneously representing multiple buyers with respect to the same property is inconsistent with these duties"; "practical considerations cause us to draw a distinction between the fiduciary duty" owed by an individual agent and the duty of the firm; agents "only earn commissions for sales to their own clients" so there is "no incentive" to under-serve [PRIMARY, accessed 2026-10-23] — [Rivkin at LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/nyctap/I08_0056.htm)
- DOS appeal decision (Lemecke): "The Legislature in enacting Real Property Law §443 (3) did not require an element of intent"; "The mere failure to provide a client with the disclosure form, by itself, violates the statute"; facts included a buyer's broker who never conveyed the seller's counter-offer to his buyer; six-month suspension effective June 1, 2004 [PRIMARY, accessed 2026-10-23] — [DOS appeal decision, Lemecke](https://docs.dos.ny.gov/ooah/decisions/appeals/17%20DOS%20APP%2004%20Lemecke.htm)
- §443(1) "Designated sales agent" is "a licensed real estate salesperson or associate broker, working under the supervision of a real estate broker, who has been assigned to represent a client when a different client is also represented by such real estate broker in the same transaction" [PRIMARY, accessed 2026-10-23] — [nysenate §443](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/443)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Rivkin's individual-agent rule maps onto software: if one licensee (the attorney-broker) is the agent for every buyer, every pair of buyers bidding on the same home triggers the individual-agent rule; the product needs a conflict check across buyers and a disclosure-and-consent step, not only a seller/buyer dual-agency step.
- [INFERENCE] Lemecke makes the §443 form a strict-liability step: the software must make it impossible to proceed to a listing agreement or buyer agreement without a delivered, acknowledged (or sworn-refused) form, and must log it.
- [INFERENCE] Designated agents need at least two licensed salespersons or associate brokers under the broker; a one-licensee brokerage cannot use them.

### Gaps
- No NY case tests whether a written "non-agent" engagement under §443(5) negates fiduciary duties.

### Gap theme G5: business development and solicitation rules

### Takeaway
Advertising the brokerage itself is regulated advertising (websites, e-mail, business cards, signs) and only the broker may place it; direct solicitation is restricted only in the single remaining cease-and-desist zone (Brooklyn) and by the blockbusting ban; no Hudson Valley solicitation zone exists.

### Cited Findings
- 175.25(a)(1): "'Advertising' and 'advertisement' mean promotion and solicitation related to licensed real estate activity, including but not limited to, advertising via mail, telephone, websites, e-mail, electronic bulletin boards, business cards, signs, billboards, and flyers"; an initial e-mail to a client or potential client must carry the required broker information (175.25(d)(4)) [PRIMARY, accessed 2026-10-23] — [DOS booklet March 2026](https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/03/real-estate-license-law_03.2026.pdf) pp.29–30
- 175.17(a)(3)(ii): the only designated cease-and-desist zone is "County of Kings", expiration "July 1, 2028" (Community Board 17); solicitation under 175.17(a)(4) includes telephone, mail, delivery services and personal contact, but not classified ads in non-real-estate periodicals, public-view ads, or radio/TV [PRIMARY, accessed 2026-10-23] — [DOS booklet March 2026](https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/03/real-estate-license-law_03.2026.pdf) pp.27–28

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Every automated marketing touch for the firm (site pages, e-mail sequences, social posts, mailers) is a 175.25 advertisement: it needs the broker's name and contact, the fair-housing homepage link (175.29(c)) and broker approval of the template. ### Gap theme G6: federal lead-paint duty on agents

### Takeaway
Federal rules put a duty on the agent, not only the seller: for pre-1978 housing the agent must inform the seller of the disclosure obligations and ensure compliance, with a liability shield only for what the seller hid.

### Cited Findings
- 40 CFR 745.115(a): the agent must "ensure compliance with all requirements of this subpart", by informing the seller of obligations under §§745.107, 745.110 and 745.113 and either confirming the seller completed them or personally ensuring they are met; (b): an agent who informed the seller is not liable for hazards the seller knew of but did not disclose to the agent [PRIMARY, accessed 2026-10-23] — [40 CFR 745.115 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/40/745.115)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] This is a checklist-and-evidence duty that software can carry fully (year-built trigger, forms, signatures, the 10-day inspection opportunity in the offer), which is why the deliverable classes disclosure delivery as fully automatable.
