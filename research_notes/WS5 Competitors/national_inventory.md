## Key question 1 — AI-native agent-replacement startups, 2023–2026 (buyer-side AI agents, seller-side AI listing platforms, AI negotiation, AI transaction coordinators)

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 the "AI replaces the agent" field is small, early and almost entirely buyer-side. The entrants with verifiable traction are Homa (FL/TX, hybrid AI + hourly licensed humans, now ~1% fee), Ridley (seller-side, launched Colorado July 2025, $6.4M seed, claims 250+ sales), reAlpha/Claire (Nasdaq micro-cap, Florida-only, 75% rebate), Landian (flat-fee referral marketplace that has dropped its "AI" framing), and TurboHome (salaried flat-fee buyer agents in CA/TX/WA). Several 2024-vintage "AI brokerages" are already dark or pivoted (Modern Realty, Bramble, GetMyHome.ai, probably Alokee; Aalto closed). None of them has a verified presence in New York State or the Hudson Valley, and every survivor puts a licensed human on offers, negotiation and closing.

### Inferences
- The field has converged on one shape: AI for search, comps, document summaries and offer drafting; licensed humans (hourly or salaried) for showings, offers, negotiation and closing; pricing drifting to ~1% or a $5k–$20k flat fee rather than zero. The pure "AI is the agent" pitch (Modern Realty 2024, Homa's original DIY vision, reAlpha's "zero-commission") has been walked back by every company that survived [INFERENCE from the findings above].
- Attrition is high and fast: of the 2023–2024 cohort (Modern Realty, Bramble, Alokee, Aalto, GetMyHome), none is verifiably operating its original consumer model in October 2026 [INFERENCE].
- No AI-native entrant has a verified New York presence. New York's conventions (attorney-drafted contracts, co-op boards in NYC, agency-disclosure regime) are not addressed by any of them; the Homa structure described in Florida relies on a "transaction broker" arrangement whose availability under New York agency law the attorney should check [INFERENCE — flagged for the New York attorney].

## Key question 2 — Flat-fee and discount brokerages and FSBO platforms, with New York coverage checked

### Takeaway
Flat-fee MLS entry is a commodity in New York: Clever's September 2026 list counts 27 providers at $89–$499 upfront (some adding 0.25%–1.25% at closing), mostly reaching OneKey MLS, with only a handful (Hauseit, Brokerless, Houzeo, a few regionals) showing specific Hudson Valley or upstate reach. ### Inferences
- The price floor for "a listing on the MLS" in New York is ~$99; the floor for "a human who will negotiate" is ~1% (Yoreevo/Hauseit/Houwzer-class) or ~$4,000–$6,000 flat (Ridley's $3,999 tier, Landian's $5,999 band, Alloy's $2,000–$6,500 "fair value"). A New York product priced far below 2.5–3% per side would compete directly with Hauseit on the seller side and with the NYC rebate brokers on the buyer side, both of which already exist and advertise "up to 2%" back [INFERENCE].
- Nobody in the table drafts the New York contract of sale: Ridley's "create contracts and closing documents" and FSBO.com's "PACT" are the only contract-generation features found, and neither names New York practice [INFERENCE].
