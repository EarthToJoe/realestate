# Riskiest assumptions, ranked, with kill criteria

Scores: **Fatal** = how much of the venture dies if the assumption is false (1 minor to 5 the whole idea).
**Uncertain** = how little we know today (1 near-certain to 5 no idea). Rank = Fatal x Uncertain.
"Tested by" names the research workstream (WS) or the real-world test that settles it. Status is updated
as findings land; kill criteria are stated so a bad finding is recognised, not rationalised.

## What the research changed (2026-10-23)

The six research reports, the memo on the father-in-law's roles and an independent fact-check have landed, and
several findings go against the brief. **The brief's version of the idea does not survive.** New York does not let
software or a company draft the contract, and in all nine Hudson Valley counties the seller's attorney drafts it, so
"the lawyer is out of the loop" is **hit** (A3). Buyer tours need a licensed person in the door, so a human-free
buyer's agent is **hit** (A6). Buyers will keep hiring their own inspector, so the free inspection as pitched is a
pure cost (A9 **hit**). No Hudson Valley MLS or portal accepts listings from software, so a person keys every
listing (A4's automation half **hit**). Pitched as "software that completely replaces the agent", the venture
**hits K4**. With a buyer's agent in the deal, as 88% of buyers have, a seller saves about a third of today's
commission, not most of it (K1 **partly hit** for the pitch). Buying customers with advertising costs more than a
flat fee earns, so K2 is hit the day the firm has to buy listings. And the father-in-law's ten years away from New
York weakens the local-trust premise behind A5, which is why the new top-ranked assumption is **A12: his network
still produces listings.** What survives is narrower and still viable: a seller-first, flat-fee brokerage with
the father-in-law as broker of record, software doing the file work, and every licensed act visibly his. Cheap
to open (**$8,236** in year one, lean), it breaks even at **2 closings** (lean) or **25** (realistic) at a
$4,500 fee, faces no low-fee or AI competitor with an office in the eight counties, and can get on the MLS.
The tests that decide it are not more research: they are ten phone calls by him, a few calls to the Department of State (DOS) and the MLS, and the first ten listings.

**Joe, 2026-10-23 (decision 21): the original idea is kept.** "Keep the original idea, that is what I said I would
build. I can pare it back on the future if need be. The idea is the idea. Completely replace the real estate agents."
The evidence and the results above stand unchanged; every failed or partly failed kill test is now carried as a
hurdle the idea has to clear (see the dated lines in the Status column and the [hurdle map](PLAN.md#hurdle-map)). The
"narrower" business described in the paragraph above is a fallback Joe may choose later, not the plan. Re-ranking for
decision 21: A6's Fatal score goes back up (the buyer side is core again), and a new A18 (the contract step without a
lawyer drafting) enters at rank 3, because "the lawyer is out of the loop" is part of the plan again and its open part is
genuinely uncertain. Other scores are unchanged.

Where workstreams gave one assumption different statuses, the status above takes the most adverse finding that
the evidence supports, and keeps the other workstreams' points in the sentence.

  is satisfied by one broker (WS2), but his personal acts per deal (WS1) and MLS review minutes (WS3) make scale
  an hours question that only the pilot can answer (WS4). Recorded as partly hit, open on scale.

  WS2 owns the law, so hit.
- **A4.** WS3 and WS2 said partly hit, WS4 dodged (cost), WS5 partly dodged, WS7 still open. The automation half is
  hit (no API, a direct kill condition in the assumption's text); the cost and access halves are dodged. Partly hit.

  assumption is about buyers using a service with no human, and both the law's best reading and the market say no.

- **K3.** WS5's deliverable said the fixed-fee exception was "not safe to rely on"; the fact-check reversed that
  (Op. 1117, 2017, fn. 1 restates Op. 1015 after Op. 1043). The fact-check wins. [→ fact-check](research/fact-check.md#check-1-the-dual-role-question-how-ops-1015-1043-1117-and-later-opinions-fit)

  fact-check left this unresolved. Plan on five, with a $2,500 placeholder for a second MLS.
