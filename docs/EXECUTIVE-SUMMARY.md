# A Hudson Valley brokerage with you as broker and software doing the work: what the research found

*October 2026. Every number below comes from one of six research reports, an independent fact-check, or a memo on
your roles; the links go to the source. Research to plan with, not legal advice.*

## The idea, as the research reshaped it

I started with "software that completely replaces both agents and the lawyer". The research says New York does
not allow that and buyers do not want it. A licensed broker must make the core decisions on every deal; only a
lawyer may draft or explain the contract; and every company that tried an AI agent with no human brought the
humans back. ([WS1](research/WS1-agent-functions.md), [WS2](research/WS2-legal-licensing.md),
[WS5](research/WS5-competitors.md))

What survives is a business I think is better suited to you:

- **A licensed Hudson Valley brokerage, with you as broker of record.** As an admitted New York attorney you can get
  the broker licence for **$185, with no course, experience or exam**. ([WS2 §1](research/WS2-legal-licensing.md#1-a-licensed-broker-is-required-for-every-core-agent-act-and-software-cannot-hold-the-licence))
- **The software does the file work.** It handles intake, pricing analysis, listing copy (checked for fair-housing
  problems), the MLS package, scheduling, message drafts, offer comparison, deadlines and records. **You make every
  licensed decision on screen**, and each decision is logged.
- **Each side keeps an independent lawyer.** In all nine Hudson Valley counties the seller's attorney already
  drafts the contract, and the firm hands it a complete, clean package. The pitch is "a cheaper, faster lawyer
  step", not "no lawyer". ([WS2 §3](research/WS2-legal-licensing.md#3-in-the-hudson-valley-the-sellers-lawyer-drafts-so-no-lawyer-fights-local-custom-too))
- **Seller-first, at a flat fee** ($4,500 is the working figure). It pays buyers' agents a market rate by default,
  and offers buyers an optional rebate service with a licensed person at every tour.

## Why it can work

- **It is cheap to open.** In the lean case (licensed home office, you unpaid, no paid advertising) year one costs
  about **$8,236**, and **two closings** at $4,500 cover it. A realistic set-up (part-time coordinator, small office,
  outside legal review, $1,000 a month of brand marketing) costs **$62,575** and breaks even at **25 closings** a
  year. AI costs **$2 to $15 a side**. ([WS4](research/WS4-cost-model.md#the-verdict-in-three-numbers-fee-cost-of-a-customer-broker-hours))
- **One MLS membership reaches every portal.** OneKey MLS feeds Zillow, Realtor.com, Redfin and Homes.com free.
  Joining costs about **$3,821** in year one, and OneKey may not refuse a broker for offering less service.
  ([WS3](research/WS3-listing-data-access.md))
- **There is a gap in the local market.** Sellers here can buy an MLS-only listing for $69 to $1,499, or full
  service for $5,000 to $12,000. Nothing sits in between with an accountable local broker. No online, discount or AI
  brokerage has an office in the eight counties. ([WS5](research/WS5-competitors.md#in-the-eight-counties-low-fee-rivals-are-remote-small-or-human))
- **Sellers choose people, not prices.** 66% of sellers picked an agent who was referred or used before. Only 4%
  said the commission mattered most. A trusted local attorney is exactly what no funded competitor can copy.
  ([WS5](research/WS5-competitors.md#can-a-locally-connected-launch-win-a-small-niche-yes-the-briefs-version-no))
- **The New York lawyer step suits a lawyer-led firm.** None of the out-of-state AI entrants handles it.

## The hurdles, plainly

1. **The licence makes certain acts yours, on every deal.** You would accept each listing, approve each ad,
   present and advise on each offer, and negotiate. Buyer tours need a licensed person at the door. At launch that
   is about **20 hours a side**. The plan needs it to fall to about **15 or fewer**. Redfin, the only measured
   benchmark, implies about 70. Rubber-stamping is not allowed: in 2025 an appeals court upheld revoking a broker's
   licences for "wholesale failure to supervise". ([WS1](research/WS1-agent-functions.md#five-licensed-acts-set-the-products-shape),
   [WS4](research/WS4-cost-model.md#humans-in-every-deal-1020-broker-hours-a-side-if-the-software-works-far-more-if-it-does-not))
2. **The lawyer step stays.** Neither the firm nor its software may draft the contract or give legal advice. You
   cannot be either party's lawyer on a deal where the firm earns a commission that depends on closing; no client
   consent cures that conflict. ([WS2 §2, §7](research/WS2-legal-licensing.md#7-what-the-father-in-law-may-and-may-not-do))
3. **A person enters every listing on the MLS.** No Hudson Valley MLS or portal accepts listings from software. The
   software prepares and checks the whole listing; a person keys it in, and you review it, in about 20 to 45 minutes
   of your time. The MLS also requires you to join the REALTOR association (HGAR, about $706 in year one).
   ([WS3](research/WS3-listing-data-access.md#no-write-api-a-person-submits-each-listing))
4. **Buyers' agents, and what sellers really save.** 88% of buyers use an agent, and listings that pay buyers'
   agents less sell measurably worse. On a $510,000 house, a seller who pays the buyer's agent the usual 2.21% saves
   about **$7,800, a third** of today's roughly 4.6% total commission (a thin survey, so indicative only). The saving
   is 66% only if we also represent the buyer, and 81% only if the buyer has no agent. "Save about a third, more if
   you buy with us too" is the honest headline. ([WS4](research/WS4-cost-model.md#what-the-seller-really-saves-a-third-unless-the-buyer-side-comes-too))
5. **We cannot afford to buy customers.** A client won through search or Zillow ads costs **$2,780 to $8,120**.
   Each $4,500 sale leaves about $2,600 to $4,150 to cover that. Purplebricks entered this market in 2018 with a
   flat fee, spent about $15,000 per listing it won, and left the US in 2019. The plan works only on referrals.
   ([WS4](research/WS4-cost-model.md#kill-criteria-where-the-numbers-land), [WS5](research/WS5-competitors.md#why-earlier-attempts-failed-cost-capital-and-the-buyers-agent))
6. **Your ten years away, and the New York office.** Since 2010, 42.5% of owner households in the eight counties
   have moved in. Nationally, agents in their first two years get almost none of their business from referrals,
   and after ten years away you start closer to them than to a local veteran. Your network has to be tested, not
   assumed. The firm also needs a physical New York office with a sign: as a non-resident you cannot use the
   out-of-state exemption, and New York has no reciprocity. Your bar registration must be current for the no-exam
   route. Your malpractice policy will almost certainly not cover brokerage claims, so the firm buys its own errors
   and omissions (E&O) insurance, about $708 a year. ([WS5](research/WS5-competitors.md#can-a-locally-connected-launch-win-a-small-niche-yes-the-briefs-version-no),
   [memo](research/memo-father-in-law.md#running-a-new-york-brokerage-from-out-of-state))

## Competitors

About 70 companies were reviewed. The idea is already in motion, but it has been split into seller-side and
buyer-side halves, and each half has gone back to human checkpoints.

- **LocQube** is closest. It is a licensed Bronx brokerage that has offered an **$899 AI-written listing** (or 1.99%
  with an agent) since April 2026. It names New York and Connecticut, with Westchester the nearest named area, and
  serves sellers only.
- **Homa** runs an AI buyer brokerage at 1% in Florida, Texas and California. It raised $5 million this month.
- **Ridley** sells AI listings in three Sun Belt states.

In the eight counties the low-fee options are remote or small: Redfin at 2% with a $6,000 New York minimum, served
from White Plains and hiring in Orange County; Opendoor's cash offers statewide; 845 Realty Guys at $9,995; NY Flat
Fee Realty at 1%. Nobody local combines software, a low flat fee and an attorney-broker. The window is probably a
year or two, not a decade. ([WS5](research/WS5-competitors.md))

## The free inspection

**Buyers will still hire their own inspector**, so the inspection would not save anyone a second one. In New York,
buyers inspect before signing the contract. The inspection industry's own pre-listing contract says the report is
"not a substitute for a buyer's home inspection". England dropped seller-supplied home packs because fewer than one buyer in
six trusted them. A full upstate package costs **$550 to $2,250 per listing**, paid whether or not the house sells.
It also turns the seller's "unknown" into "known", which they must then disclose. Your friend would need a New York
inspector's licence and would have to work for the seller at his normal price. A free favour to a firm that steers
buyers to him is the arrangement federal anti-kickback law forbids. **Verdict:** drop it as pitched, and run a capped
pilot on the first ten listings (about $10,000) with the seller as the inspector's client. The new line is "you'll
know what a buyer's inspector will find before they do, and you decide what to fix". ([WS6](research/WS6-free-inspection.md#three-designs-one-recommendation))

## Your role: two options

([memo](research/memo-father-in-law.md))

- **A. Broker only.** This is the simplest option. The firm charges a flat fee or a commission at closing. Sellers
  and buyers hire independent attorneys, and the firm keeps a directory of them with no referral payments. You
  approve every licensed step.
- **B. Broker and the seller's lawyer, on some listings.** This is allowed only if the brokerage fee is fixed,
  non-refundable and owed whether or not the house sells, and the seller consents in writing. Your legal fee is
  billed by your own law practice, never by the firm, and you are never the buyer's lawyer. The ethics opinions that
  allow it (NYSBA 1015 and 1117) are advisory and have not been restated since 2017, so get written ethics advice
  first. It also means active registration, CLE, a New York law office and malpractice cover.
  ([fact-check](research/fact-check.md#check-1-the-dual-role-question-how-ops-1015-1043-1117-and-later-opinions-fit))

Both options need the $185 licence, a New York office with a sign, and your recorded approval of every licensed act.

## What the demo shows

The demo follows one sale of a real Dutchess County house, 146 Rt 292 in Holmes, over about 14 minutes. The house is
not for sale, and every screen says so. The property facts and eight comparable sales come from county records, and
the people are fictional. There are ten screens, from intake to closing. Each screen shows what the AI drafts and
then stops at the person who must act. There are 24 such checkpoints, and the timeline at the end is built from them,
not drawn by hand. Some moments to watch:

- the fair-housing checker catches a planted phrase and names the rule it breaks;
- the MLS step shows a finished listing package and says plainly that a licensed person keys it in;
- the contract draft appears in the seller's attorney's workspace, not the firm's.

The MLS, the portals, sent messages and signatures are simulated. ([plan](PLAN.md#what-the-demo-shows-versus-what-is-real))
