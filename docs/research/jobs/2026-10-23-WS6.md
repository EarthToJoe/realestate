# Your workstream: WS6, the free upfront inspection offering

Brief's exact ask: "Free upfront inspection offering: evaluate the pros, cons, liability exposure, and
whether buyers would actually skip their own inspection. Note my seller-reluctance concern." From the brief's
background: "offer a free, comprehensive upfront home inspection (he has a friend upstate who could do it)
before listing. The seller gets the chance to fix everything first, the buyer may not feel the need to pay
for their own inspection, and the seller doesn't pay for a second one. My reservation: not every seller will
want to make all the fixes. Evaluate this honestly, including liability."

Specific requirements:
- Cover: NY home-inspector licensing (RPL Article 12-B) and whether the friend is licensed or would need to
  be; who the inspector's client is and who may rely on the report; conflict-of-interest rules for inspectors
  and for brokers recommending or paying for inspections (including any NY rule on inspectors working for or
  being paid by the brokerage, and RESPA where relevant); what happens to the seller's disclosure duties once
  a defect is known (Property Condition Disclosure Statement, misrepresentation, the NY caveat emptor
  exceptions); liability exposure for the brokerage, the inspector and the seller, and insurance that covers
  it; evidence on whether buyers skip their own inspection when a pre-listing report exists (lender rules,

  raise prices; the cost per inspection in the Hudson Valley (dated); and Joe's seller-reluctance concern
  (what sellers do with a report they don't like, and the disclosure trap it creates).
