# Your workstream: WS1, agent function inventory

Brief's exact ask: "Agent function inventory: start by figuring out everything a NY buyer's and seller's
agent actually does. Pull from the NY licensing course curricula (what classes they take, what they're
trained on), broker duties, fiduciary expectations, and typical transaction timelines. Mark each function as
fully automatable, partially automatable, or requires a human/licensed person."

Notes files (yours to append to):
- `research_notes/WS1 NY agent functions/licensing_curriculum_and_duties.md`
- `research_notes/WS1 NY agent functions/transaction_tasks_and_timeline.md`

Deliverable: `docs/research/WS1-agent-functions.md`
Handoff: `docs/research/handoff/WS1.md`

Specific requirements:
- **Every function gets an ID F1, F2, F3, ...** that stays stable (other documents will cite them). Group
  them by transaction stage (business development and client acquisition; listing intake and agreements;
  pricing; marketing; showings; offers and negotiation; contract to close; post-close; ongoing duties and
  compliance), and cover both the seller's side and the buyer's side, plus the brokerage-level duties (broker
  supervision, record keeping, escrow, advertising rules, agency disclosure).
- The core is one inventory table: `ID | Function | Side (seller / buyer / both / brokerage) | Stage |
  Classification | Why (legal rule or practical reason) | What the software can do | Source`. Classification
  is exactly one of: **Fully automatable**, **Partially automatable**, **Human or licence required**. For
  "Human or licence required", name whether the reason is legal (cite the statute, regulation or rule, e.g.
  RPL 440, 442-d, 443, 175 NYCRR) or practical (physical presence, trust, judgment), so WS2's legal
  constraints (L-IDs, written in parallel) can be mapped to your F-IDs later.
- Explain what the NY 77-hour salesperson course and the broker course actually train (topics and hours),
  and which of those topics map to which F-IDs.
- Cover fiduciary duties (OLDCAR or the NY equivalent) and what each means for a software-led service.
- Give a typical Hudson Valley/upstate NY transaction timeline (listing to close, buyer search to close),
  with where attorneys, the licensee, the inspector, the appraiser, the lender and the title company act.
- End with counts: how many functions in each class, and which "Human or licence required" functions are
  the ones that most limit the business (the true bottleneck list).
- Handoff traceability items: every F-ID with its class and the human checkpoint it implies for the POC.
