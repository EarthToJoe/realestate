## 2. Kill criteria

- A9 kill criterion: hit for the proposal as stated (second limb: buyers do not skip); first limb dodged (liability containable). Report plainly: the "free inspection so buyers skip theirs" pitch should be dropped.
- K4 (pitched or built as autonomous): dodged at this step, provided the POC keeps the inspection, the PCDS and any condition statement behind human checkpoints (licensed inspector; seller's attorney; broker approval of any condition wording).
- K7 (needs outside capital): not hit by WS6, but pressure: Design 1 at 50–100 listings a year would be roughly $27,000–$225,000 a year of upfront spend on listings that may not close; the capped pilot (about $10,000) keeps it inside a cash-positive model.
- K5 (customers come for price): left open; a "free inspection" headline is a price-like lure, so the pilot should record how each seller found the firm.
- K3 (attorney earns commission while acting as counsel): tangential; if the father-in-law were to act as a seller's lawyer reviewing the report while his firm brokers the sale, he would hold it under a confidentiality duty and a disclosure duty at once. The Q4 provisional (he does not act as a party's lawyer) avoids this.
- K1, K2, K6, K8, K9: not affected by WS6 findings.

## 3. Questions for Joe

- Q-WS6-1. What is the friend's name, and is he a New York-licensed home inspector (or a NY-licensed architect or engineer)? Provisional: status unknown; the plan requires a current licence, which the software checks against the State register; the demo uses a fictional licensed inspector. Unblocks: whether the friend can be on the inspector list and in the pilot.
- Q-WS6-2. Will the friend work at his normal full price, paid by the brokerage, with the seller (not the brokerage) as his client, a signed disclosure that he knows your father-in-law, no repairs on houses he inspects, and nothing given back to the brokerage? Provisional: these are required; if he will not, he is not used. Unblocks: the Design 2 pilot provider.
- Q-WS6-3. Does your father-in-law accept dropping the "buyers won't need their own inspection" pitch from all marketing? Provisional: dropped; the new line is "you'll know what a buyer's inspector will find before they do, and you decide what to fix". Unblocks: demo script and marketing copy.
- Q-WS6-4. Pilot size and cap. Provisional: first 10 listings; cap $1,500 per listing (inspection plus radon, with water, septic and termite only where the house has them); paid once the listing agreement is signed, whether or not the house sells. Unblocks: WS4 cost line and the plan's phase budget.
- Q-WS6-5. Which design goes in the demo? Provisional: Design 2 as the main flow, with Design 3 (inspection credit plus a disclosed list of three licensed inspectors) as the branch for sellers who decline. Unblocks: POC screen list.
- Q-WS6-6 (father-in-law). Does he already have an E&O insurer from his law practice or the old brokerage that could quote real estate E&O and answer, in writing, whether a brokerage-funded inspection programme is covered? Provisional: the new brokerage buys real estate E&O and asks the carrier in writing before the pilot. Unblocks: launch of the pilot.

## 4. Traceability items (WS6-I IDs; map to WS1 F-IDs and WS2 L-IDs when those land)

Legal anchors in brackets so the orchestrator can match WS2 L-IDs.

- WS6-I1 Inspection offer screen: optional opt-in with a plain-language explainer that a report becomes something the seller must disclose; seller acknowledgment recorded. Checkpoint: seller consent. [RPL §§461–462; caveat emptor/Simone]
- WS6-I2 Inspector licence check: live lookup against the State register (data.ny.gov r9fr-48vu, refreshed daily) showing licence number, issue and expiry dates; booking blocked if not current; inspector's general liability and E&O certificates uploaded. Checkpoint: automatic block plus broker sign-off. [RPL §§444-d, 444-g(2), 444-o; 19 NYCRR §197-1.1]
- WS6-I3 Payment and conflict disclosure: one form stating the seller is the client, the brokerage pays a fixed retail fee not tied to the sale, personal relationships (e.g. the friend), and that the inspector gives the brokerage nothing; signed by seller, brokerage and inspector before booking. Checkpoint: all-party signatures. [19 NYCRR §197-4.7(b), (c), (e), (i); RPL §444-h(1)(c), (e); RESPA 12 U.S.C. 2607]
- WS6-I4 Scope builder: shows the licensed inspection and, as separate services with separate providers, radon (ELAP lab), well water (ELAP lab), septic, termite; lists what is not included (code compliance, mold, lead, buried tanks) unless added; shows each price and the cap. [RPL §444-b(4); 19 NYCRR §§197-5.8(c)(12), 197-5.16; PHL §502]
- WS6-I5 Pre-inspection agreement check: confirms the inspector's agreement contains the mandatory DOS clauses and a fee cap, and flags or strikes any clause making the seller pay the inspector's legal costs if a buyer sues. Checkpoint: broker review. [19 NYCRR §197-4.2; Smith-Hoy; Rector; InterNACHI template clause 5]
- WS6-I6 Seller-only report vault: report delivered to the seller only, within five business days; every view logged; brokerage access only after the seller's written consent; report treated as PII (photos of the home's interior) with export and delete. [RPL §444-g(3); 19 NYCRR §197-4.3; project PII rules]
- WS6-I7 Attorney review checkpoint: the seller's own attorney reviews the report against the PCDS before the seller signs; the software never pre-fills PCDS answers and works from the statutory text, not DOS's stale form link. Checkpoint: human (attorney), with reason shown. [RPL §462; LIBOR guidance that licensees must not help fill in the PCDS]
- WS6-I8 Repair-or-disclose screen: each finding gets "fix", "disclose" or "price/credit"; there is no "ignore"; the demo shows why (a false "No" after a report is evidence of concealment). [RPL §465; Simone v. Homecheck]
- WS6-I9 Disclosure attachments tracker: radon report (Q24), petroleum (Q26), mold (Q27), pest (Q31) reports flagged for attachment; water testing described (Q35); for pre-1978 houses every lead-related record queued for the buyer with the lead pamphlet and warning; revised PCDS prompted if the report post-dates an earlier PCDS. Checkpoint: broker confirms lead-record compliance as seller's agent. [RPL §462(2); 40 CFR 745.107, 745.115; 42 U.S.C. 4852d]
- WS6-I10 Broker knowledge checkpoint: if the seller shares the report with the brokerage, the licensed broker reviews findings as known material facts for disclosure to buyers. Checkpoint: licensed broker. [RPL §443 form; RPL §441-c]
- WS6-I11 Condition-claims copy guard: the listing and marketing generator blocks "pre-inspected", "certified", "no issues", "move-in ready per inspection", "no need for your own inspection" and any summary of the report; any condition statement needs broker approval. Runs beside the fair-housing checks. [RPL §441-c "misleading advertising"; Jablonski; pending S8729-B (GBL §349)]
- WS6-I12 Buyer-facing notice (only if the seller chooses to share the report): "prepared for the seller; not a substitute for your own inspection; you may hire any licensed inspector"; buyer acknowledgment recorded; no public hosting of the report. [RPL §444-g(1); Sykes; Burbach pattern avoided; PCDS buyer warning]
- WS6-I13 Decline path: sellers who decline see the alternative (inspection credit; list of at least three licensed inspectors, no payments to be listed, relationships disclosed). [DOS FAQ on consumer incentives; 19 NYCRR §197-4.7(e)]
- WS6-I14 Pilot metrics panel: per-listing inspection cost; seller decline rate; repairs made versus disclosed; renegotiation, fall-through and days to contract versus matched OneKey comparables; how each seller found the firm. This is the test that decides keep-or-kill for the narrowed design. [A9, K5, K7]

## 5. Open gaps that still matter, and what would close them

- The friend's licence and insurance: Joe supplies his name; one lookup on data.ny.gov (dataset r9fr-48vu) plus a request for his general liability and E&O certificates.
- Whether the brokerage's E&O policy covers claims from a brokerage-funded inspection programme: a written question to the E&O carrier (through the brokerage's insurance agent) before the pilot.
- The Department of State's view of brokerage-paid, seller-engaged pre-listing inspections with all-party disclosure: no guidance exists; a short written inquiry to DOS Division of Licensing Services citing 19 NYCRR §197-4.7(c), (e) and RPL §444-h(1)(c) would close it.
- Any New York appellate case on a buyer suing a seller's pre-listing inspector or a brokerage that distributed the report: none found on the open web; a Westlaw or Lexis search by the father-in-law's office (Second and Third Departments, "home inspector" with "negligent misrepresentation") would close it.
- Hudson Valley base inspection prices: quote-only; three phone quotes (for example Hudson Valley Property Inspections, 845-399-3983, listed on its pricing page) would give dated local figures.
- Who may sign a termite (NPMA-33) report in New York: forum claim only; DEC's Bureau of Pest Management can confirm.
- Whether a general report line such as "possible lead paint" triggers the federal lead-report duty: guidance silent; treated conservatively as yes; the seller's attorney decides case by case.
- Measured effects (price, speed, fall-through) and seller decline rates: no study exists anywhere found; only the pilot (WS6-I14) can answer.
