## 2. Kill criteria

- K4 (pitched or built as autonomous) | **hit if the venture is pitched as "software that completely replaces the agent"**; dodged by a licensee-in-the-loop design. 21 functions need a person and 46 more need a per-case human step.
- A6's kill criterion ("showings … legally or practically require a licensee") | **met** for brokerage-run tours (F39, F40).
- A3's kill criterion | **first half met** (a lawyer on each side is the Hudson Valley norm; F52, F53); second half is WS2's.
- A1's kill criterion ("a licensed person must personally perform core acts … in a way that needs an agent per deal anyway") | **partly met**: personal performance of negotiation, listing acceptance and tours is required; "an agent per deal anyway" is not established, it becomes broker hours per deal (WS4).
- K1 (economics need the buyer-agent fee to disappear) | **partly relevant**: the buyer side is the most human-heavy side (F39, F47–F49, F51), so any model that relies on serving buyers without people is weak; numbers are WS4's and WS5's.
- K3 (attorney earns a commission while acting as counsel) | not tested by WS1; the inventory separates broker acts (F48) from attorney acts (F52, F53), consistent with WS2's notes.
- K5 (customers come for price, not the attorney) | supported in direction: acquisition is trust-led (F4, F84).
- K9 (MLS membership) | open; F28 and F29 depend on it (WS3).
- K2, K6, K7, K8 | not addressed by WS1.

## 3. Questions for Joe (and his father-in-law)

- **Q-WS1-1. Who takes buyers on tours?** (a) Your father-in-law personally; (b) one or more licensed salespersons he sponsors, paid only through the brokerage; (c) no buyer tours at launch, listing side only. *Provisional:* (a) at launch, (b) once volume exceeds his hours; on our own listings, sellers host showings and open houses themselves. *Unblocks:* the buyer tour screen (a "licensed showing agent assigned" checkpoint) and WS4's hours.
- **Q-WS1-2. Will he personally negotiate every offer at launch?** *Provisional:* yes; the POC shows a "licensee negotiates" checkpoint with software-drafted counters for his approval. *Unblocks:* the offers screens.
- **Q-WS1-3. When our seller meets our buyer: dual agency with written consent, or represent the seller only?** *Provisional:* represent the seller only on our own listings; offer the buyer a referral to an outside agent or the unrepresented path with the agency form; dual agency only with written informed consent, and never marketed as "full representation of both sides". *Unblocks:* consent screens and the "replace both agents" wording in the demo and plan.
- **Q-WS1-4. Will the brokerage ever hold deposits?** *Provisional:* no; the seller's attorney holds them, as is the Hudson Valley custom, so no broker escrow account (175.1) at launch. *Unblocks:* whether the POC needs an escrow ledger.
- **Q-WS1-5. Ask your father-in-law to confirm today's customs in his counties** (who drafts, usual deposit, mortgage-commitment period, whether brokers attend closings). *Provisional:* the NYSBA county sheets (2018–2019) used in the deliverable. *Unblocks:* timeline defaults and the contract step.
- **Q-WS1-6. Send a written question to the Department of State?** About which tasks software may perform under a broker's supervision and whether unaccompanied or unlicensed showings of for-sale homes are licensed acts. *Provisional:* draft it for his signature (he is the licensee) and send it after the meeting. *Unblocks:* the largest remaining legal uncertainty in the inventory.
