## 3. Questions for Joe (and his father-in-law)

- **Q-WS5-1. Who are the first ten sellers he could call this month?** Names, not categories. Include estates, divorces, relocations, and owners selling to someone they know.
  - *Provisional:* treat the network as unproven. Before building past the demo, run a 30–60 day "ten calls" test; if fewer than a handful would list, re-plan around a local partner broker.
  - *Unblocks:* A5, K2, and whether the build goes past the demo.
- **Q-WS5-2. Will he (or a trusted local licensee) be physically present in the Hudson Valley for listing appointments?** Also: is his NY attorney registration active rather than "retired"? A retired registrant may not practise law (22 NYCRR Part 118).
  - *Provisional:* assume yes to both; if not, the "known local lawyer" positioning weakens to "remote attorney-broker plus software", which is the Redfin/Purplebricks model.
  - *Unblocks:* A5, the brand story, WS2's licensing path.
- **Q-WS5-3. Lead pitch: "software replaces both agents and the lawyer", or "an attorney-run brokerage that sells your home for a flat fee, with an optional buyer rebate"?**
  - *Provisional:* the second, seller-first. The first hits K1, K4 and A6 on the evidence.
  - *Unblocks:* demo narrative, marketing copy, POC buyer-side depth (Q9).
- **Q-WS5-4. Default buyer-agent compensation on listings?**
  - *Provisional:* the seller chooses, with the firm recommending a market-rate offer (about 2–2.5%) and showing the steering evidence; never default to 0%.

## 4. Traceability items (WS5-C IDs; competitor features or failure modes the POC must answer or avoid)

- **WS5-C1** Every licensed act (listing agreement, price, offer, counteroffer, negotiation message) stops at a visible "broker approves" checkpoint. This answers the failure of the 2024 AI cohort and copies the shape every survivor converged on (Homa, Ridley, LocQube).
- **WS5-C2** The listing intake makes buyer-agent compensation an explicit seller decision, shown with local data and the steering risk; it is never silently zero (K1, K6; Barwick, Iowa Law Review).
- **WS5-C3** Pricing is one fixed fee shown up front, with no closing-day percentage or hidden minimum. Hidden minimums and closing fees are the main complaint against Houzeo and Hauseit-style offers, and OneKey bars calling services "free" while being paid.
- **WS5-C4** A savings comparison screen shows the venture's fee against local anchors on the seller's own price: Redfin 2% / $6,000 minimum; Hauseit $7,500 minimum; 845 Realty Guys $9,995; NYFFR 1%. Every figure is sourced and dated so the advertising stays accurate.
- **WS5-C5** Contract step: generate the fill-in offer data, then hand off to each party's NY attorney at a visible checkpoint. No competitor removes the NY attorney; even Opendoor uses one on every deal.
- **WS5-C6** The listing step shows which MLS gets the listing (OneKey and/or HVCR) and why. This fills the Columbia/Greene/northern Dutchess gap online vendors leave.
- **WS5-C7** The buyer flow is an optional rebate service with a licensed human (or a licensed showing agent) at tours and offers, and a written buyer agreement before the first tour. Do not demo an "AI buyer's agent with no human" (A6 hit; Homa's walk-back).
- **WS5-C8** Intake records how the client found the firm (referral, past client, law-client hand-off with Rule 5.7 notice, ad, web), so K5 and the ten-calls test are measured.
- **WS5-C9** When a law client is referred into the brokerage, show a Rule 5.7 written notice ("brokerage services are not legal services") and keep law-practice confidential data out of brokerage lead generation (Ops. 1043/1237, Rule 5.7).
- **WS5-C10** AI advice to customers (pricing, negotiation, compensation) passes a rules check and broker review before sending. A general chatbot told a Hudson Valley seller something he had to catch (NYT, May 2026), and Beycome disclaims its AI's output.
- **WS5-C11** No "autonomous", "no agent needed" or "no lawyer needed" language anywhere in the UI or the generated marketing (K4; WS2 on not telling parties they need no lawyer).
- **WS5-C12** The marketing step goes beyond MLS entry (photos, copy, portal syndication, open-house scheduling). Sellers rank marketing (23%) and pricing (19%) as what they most want; MLS-only vendors leave both to the seller.
- **WS5-C13** A dashboard placeholder tracks days-on-market and sale rate for the firm's listings against matched comparables, so a local steering penalty (K6) is detected early.
- **WS5-C14** Remote-friendly seller and buyer flows (e-sign, virtual tour, messaging) serve NYC-based second-home owners and buyers, whom no local network reaches.
- **WS5-C15** No AI-altered listing photos without disclosure (NY DOS Trend Alert, 2025-11-13). Competitors' AI staging features are a compliance trap.
- **WS5-C16** The demo's "AI does it" moments show speed and responsiveness (instant drafts, 24/7 replies), the advantage software has over capacity-capped local discounters (845 Realty Guys caps at 5 listings a month).

## 6. Contradictions with other workstreams' notes

- **WS2 deliverable L27 vs ethics opinions.** WS2's deliverable says he "may be the seller's lawyer and broker together if the brokerage fee is fixed, non-refundable…" (Op. 1015). NYSBA Op. 1043 (Jan 2015, five months later) says a lawyer "may not act as a lawyer and a broker in the same real estate transaction, with or without client consent" and does not mention 1015. Op. 1273 (2024) reaffirms the per se bar. WS2's own notes call the flat prohibition "the governing line". Suggest WS2 mark L27 as narrow and not to be relied on without ethics counsel. This does not affect the Q4 provisional plan, which avoids the dual role.
- **WS4 notes on Hauseit.** WS4 notes list Hauseit's Base/Core/Pro ($399/$699/$1,199) as available in the Hudson Valley. Hauseit's page says Core and Pro are NYC-only, so Hudson Valley sellers get Base $399 only. WS4's 1% / $7,500-minimum figure is correct.
