# New York keeps the broker and the lawyers

**Is the lawyer really out of the loop? No: New York lets the brokerage's software fill in the blanks of an offer, but it may not draft or explain the contract or tell anyone they do not need a lawyer, and in every Hudson Valley county the seller's lawyer customarily drafts the contract, so both sides will still have lawyers and the honest pitch is "a cheaper, faster lawyer step", not "no lawyer".** A licensed broker is also required: listing a home, marketing it for a fee, presenting offers and negotiating are licensed acts in New York whoever or whatever performs them; software cannot hold a licence, and what it does counts as the act of the broker who runs it (L1, L2, L18). The workable structure is the father-in-law as broker of record (as an admitted attorney he gets a broker licence for $185 with no course, experience or exam, L3) who personally approves every licensed act, with the software doing the clerical, drafting and scheduling work under his supervision. He may not be either party's lawyer in a deal where his brokerage earns a commission that depends on closing (a "per se", non-waivable conflict under the state bar's ethics opinions, L26), but he may be the seller's lawyer and broker together if the brokerage fee is fixed, non-refundable and paid whether or not the house sells, with the seller's written consent (L27). Joe may own equity in the brokerage and sell it software for a fixed fee, but may not be paid per deal or out of commissions (L7). These findings **hit kill criterion A3** (the "no lawyer" pitch must change) and **confirm K4** (no autonomous licensed acts); the right structure **dodges A2 and K3**; A1's question of scale stays open. Fair housing is where New York actually punishes brokers (licences revoked since 2020 for steering, "school talk" and unequal pre-conditions; 2025–2026 settlements for ignoring voucher holders), and since December 2025 New York also judges practices by their effect, so the copy, chat and lead-handling pipeline needs hard checks and logged tests even as federal enforcement retreats; disclosures, e-signatures and data rules are real but manageable as engineered paperwork. The full constraint table is in section 10 and the checkpoint map in section 11.

Plain-word glossary used below: **broker** = the licensed person or firm legally allowed to sell homes for others for a fee; **broker of record / representative broker** = the licensed individual who holds the firm's licence and answers for everything it does; **salesperson** = a licensee who may work only under a broker; **DOS** = the New York Department of State, which licenses and disciplines brokers; **UPL** = the unauthorised practice of law; **binder** = the short offer or deal sheet signed before the formal contract; **NYSBA** = the New York State Bar Association, whose ethics committee issues the opinions cited; **PCDS** = the Property Condition Disclosure Statement a seller must give a buyer.

## What decides go or no-go: three rules block the original idea, none blocks a broker-run product

Joe's brief describes software that "completely replaces the real estate agent" on both sides and "drafts the contract, so the lawyer is out of the loop as well" ([BRIEF](../BRIEF.md)). Three sets of rules block that version outright. First, the licensing statute makes listing, selling, "offer[ing] or attempt[ing] to negotiate" and collecting a fee "for another" the acts of a real estate broker, and forbids any "person, co-partnership, limited liability company or corporation" from doing them without a licence ([RPL §440, §440-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/440)) [PRIMARY]; nothing in the statute exempts software, so a company that ran the software and took the fee without a licensed broker would be committing a misdemeanour on every deal and could not sue for its fee ([RPL §442-d, §442-e](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442-E)) [PRIMARY]. Second, the contract cannot be the software's to write: New York lets a broker fill in the non-legal blanks of a bar-approved form, or a simple form made "subject to approval by each party's attorney", at no separate charge, and nothing more ([AG Formal Opinion 96-F11, 1996](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/96-F11_pw.pdf); [DOS Legal Memorandum LI04, 2020](https://dos.ny.gov/legal-memorandum-li04-real-estate-brokers-and-salespersons-and-unauthorized-practice-law)) [PRIMARY]. Third, the father-in-law cannot be the parties' lawyer on commission deals his brokerage handles ([NYSBA Op. 1117, 2017](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1117/)) [PRIMARY].

None of that blocks the product that survives: a licensed, attorney-led brokerage whose software does the work that New York already allows unlicensed staff and brokers' forms to do (drafting listings and ads for approval, scheduling, assembling disclosures, filling in offer forms, routing signatures, tracking deadlines), with the licensed human visibly approving each licensed act. That is a cheaper brokerage, not an agent-free one, and it is consistent with what WS5 found about who survives in this market ([WS5 notes](../../research_notes/WS5%20Competitors/why_attempts_failed_and_niche_evidence.md)) [INFERENCE].

## 1. A licensed broker is required for every core agent act, and software cannot hold the licence

The definition is functional. A broker is anyone who, "for another and for a fee, commission or other valuable consideration, lists for sale, sells, at auction or otherwise, exchanges, buys or rents, or offers or attempts to negotiate a sale" ([RPL §440(1)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/440), current text confirmed in [DOS's March 2026 compilation](https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/03/real-estate-license-law_03.2026.pdf)) [PRIMARY], and DOS summarises the trigger as anyone who, "on behalf of another and for a fee", negotiates a sale ([DOS broker FAQ](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-frequently-asked-questions)) [PRIMARY]. The statute regulates people and companies and never mentions technology, so the acts of a system are the acts of whoever operates it and takes the fee [INFERENCE]. The penalties make an unlicensed version unworkable: "the commission of a single act" is a misdemeanour prosecuted by the Attorney General; a consumer can recover between one and four times the fee; repeated acts are presumed to be for a fee; and no unlicensed person may sue to collect ([RPL §442-e](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442-E); [§442-d](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442-D)) [PRIMARY]. A Westchester court has awarded the maximum four times the commission where an unlicensed person pocketed a "broker's fee" ([Handy v. Cohen, Mount Vernon City Court 2003, as summarised by NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/node/169608)) [SECONDARY].

DOS's own list of what unlicensed staff may do shows where software fits. They may answer phones, arrange appointments, "Write ads for approval of broker", "Type contract forms for approval by broker", "Schedule appointments for licensees to show listed property" and "Gather information for a comparative market analysis"; anything specified in §440 is reserved to licensees; and "The licensed broker is explicitly responsible for the supervision and control of activities conducted in the name of the licensed real estate business" ([DOS, Unlicensed Real Estate Assistants](https://dos.ny.gov/unlicensed-real-estate-assistants)) [PRIMARY]. DOS also says the representative broker "is responsible for the supervision and conduct of the real estate brokerage business" and that "All listings, although perhaps negotiated by a salesperson, are accepted by the representative broker" ([DOS FAQ](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-frequently-asked-questions)) [PRIMARY]. The licensed acts the software must therefore stop at are: accepting a listing, placing advertising, presenting an offer or counteroffer, and negotiating price or terms. New York's agency-disclosure statute already defines a buyer's agent by conduct, as one who "finds a buyer for a property and presents an offer to purchase to the seller or seller's agent and negotiates on behalf of the buyer" ([RPL §443(1)(c)](https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/03/real-estate-license-law_03.2026.pdf)) [PRIMARY].

Two questions remain open. **Showings:** no DOS text says in so many words that letting a buyer into a house is a licensed act, but DOS lets unlicensed staff only schedule showings "for licensees"; the owner may always show his own home because he is not acting "for another" [INFERENCE]. Until DOS or counsel says otherwise, the product should treat each showing as a licensee-controlled step (L19). **AI as such:** no New York statute, regulation, DOS opinion or decision on software or AI performing brokerage acts was found after targeted searches of DOS hearing decisions and Attorney General releases [observation, 2026-10-23]. The signals point one way. A pending Assembly bill would require brokers using AI "virtual agents" to commission an annual independent disparate-impact audit and file a summary with the Attorney General ([A9028, in committee since 2026-01-07](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A9028)) [PRIMARY], and Maine's real estate commission told licensees in May 2026 that AI tools "lack the ability to exercise judgement, understand laws/rules, and bear no responsibility for their errors or omissions" ([Maine REC agenda, 2026-05-21](https://www.maine.gov/pfr/professionallicensing/sites/maine.gov.pfr.professionallicensing/files/inline-files/Real-Estate-Commission-Agenda-May-21-2026.pdf)) [PRIMARY document read via search summary; not NY law]. Neither contemplates an autonomous agent.

The broker requirement is not only a matter of who acts but of real control. DOS revoked a representative broker's licence where he "was not present in the brokerage office and did not manage, control or participate in the corporate real estate brokerage business" while a salesperson ran the firm under his licence and paid unlicensed people, holding that "In availing the corporate real estate broker license ... the representative real estate broker, engaged in fraud" ([DLS v. Flagship Marketing Group, 7 DOS 00, 2000](https://docs.dos.ny.gov/ooah/decisions/non_indexed/FLAGSHIP.htm)) [PRIMARY]. A brokerage in which Joe and the software ran everything while the father-in-law lent his licence would invite exactly that charge ("availing" the licence) [INFERENCE]. The design answer is a recorded approval by the broker on every licensed act, which the brief's demo requirement wants anyway.

Practical licence mechanics are light. An admitted New York attorney applies on DOS form DOS-0036-f-a by paper, ticks "Attorney Admitted to NYS Bar", and is "exempt from the educational, experience and examination requirements"; the fee is $185 for two years ([DOS broker application, Rev. 09/23](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-application)) [PRIMARY], made up of $155 plus a $30 anti-discrimination surcharge ([RPL §441-b](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/441-B)) [PRIMARY], and attorneys are exempt from the 22.5-hour continuing-education requirement ([DOS FAQ](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-frequently-asked-questions)) [PRIMARY]. An LLC brokerage is licensed through a member who "meets the qualifications for licensure"; its licence entitles "one member thereof or one manager thereof to act as a real estate broker", others need their own licence, and "No license as a real estate salesperson shall be issued to ... any manager or member of a limited liability company" licensed as a broker ([RPL §441-b(2)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/441-B)) [PRIMARY]. The firm needs DOS approval of its name before filing, an assumed-name filing if it trades under a brand ([GBL §130](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/130)) [PRIMARY], and "a definite place of business within this state" with a sign "readable from the sidewalk" (a home office is allowed "provided that it is properly licensed") ([RPL §441-a(3); 19 NYCRR 175.25(c)(6)](https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/03/real-estate-license-law_03.2026.pdf)) [PRIMARY]. MLS access needs more than the exemption: OneKey MLS requires a participant to be "a REALTOR of any Board and hold a valid state-issued Real Estate Broker's license" (OneKey Rule 202.1, per [WS3's notes](../../research_notes/WS3%20Listing%20and%20data%20access/mls_access.md)) [PRIMARY via WS3], which is a further reason the plan assumes he takes the licence (Q4).

## 2. The software may fill in an offer; only lawyers may draft or explain the contract

Three Judiciary Law sections draw the line. No natural person may "ask or receive, directly or indirectly, compensation for ... preparing deeds, mortgages, assignments, discharges, leases or any other instruments affecting real estate" without admission to the bar (§484, quoted in [AG 96-F11](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/96-F11_pw.pdf)) [PRIMARY]; no corporation may "render legal services of any kind" or "ask or receive directly or indirectly, compensation for preparing deeds, mortgages ... or any other instruments affecting real estate", and an officer's bar admission "shall not be held to permit or allow" it ([Judiciary Law §495](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/495)) [PRIMARY]; and §478 bars practising law or holding out as entitled to ([Judiciary Law §478](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/478)) [PRIMARY]. Violations are misdemeanours, a corporation can be fined up to $5,000, and false holding-out that causes a loss over $1,000 is a felony ([§495](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/495); [§485-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/485-A)) [PRIMARY]. The attorney-founder's membership of the company does not cure the company's drafting [INFERENCE from §495(2)].

Brokers have a narrow, long-standing privilege, and it defines what the software may produce. The Attorney General's 1996 opinion, which DOS says it applies in discipline cases, allows a broker to prepare "a simple fill-in-the-blanks purchase and sale contract" if it includes "a condition making it subject to approval by each party's attorney", or to use "a fill-in-the-blanks form that has been approved by a recognized bar association in conjunction with a recognized realtors' association", filling in only "non-legal provisions such as the names of the parties, the date and location of the closing, a description of the property, the consideration for sale and any other relevant facts"; the document must "clearly and prominently indicate on its face that it is a legally binding document and clearly and prominently recommend that the parties seek advice and counsel from their lawyers"; brokers "may not add provisions ... unless they make the entire contract subject to and conditioned upon the review and approval of each party's attorney", may prepare contracts "only as an incident of the purchase and sale", and "may not charge a separate fee for preparation of the contract or share in the fees of attorneys" ([AG 96-F11](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/96-F11_pw.pdf)) [PRIMARY]. DOS's 2020 memorandum restates the same rules and adds that brokers may not "discourage the parties from seeking advice from their attorneys" ([DOS LI04](https://dos.ny.gov/legal-memorandum-li04-real-estate-brokers-and-salespersons-and-unauthorized-practice-law)) [PRIMARY]. The courts behind these documents disciplined brokers who devised mortgage terms (Duncan & Hill, 62 A.D.2d 690), "inserted broad, legally significant contingency clause[s]" and charged a fee (Mulford, 1986), "discouraged purchaser from seeking legal advice" (Sorrentino, 1986), and "gave advice as to their legal effect" (Tucci, 1978) (all as quoted in [AG 96-F11](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/96-F11_pw.pdf)) [PRIMARY].

Advice is covered even when free and even when it is "only speech". In September 2025 the Second Circuit vacated an injunction that had let a nonprofit's trained non-lawyers give free legal advice to New Yorkers sued for debts, holding that New York's UPL law regulates speech in a content-neutral way subject only to intermediate scrutiny ([Public Citizen summary of Upsolve v. James, 2025-09-10](https://clpblog.citizen.org/second-circuit-holds-unlawful-practice-law-can-apply-to-debt-collection-advice/)) [SECONDARY], and the Supreme Court denied review on March 30, 2026 ([Supreme Court docket 25-948](https://www.supremecourt.gov/search.aspx?filename=/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-948.html)) [PRIMARY]. A chatbot that tells a buyer what a clause means for them, whether to sign, or which contingency to pick is therefore giving legal advice whether or not anyone pays for it [INFERENCE]. No New York authority addresses AI-generated documents as such; the functional test applies [observation].

A contract of sale is also governed by the statute of frauds: it "is void unless the contract or some note or memorandum thereof, expressing the consideration, is in writing, subscribed by the party to be charged, or by his lawful agent thereunto authorized by writing" ([GOL §5-703](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/5-703)) [PRIMARY]. An e-mail can satisfy it ([Naldi v. Grunberg, 1st Dep't 2010, via summaries](https://www.nycourts.gov/REPORTER/3dseries/2010/2010_07079.htm)) [SECONDARY], and NYSBA warns consumers that a binder, receipt or purchase offer "may constitute a binding contract obligating you to purchase the house" ([NYSBA LegalEase](https://nysba.org/legalease-buying-and-selling-real-estate/)) [SECONDARY], while courts refuse to enforce papers that show the parties did not mean to be bound until a formal contract was signed ([Piller v Marsam Realty, 2d Dep't 2016, via summary](https://nycourts.gov/REPORTER/3dseries/2016/2016_00952.htm)) [SECONDARY]. Where a contract is "subject to" attorney approval, either side's attorney may disapprove within the window for any reason or none ([Moran v. Erk, 11 N.Y.3d 452, 2008](https://www.law.cornell.edu/nyctap/I08_0176.htm)) [PRIMARY].

## 3. In the Hudson Valley the seller's lawyer drafts, so "no lawyer" fights local custom too

New York has no statutory attorney-review period; the practice is customary and regional. NYSBA's consumer guide says the first paper is usually prepared upstate by "a real estate agent or broker" and downstate by "the seller's attorney", placing upstate "north of Sullivan, Ulster and Dutchess Counties and parts of Greene and Columbia Counties", and says a broker-prepared contract "will usually be subject to approval by the parties' attorneys within a specified short time" ([NYSBA LegalEase](https://nysba.org/legalease-buying-and-selling-real-estate/)) [SECONDARY]. The Court of Appeals has noted that "Attorney approval contingencies are routinely included in real estate contracts in upstate New York" ([Moran v. Erk](https://www.law.cornell.edu/nyctap/I08_0176.htm)) [PRIMARY]. NYSBA's county-by-county closing-customs surveys settle the starting market: **all nine Hudson Valley counties follow the downstate model.**

Legally, no statute forces a buyer or seller to hire a lawyer, and NYSBA's guide says only that consumers "should consider seeking legal advice" ([NYSBA LegalEase](https://nysba.org/legalease-buying-and-selling-real-estate/)) [SECONDARY]. But four things together keep lawyers in every Hudson Valley deal the brokerage touches. The brokerage may not draft the contract beyond fill-in (section 2). It must recommend counsel on the face of any paper it prepares and may never discourage counsel (L22, L23). Local practice hands the contract to the seller's attorney and the deposit to that attorney's escrow, so a seller without a lawyer would be asking the buyer's lawyer to draft against them, and a buyer without one faces a lawyer-drafted rider alone. And a lawyer for one side may give an unrepresented other side no advice "other than the advice to secure counsel" ([Rule 4.3, NYSBA Rules as amended through July 1, 2026](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/NYSBA-NY-Rules-of-Professional-Conduct-as-amended-through-July-1-2026-1.pdf)) [PRIMARY]. The product's lawful role is therefore to make the lawyer step faster and cheaper (a clean, complete offer package; all disclosures done on time; a shared timeline; a directory of independent local real-estate attorneys with published flat fees and no referral payments, L31), not to remove it [INFERENCE].

## 4. Fair housing is where New York actually punishes brokers

Federal law forbids any "notice, statement, or advertisement" about a home's sale that "indicates any preference, limitation, or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin" ([42 U.S.C. §3604(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/3604)) [PRIMARY]. New York's Human Rights Law protects more classes for sales and brokers: "race, creed, color, national origin, citizenship or immigration status, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, military status, sex, age, disability, marital status, status as a victim of domestic violence, lawful source of income or familial status", and bars a broker from circulating any statement or advertisement "which expresses, directly or indirectly, any limitation, specification or discrimination" on those grounds ([Executive Law §296(5)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/296)) [PRIMARY]. "Lawful source of income" includes Section 8 vouchers and all public assistance ([Executive Law §292(36)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/292)) [PRIMARY]. A finding that a licensee engaged in an unlawful discriminatory practice is "presumptive evidence of untrustworthiness" and grounds for revocation ([19 NYCRR 175.17(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/19-NYCRR-175.17)) [PRIMARY]. Westchester's county law adds further classes and its own enforcer ([Westchester Human Rights Commission, via search text](https://humanrights.westchestercountyny.gov/fair-housing)) [SECONDARY].

New York and Washington moved in opposite directions in 2025–2026, and the stricter state rule is the one the product must meet. HUD withdrew its April 2024 guidance on advertising housing through digital platforms (effective 2025-09-17, notice published 2026-04-06) ([Federal Register record 2026-06624](https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents/2026-06624.json); [Orrick summary](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-04-10/hud-withdraws-eight-fair-housing-guidance-documents-as-part-of-deregulatory-review/)) [PRIMARY metadata / SECONDARY], has proposed deleting its disparate-impact rule (not final as of 2026-10-23) ([HUD supplemental proposal, FR-6540-P-02](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-16228.pdf)) [PRIMARY], and told agents in April 2026 that merely discussing crime or school quality does not violate the federal Act ([NAR FAQ describing HUD's 2026-04-24 letter](https://www.nar.realtor/fair-housing/faqs-on-steering-crime-and-schools)) [SECONDARY]. New York instead wrote housing disparate impact into statute on 2025-12-19: a practice is unlawful if it "actually or predictably results in a disparate impact" on a protected group, unless it is "necessary to achieve one or more substantial, legitimate, nondiscriminatory interests" that "could not be served by another practice that has a less discriminatory effect", with the justification "supported by evidence and ... not ... hypothetical or speculative" ([Executive Law §296(5-a), Ch. 649 of 2025](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/296); [S4067-A](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S4067/amendment/A)) [PRIMARY]. A New York practitioner's reading is that what "HUD now permits federally may still violate the New York State Human Rights Law" ([HGAR, 2026-04-30](https://hgar.com/new-york-codifies-disparate-impact-a-defining-moment-for-fair-housing-compliance-and-the-effect-of-huds-recent-statement)) [SECONDARY]. Complaints may be filed with the state Division of Human Rights within three years ([Executive Law §297(5)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/297)) [PRIMARY]; the earlier notes' "one year" was wrong.

The enforcement record is the reason this section leads the product's guardrails. Newsday's review of DOS rulings found four agents disciplined for fair-housing violations in 2016–2019 against "more than 40 agents statewide" in 2020–2023, "with six license revocations and 21 suspensions"; school-district opinions, "positive or negative", drew penalties from a $500 fine to a three-month suspension because judges treated them as a proxy for racial steering ([Newsday, 2024-02-15, PDF copy](https://agmblaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/NYS-punishes-Long-Island-real-estate-agents-after-Newsdays-three-year-investigation-on-fair-housing-Newsday.pdf)) [SECONDARY]. DOS revoked a licence where an agent pointed a Black tester to other towns, cited "Wikipedia ... how many Haitians, how many whites, blacks" and required him, but not a white tester, to sign a buyer agreement before any showing ([DLS v. Abarca, 160 DOS 21, 2021](https://docs.dos.ny.gov/ooah/decisions/2021/Abarca,%20Aminta.pdf)) [PRIMARY]. The Attorney General settled with Long Island brokerages over unequal pre-approval and exclusivity demands ([AG, 2022-08-30](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2022/attorney-general-james-and-governor-hochul-tackle-discriminatory-practices-long); [AG, 2023-03-15](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2023/attorney-general-james-stops-discriminatory-practices-long-island-real-estate)) [PRIMARY] and with a New Rochelle firm whose agents turned away Section 8 applicants, which must now state on every listing that vouchers are accepted ([AG, 2024-02-04](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2024/attorney-general-james-stops-illegal-housing-discrimination-westchester-county)) [PRIMARY]. The 2025–2026 record is dominated by source-of-income cases found by testing: a brokerage whose agents "will stop responding to a person once they indicate that they will cover rent using a housing voucher" paid $40,000 and must place ten subsidy holders a year for three years ([DHR, 2025-12-01](https://dhr.ny.gov/news/40k-settlement-income-based-complaint)) [PRIMARY]; a race-steering case against buyers' agents ended with damages, logos, training and a year of monitoring ([DHR/Governor, 2026-04-07](https://dhr.ny.gov/node/8301)) [PRIMARY]; and an Orange County landlord was fined $10,000 for failing to document training it had promised in an earlier settlement ([DHR, 2025-03-31](https://dhr.ny.gov/node/7941)) [PRIMARY]. Testing is funded by a surcharge on every broker licence and by fines ([AG, 2025-02-21](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2025/attorney-general-james-announces-970000-grants-fund-fair-housing-programs)) [PRIMARY].

For software these cases translate into concrete rules [INFERENCE]. The model's words are the evidence, so the chat and e-mail channels must never offer opinions on schools, neighbourhood composition, crime or "who lives there", must give every user the same information at the same level of detail, and must apply the broker's published standardized operating procedures (ID, exclusive agreement, mortgage pre-approval) identically to everyone, because unequal pre-conditions are the conduct DOS revoked licences for ([RPL §442-h(4)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442-H)) [PRIMARY]. Listing copy must describe the property, not the people it suits: HUD's 1995 advertising memo (its text no longer on HUD's site) held that ads "should not express a preference or a prohibition, but can simply state nondiscriminatory facts", treating "master bedroom" and "mother-in-law suite" as physical descriptions ([Oregon REALTORS summary, 2024-05-03](https://oregonrealtors.org/news/tip-of-the-week/permissible-advertisements-under-the-fair-housing-act/)) [SECONDARY], but OneKey MLS's own word list, updated March 2025, flags "Mother-in-law apt.", "Family", "Exclusive", "Executive", "Private" and "Quality neighborhood" for removal (per [WS3's notes](../../research_notes/WS3%20Listing%20and%20data%20access/mls_access.md)) [PRIMARY via WS3], so the stricter MLS list plus New York's fifteen classes is the floor. Equal treatment includes speed: going quiet after a buyer or renter mentions a voucher is the conduct DHR settled in 2025, so response-time targets must be identical and logged per lead. And because New York now judges practices by their effect (§296(5-a)), every automated rule that sorts people (lead scoring, reply priority, which listings are shown, ad audiences, SOP prerequisites) needs a written, evidence-backed justification and a periodic test for disparate effect, kept at least three years [INFERENCE]. Ad targeting is the scaled risk: §3604(c) and §296(5)(c)(2) reach any advertisement that "directly or indirectly" expresses a limitation, and the Justice Department's 2022 settlement made Meta drop lookalike audiences for housing ads and bar targeting housing ads by age, gender or ZIP code ([Meta newsroom, updated Dec. 2023](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/01/an-update-on-our-ads-fairness-efforts/)) [PRIMARY]. HUD's withdrawn 2024 guidance no longer binds anyone, but its practical advice (housing ad category, no proxy targeting, monitor delivery, keep records) remains the best evidence of reasonable care under New York's effects test [INFERENCE]. The required notices are mechanical: the DOS housing-discrimination disclosure form (DOS-2156, current revision 11-25, [DOS](https://dos.ny.gov/housinganti-discrimination-form-dos-2156)) [PRIMARY] "upon first substantive contact" by e-mail, text, electronic message or paper (a link is fine if the message says what it contains; oral delivery does not count), kept three years with a sworn declaration if the person declines to sign ([19 NYCRR 175.28](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/19-NYCRR-175.28)) [PRIMARY]; a homepage link to DOS's fair-housing notice and posting at offices and open houses ([19 NYCRR 175.29](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/19-NYCRR-175.29)) [PRIMARY]; and seller-solicitation that respects do-not-solicit requests and any DOS cease-and-desist zone (none is active in the Hudson Valley as of the DOS list updated 10/1/2026) ([DOS cease-and-desist zones](https://dos.ny.gov/cease-and-desist-zones)) [PRIMARY].

The copy and conversation pipeline therefore needs seven checks [INFERENCE]: a protected-class and proxy-term screen on every generated listing, ad, e-mail and chat reply (all fifteen New York classes and the OneKey word list, not only the seven federal classes); a "describe the property, not the buyer" rewrite rule; a hard refusal list for schools, demographics, crime and "good area" questions, with the same neutral links to public data sources given to everyone (HUD's 2026 letter does not relax this in New York, whose cases punished exactly that talk); source-of-income neutrality, with identical response-time targets logged per lead; an ad-targeting policy that uses housing special-ad categories, never targets by age, gender, family status or ZIP, and logs every audience; a documented justification and disparate-effect test for every automated rule that sorts people (§296(5-a)); and a licensee approval step on every outbound advertisement, with the check results stored. A pending bill would turn the logging into an annual independent audit for AI "virtual agents" (A9028, L42).

## 5. Disclosures are fixed checkpoints the software can run on time

**Property Condition Disclosure Statement.** Every seller of a one-to-four-family home "shall complete and sign" the statement and deliver it "prior to the signing by the buyer of a binding contract of sale", and is liable only for a "willful failure", in the buyer's actual damages ([RPL §462](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/462); [§465](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/465)) [PRIMARY]. The seller answers from "actual knowledge" and need not search records; the seller's agent must "timely inform each seller ... of the seller's obligations" and the buyer's agent must inform the buyer before the buyer signs ([RPL §466](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/466)) [PRIMARY]; the Long Island board's guidance is that licensees must not fill it in for the seller ([LIBOR PCDS FAQ](https://www.lirealtor.com/docs/default-source/default-document-library/PCDS-FAQ.pdf)) [SECONDARY]. The form changed twice: the March 2024 amendment (Ch. 484 of 2023) removed the credit and added flood questions ([NYSBA](https://nysba.org/pcda-amended-500-seller-credit-deleted-and-additional-questions-added-to-pcds/)) [SECONDARY], and Chapter 353 of 2024, effective July 1, 2025, added a septic operation-and-maintenance paragraph to question 36, still 56 questions ([A9330-A](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2023/A9330/amendment/A); [RPL §462, rev. 2025-07-04](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/462)) [PRIMARY]. DOS says the "eff. 7/1/25" form "IS REQUIRED BEGINNING JULY 1, 2025" ([DOS](https://dos.ny.gov/become-real-estate-broker)) [PRIMARY], and NYSAR's guidance is that a seller who gave the older form at listing must re-issue the new one if the buyer had not signed before that date ([HGAR/NYSAR legal update](https://hgar.com/nysar-legal-update-amended-pcds-must-be-used-beginning-july-1-2025)) [SECONDARY]. The product must therefore version-pin the PCDS and never pre-fill a seller's answers.

**Agency disclosure.** The New York State Disclosure Form for Buyer and Seller (DOS-1736-f, Rev. 09/21, available in 13 languages; DOS says nothing about electronic delivery, which ESRA supports, [DOS](https://dos.ny.gov/buyer-and-seller-disclosure-form-english)) [PRIMARY] must go to a seller before the listing agreement, to a buyer (or the buyer's agent) at the first substantive contact, and to a buyer before any buyer-agency agreement, each with a signed acknowledgment kept three years, or a sworn declaration of refusal; advance consent to dual agency is given on the form ([RPL §443](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/443)) [PRIMARY]. A broker must "make it clear for which party he is acting" and may not be paid by both sides without the client's knowledge and consent ([19 NYCRR 175.7](https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/03/real-estate-license-law_03.2026.pdf)) [PRIMARY]. **Housing-discrimination disclosure:** DOS-2156 at first substantive contact (section 4). **Exclusive listings** must carry DOS's six-point "EXPLANATION" of exclusive right to sell versus exclusive agency, initialled by the owner, must not auto-renew, must not be "net" listings, and (for MLS listings) must give the owner the choice of routing offers through the listing or selling broker ([19 NYCRR 175.24, 175.15, 175.19](https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/03/real-estate-license-law_03.2026.pdf)) [PRIMARY].

**Other property disclosures** each have a fixed trigger. Pre-1978 homes need the EPA pamphlet, known lead information, a Lead Warning Statement and a 10-day inspection opportunity before the buyer is bound, and an agent "must ensure compliance"; knowing violators face treble damages and HUD's penalty is $22,263 per violation ([42 U.S.C. §4852d](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/4852d); [24 CFR 30.65](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/30.65)) [PRIMARY]. The seller delivers a smoke-detector compliance affidavit at conveyance ([Executive Law §378(5-b)(e)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/378)) [PRIMARY]; must give two years of heating and cooling bills within 15 days of a written request ([Energy Law §17-103](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ENG/17-103)) [PRIMARY]; and must present the statutory agricultural-district notice when the contract is presented for land in an agricultural district ([Agriculture and Markets Law §310](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/AGM/310)) [PRIMARY]. A separate statute the earlier notes missed requires four more seller notices: written notice before accepting an offer if no utility provides electric service, if the property "is subject to an electric, gas and/or water utility surcharge", or if it "is subject to a green jobs-green New York on-bill recovery charge" (each with amounts and terms, and the buyer's actual damages as the remedy), and disclosure before contract of any known uncapped natural gas well ([RPL §242](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/242)) [PRIMARY]. Deaths, felonies and non-transmissible illnesses connected with a house are "not material"; the seller may choose whether to answer a buyer's written inquiry ([RPL §443-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/443-A)) [PRIMARY]. Westchester requires the seller to order a certified private-well test on signing the contract and deliver the results ([Westchester Local Law 7 of 2007](https://www.westchesterlegislatorsny.gov/images/archive/LocalLaws/2007LocalLaws/7PrivateWellTestingLegislation.pdf)) [PRIMARY]; Rockland's Chapter 389 requires a test by a county-registered certified lab after contract, confirmation to the buyer within 10 days, a joint certification to the county health department within a month after closing, and a county flyer says the agent must tell the client about the law before a binding contract ([ecode360, via search text](https://www.ecode360.com/9669886)) [SECONDARY]. No Hudson Valley county requires a septic inspection at transfer (such rules exist around Adirondack lakes) ([Queensbury Ch. 137 example](https://www.queensburyny.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/ADOPTED-Chapter-137-NEW-CHAPTER-Septic-Inspection-Upon-Property-Transfer-October-15-2018.pdf)) [PRIMARY example; absence is an observation].

## 6. E-signatures work for everything except the deed's acknowledgment and powers of attorney

New York's Electronic Signatures and Records Act gives an electronic signature "the same validity and effect as the use of a signature affixed by hand" ([State Technology Law §304](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/STT/304)) [PRIMARY], and its exceptions cover wills, trusts, powers of attorney and similar fiduciary documents and possession-based title instruments, not deeds, mortgages or contracts of sale; the section was revised in December 2025 and February 2026 without touching real property ([STL §307](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/STT/307)) [PRIMARY]. Federal E-SIGN independently bars denying a contract effect "solely because it is in electronic form", but where a law requires written disclosures to consumers, electronic delivery counts only after the consumer's affirmative consent following notice of the right to paper ([15 U.S.C. §7001(a),(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001)) [PRIMARY]. So listing agreements, agency and fair-housing forms, offers, contracts and riders can all be signed electronically, with an E-SIGN consent screen before statutory disclosures go out electronically (L34).

Deeds and mortgages must be acknowledged to be recordable ([RPL §291](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/291)) [PRIMARY]. Since January 31, 2023 that can be done remotely, but only by a registered New York electronic notary "located within the state of New York" (the signer may be anywhere), with an audio-video recording kept "at least ten years"; county clerks "shall accept for recording" a notary-certified paper copy; and no business may "exclusively require" electronic notarisation ([Executive Law §135-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/135-C); [DOS notary FAQ](https://dos.ny.gov/notary-public-frequently-asked-questions)) [PRIMARY]. E-SIGN treats an electronic notarisation as valid where the notary's electronic signature and required information are attached ([15 U.S.C. §7001(g)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001)) [PRIMARY], and Fannie Mae accepts remotely notarised mortgages where state law expressly permits remote notarisation ([Fannie Mae Selling Guide, via summary](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/a2-4.1-04/notarization-standards)) [SECONDARY]. A power of attorney is outside ESRA and must be signed, acknowledged like a deed and witnessed by two people ([GOL §5-1501B](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/5-1501B)) [PRIMARY], so it remains a paper, in-person event. CSC lists eight Hudson Valley county clerks (Columbia, Dutchess, Greene, Orange, Putnam, Rockland, Sullivan, Ulster) in its e-recording network ([CSC](https://www.cscglobal.com/service/erecording/erecording-availability/new-york)) [SECONDARY]; Westchester was not on that list. Closing documents stay with the attorneys and the title company; the product tracks them (L35).

## 7. What the father-in-law may and may not do

**Licence or exemption.** As an admitted attorney he may personally do broker work without a licence, because Article 12-A "shall not apply to ... attorneys at law" ([RPL §442-f](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442-F)) [PRIMARY]. The exemption is personal and does not cover the brokerage LLC, cannot sponsor salespersons (a salesperson is by definition "associated with a licensed real estate broker" and may be paid only by one) and does not meet MLS rules ([RPL §440(3), §442-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/440)) [PRIMARY]; ([WS3 notes](../../research_notes/WS3%20Listing%20and%20data%20access/mls_access.md)) [PRIMARY via WS3]. Taking the licence costs $185 and no exam (section 1). Once licensed he carries every broker duty: supervision, advertising rules, SOP posting, fair-housing liability, and DOS discipline up to revocation, with fines currently capped at $2,000 ([RPL §441-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/441-C)) [PRIMARY] (a passed but unsigned 2026 bill would raise the cap to $5,000, L20).

**Supervising salespersons.** If the firm ever hires licensees, he must give them "regular, frequent and consistent personal guidance, instruction, oversight and superintendence" ([19 NYCRR 175.21](https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/03/real-estate-license-law_03.2026.pdf)) [PRIMARY]; DOS reads that as policies, training and monitoring, not watching "every minute of the day" ([DLS v. Metalios, 1105 DOS 09](https://docs.dos.ny.gov/ooah/decisions/Real_estate/2009/Metalios_Menalaos.htm)) [PRIMARY]. With no salespersons, the duty that matters is controlling the software and anyone helping him (section 1).

**Broker and lawyer in the same deal.** NYSBA's ethics committee has held for decades that "a lawyer may not act as a lawyer and a broker in the same real estate transaction, with or without client consent, and whether or not the lawyer charges for legal services", and that the rule applies whenever the lawyer "has a financial interest in the real estate broker's commission, whether or not the lawyer is acting as a broker" ([Op. 1043, 2015](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1043/)) [PRIMARY]; the conflict "cannot be cured by the client's consent because it is non-consentable" ([Op. 1117, 2017](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1117/)) [PRIMARY]; and a lawyer who co-owns a brokerage may not take closing referrals from its agents even if he waives his firm's share ([Op. 1237, 2022](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1237/)) [PRIMARY]. The one exception is live, not doubtful as the earlier notes suggested: where "brokerage services are compensated by a fixed and non-refundable fee, not contingent on closing", there is no per se conflict and other conflicts can be waived by informed consent ([Op. 1117, footnote 1, citing Op. 1015](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1117/)) [PRIMARY]; Op. 1015 adds that consent must be "confirmed in writing", the combined fee "may not be excessive", and separate offices help ([Op. 1015, 2014](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1015/)) [PRIMARY]. He also may not act as a client's lawyer and the title insurer's agent in the same deal unless his title role is "purely ministerial" ([Op. 1283, 2025-08-20](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1283-attorneys-referrals-to-title-abstract-company-owned-by-referring-attorney/)) [PRIMARY].

**Fee structures.** As broker he may charge any negotiated fee ("The commission or compensation of a real estate broker is not regulated", [DOS FAQ](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-frequently-asked-questions)) [PRIMARY], including a flat fee, and may rebate part of it to the buyer or seller ([RPL §442](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442)) [PRIMARY]. If he wants to be the seller's lawyer too, the brokerage fee must be fixed, non-refundable and payable whether or not the sale closes; the legal fee must be billed by his law practice, not by an LLC Joe co-owns, because a lawyer "shall not share legal fees with a nonlawyer" and may not practise through an entity a nonlawyer owns ([Rule 5.4](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/NYSBA-NY-Rules-of-Professional-Conduct-as-amended-through-July-1-2026-1.pdf); [Op. 1082, 2016](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1082/)) [PRIMARY]. He can never be the buyer's lawyer where his firm represents the seller [INFERENCE from Rule 1.7].

**Broker of record for a firm Joe co-owns.** Yes, on the texts found. Op. 1237's inquirer co-owned a brokerage with non-lawyers and the committee raised no objection to the ownership itself ([Op. 1237](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1237/)) [PRIMARY]; Article 12-A requires disclosure of every member but tests trustworthiness only for those "for whom a license as a broker is asked" ([RPL §441(1)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/441)) [PRIMARY], and DOS's application asks nothing about non-acting owners ([DOS-0036-f-a](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-application)) [PRIMARY]. Three conditions attach. Every brokerage customer must be told in writing "that the services are not legal services" and that client-lawyer protections do not apply ([Rule 5.7(a)(4)](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/NYSBA-NY-Rules-of-Professional-Conduct-as-amended-through-July-1-2026-1.pdf); [Op. 1252, 2023](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1252/)) [PRIMARY]. Joe may not "direct or regulate" his professional judgement (Rule 5.7(b), 5.4(c)) [PRIMARY]. And marketing the firm as "run by a lawyer" invites the presumption that legal services are on offer, which Rule 7.1's ban on misleading omissions and Rule 5.7 make risky [INFERENCE].

## 8. Joe can own and build, but cannot be paid per deal

A broker may pay "any part of a fee, commission or other compensation" for "any service, help or aid" in a sale only to licensed salespersons and brokers, out-of-state brokers, or an entity wholly owned by the broker's own licensees ([RPL §442(1)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442)) [PRIMARY]. DOS applies that to staff: unlicensed help may be paid "on an hourly, per activity, or salaried basis", but "If compensated on a completed transaction basis, the assistant must be licensed" ([DOS](https://dos.ny.gov/unlicensed-real-estate-assistants)) [PRIMARY]. The federal settlement-services law adds that no one may pay or accept a referral fee or an unearned split in a deal with a federally related mortgage, and repeated payments "connected in any way with the volume or value of the business referred" are evidence of a referral agreement ([12 CFR 1024.14](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1024/subpart-B/section-1024.14)) [PRIMARY]. Market practice confirms the reading: Zillow, which charges agents a share of commission payable at closing, took out brokerage licences, New York's included, so its fee is a broker-to-broker payment ([The Real Deal, 2020, via search](https://therealdeal.com/2020/02/04/heres-why-zillow-is-getting-brokerage-licenses/)) [SECONDARY].

| Structure for Joe | Lawful on the texts? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Owns equity in the brokerage LLC; receives pro-rata profit distributions | Yes, with care [INFERENCE] | No statute or DOS statement forbids it; keep distributions tied to ownership, not to his work on deals; operating agreement gives the broker sole control of licensed activity. DOS has not confirmed (open question). |
| Owns the software company; licenses software to the brokerage for a fixed monthly or per-seat fee | Yes [INFERENCE] | Payment for "goods or facilities actually furnished", not a share of commission ([12 CFR 1024.14(g)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1024/subpart-B/section-1024.14)) [PRIMARY]. |
| Salaried or hourly employee of the brokerage for non-licensed work | Yes | DOS assistants rule. |
| Software fee per closing, or a percentage of commission | **No** | §442(1); DOS "completed transaction" rule; RESPA §8 evidence; Handy v. Cohen damages up to 4x. |
| Joe solicits listings, negotiates, presents offers, shows homes | **No** | §440-a; also DOS "availing" theory (Flagship). |
| Joe takes a salesperson licence while an LLC member | **No** | §441-b(2) bars a salesperson licence for an LLC member; he would need a full broker licence. |

## 9. AI, outreach and personal-data rules

**AI-specific law.** New York's AI-companion law, in force since November 5, 2025 ([Governor's release](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-pens-letter-ai-companion-companies-notifying-them-safeguard-requirements-are)) [PRIMARY], requires a "not communicating with a human" notice and a self-harm protocol, with penalties "up to fifteen thousand dollars per day", but it excludes "any system used by a business entity solely for customer service" or to provide information about the business's services ([GBL §1700](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1700); [§1703](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1703)) [PRIMARY]; a brokerage assistant that answers listing questions and schedules stays outside it if it does not cultivate a personal relationship [INFERENCE]. Advertisements using an AI-generated "synthetic performer" must say so conspicuously from June 9, 2026 ([GBL §396-b](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/396-B)) [PRIMARY], and any price "set by an algorithm that uses personal data" must carry the statutory notice ([GBL §349-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/349-A)) [PRIMARY], which a single published fee avoids. More is queued. Bills that passed both houses but had not been delivered to the Governor by 2026-10-23 would require a notice on any generative-AI interface that its outputs "may be inaccurate" (A3411-B, $1,000 per affected user, 90 days after signing), ban personalised "surveillance pricing" outright (A9349-B), require provenance data on AI audio and video (S6954-B) and regulate health data (NYHIPA, S9269) ([A3411-B](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A3411/amendment/B); [A9349-B](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A9349); [S6954](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S6954); [S9269](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S9269)) [PRIMARY]. The New York AI Act, which would treat housing as a "consequential decision" requiring notice, a human alternative and audits, passed the Senate only ([S1169](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S1169/amendment/A)) [PRIMARY]. A December 2025 presidential order set up a federal task force to challenge state AI laws, but law-firm summaries report no suit and no preemption statute; state AI laws "remain in effect unless and until Congress acts or courts intervene" ([Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/federal-government/102lxoh/federal-ai-preemption-takes-center-stage-key-deadlines-strategic-risks-and-op)) [SECONDARY]. The FTC's position is that "there is no AI exemption from the laws on the books", and it ordered DoNotPay to stop claiming its AI could replace professionals without evidence ([FTC, 2024-09-25](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/09/ftc-announces-crackdown-deceptive-ai-claims-schemes)) [PRIMARY]; fake or AI-written reviews are banned ([16 CFR 465.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.2)) [PRIMARY]. "AI that replaces your agent and your lawyer" is the kind of claim those cases target [INFERENCE].

**Outreach.** Autodialed or "artificial or prerecorded voice" calls to mobiles need prior express consent, marketing texts and calls need prior express written consent, revocation "by any reasonable method" must be honoured within ten business days, and private suits pay $500 to $1,500 per message ([47 U.S.C. §227](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/227); [47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/64.1200)) [PRIMARY]. The FCC ruled in February 2024 that AI-generated voices are "artificial" voices under that law, so an AI voice agent calling consumers needs the same consent as a robocall ([Mayer Brown on FCC 24-17](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/02/fcc-declares-authority-and-intent-to-regulate-ai-generated-calls-under-the-tcpa)) [SECONDARY]; the rule that one "stop" revokes consent for all unrelated messages is postponed to January 31, 2027, but the other revocation duties apply now ([Orrick on DA 26-12](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-01-16/fcc-extends-robocall-consent-revocation-rule-waiver-to-january-2027/)) [SECONDARY]. New York's telemarketing law covers texts as well as calls, limits them to 8 a.m. to 9 p.m., requires the caller's name and the do-not-call option first and the recording status, purpose and goods within 30 seconds, requires an express written agreement before any call that "delivers a pre-recorded message", and carries fines up to $20,000 per violation ([GBL §399-z](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/399-Z); [Ch. 539 of 2024](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2023/S8182/amendment/B)) [PRIMARY]. Commercial e-mail must identify itself, give an address and an opt-out honoured within ten business days, with penalties up to $53,088 per message, unchanged for 2026 ([FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)) [PRIMARY].

**Recording and transcribing calls.** New York is a one-party-consent state: its crime of wiretapping is recording "by a person other than a sender or receiver thereof, without the consent of either" ([Penal Law §250.00](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/250.00)) [PRIMARY], and federal law likewise allows a party to record ([18 U.S.C. §2511(2)(d)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2511)) [PRIMARY]. But calls with people in Pennsylvania, Massachusetts, California and other all-party states need everyone's consent, and Connecticut imposes civil liability for recording a phone call without consent or a recorded warning ([Reporters Committee guide](https://www.rcfp.org/reporters-recording-sections/telephone-and-electronic-communications/)) [SECONDARY]. A federal court let a class action proceed against an AI call-transcription vendor that could reuse call content for its own purposes ([Ambriz v. Google, N.D. Cal. 2025, via ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/privacy/federal-judge-allows-google-customer-service-ai-class-action-to-proceed/)) [SECONDARY]. The design that works everywhere is an announcement at the start of every call that it is recorded and transcribed by an automated assistant for the brokerage, plus vendor contracts that bar any reuse or training on call content [INFERENCE].

**Personal data.** The brokerage will hold "private information" (driver's-licence numbers, account numbers, and possibly medical details from accessibility requests) and must run a written security programme with administrative, technical and physical safeguards ([GBL §899-bb](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/899-BB)) [PRIMARY], notify affected New Yorkers within 30 days of discovering a breach and also notify the Attorney General, DOS and the State Police ([GBL §899-aa](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/899-AA)) [PRIMARY], never require a Social Security number to be sent over an unsecured connection ([GBL §399-ddd](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/399-DDD)) [PRIMARY], and destroy records properly ([GBL §399-h](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/399-H)) [PRIMARY]. The SHIELD provisions have not changed since March 2025 and New York still has no comprehensive privacy statute (the New York Privacy Act is in committee) ([S3044](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S3044)) [PRIMARY]. The brokerage must also keep transaction records, agency acknowledgments and fair-housing disclosures for three years ([19 NYCRR 175.23](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/19-NYCRR-175.23)) [PRIMARY], and because fair-housing claims can be brought for two years under federal law (tolled while an administrative complaint is pending) and three years at the state Division of Human Rights ([42 U.S.C. §3613](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/3613); [Executive Law §297(5)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/297)) [PRIMARY], conversation logs, SOP-application logs, ad audiences and disparate-effect tests should be kept at least three years and protected like the personal data they contain [INFERENCE]. That retention floor limits what a deletion request can remove. Real-estate wire and e-mail fraud cost victims $173.6 million in 2024 ([FBI IC3 2024 report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2024_IC3Report.pdf)) [PRIMARY] and $275.1 million in 2025 ([NAR on the IC3 2025 report](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/online-real-estate-fraud-climbed-to-275m-in-2025-fbi-says)) [SECONDARY]; no New York statute mandates a warning, but never sending or changing wiring instructions through the chat or e-mail channel is the standard of care [INFERENCE].

## 12. What may change in the next twelve months

Two bills passed both houses on June 1, 2026 and had not reached the Governor by 2026-10-23: A10679-B, which would make public marketing, cooperation with buyers' agents and showing availability statutory duties of every listing agent and raise DOS's maximum fine to $5,000 ([A10679-B](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A10679/amendment/B)) [PRIMARY], and S8870/A9518, which would require four consecutive years as a salesperson before a broker licence, a change to the experience route that does not mention attorneys ([S8870](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8870)) [PRIMARY]. ## Conclusion

The research turns the venture's legal question from "can software replace the agent and the lawyer?" (it cannot, in New York) into "how much of a licensed broker's and a lawyer's work can software make cheap without crossing the lines?", and the answer to the second is: most of the clerical, drafting, disclosure, scheduling and tracking work, provided a named licensee makes every licensed decision on screen and the contract stays with the lawyers. That reframing is not a weaker pitch for this market. In the Hudson Valley the lawyer already owns the contract by custom, so an attorney-led brokerage that delivers a complete, compliant, fair-housing-checked deal package to the lawyers in days is selling into the existing process rather than fighting it, and the Op. 1015/1117 fixed-fee path even lets the father-in-law offer sellers his legal work and the brokerage together at one upfront price, which no commission brokerage can.

The structural risks are now specific and cheap to close: one DOS question about Joe's passive ownership, a decision on whether the father-in-law will be broker-only or also seller's counsel on fixed-fee deals, and a fair-housing design that treats every chat reply and ad audience as evidence. The remaining legal unknowns (unaccompanied showings, the fate of two bills on the Governor's desk) affect how the product is staffed, not whether it is lawful.
