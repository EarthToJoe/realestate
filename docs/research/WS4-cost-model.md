# Opening costs little; customers and hours decide

**Bottom line (2026-10-23).** An attorney-led, one-broker brokerage on OneKey MLS is cheap to open. In the lean case, year one costs about **$8,200 in cash**. The lean case means a licensed home office, the father-in-law as unpaid broker of record, Joe doing the coordination, no paid advertising and OneKey counties only. Most of that $8,200 is the MLS ($3,636) and insurance ($1,800). A realistic set-up costs about **$62,600** a year. It adds a part-time coordinator, a small office, outside legal review, $1,000 a month of brand marketing and an MLS-fed website. A full build-out costs about **$140,800**. Each closed listing costs the firm about **$350** (lean), **$1,900** (realistic) or **$7,470** (full build-out). The full build-out includes free pre-listing inspections, premium media and leads bought from search ads. At a **$4,500 flat seller fee**, break-even is **2 closings** a year (lean) and **about 25** (realistic). The full build-out **never breaks even**: every closing loses money until the fee reaches about 2% of price, which is Redfin's price, not a disruptor's.

Three things decide the venture, and the AI bill is not one of them. AI costs $2–$15 per side.

1. **The cost of a customer.** Search-ad leads for agents and brokers cost **$157.59–$162.39 each**. At 2–5% conversion that is **$3,150–$8,120 per signed client**, more than a flat fee earns. **K2 is hit the moment the firm must buy listings.**
2. **The broker's hours.** The plan assumes 10–15 broker hours a side. The only measured analogue, Redfin, averaged about **28 sides per lead agent a year** with support staff behind them, which implies far more hours.
3. **What the seller actually saves.** If the buyer still has an agent (88% of buyers do), the seller saves about **a third** of today's ~4.6% New York commission, not most of it. **K1 is partly hit for the pitch**, though not for the firm's own margin.

Two further findings bear on the plan:

- **The free inspection does not fit a flat fee.** It costs $900–$1,800 per listing and is the line that turns the full build-out negative. **A9 is partly hit.**
- **The lawyer's malpractice policy will not cover the brokerage.** New York courts let a lawyer's malpractice insurer refuse claims tied to a business he owns. The brokerage needs its own errors-and-omissions policy, and the father-in-law should ask his carrier whether brokerage claims could reach his law firm's cover.

## The verdict in three numbers: fee, cost of a customer, broker hours

This section gives the numbers a reader needs for go/no-go. The detail behind every line is in the tables further down. A **side** is one party's half of a sale: the seller side or the buyer side. **Contribution** is the fee minus the costs that come with each closing. **Break-even** is the number of closings a year whose contribution covers the fixed costs.

All prices below are as read on 2026-10-23. The worked example uses a **$510,000** house, Orange County's Q3-2026 single-family median. That is close to Ulster ($509,500) and between Sullivan ($371,000) and Rockland ($800,000) ([OneKey Q3-2026 county reports](https://corporate.onekeymls.com/market-statistics)) [PRIMARY].

The three cost cases are defined as follows; all the hour counts are [INFERENCE]:

- **Lean.** Licensed home office. The father-in-law is the unpaid owner-broker at about 8 hours a side. Joe does coordination and software, and his time is not costed. OneKey counties only, the free MLS search widget, mechanical lockboxes, median insurance, no paid ads, $315 of media, no free inspection, and every listing sells.
- **Realistic.** A 250 sq ft office. A part-time coordinator (1,000 hours a year). SentriLock lockboxes and an MLS-fed website. Cautious insurance, $6,000 of outside legal review, a CPA, $1,000 a month of brand marketing, and $1,000 of lead spend per closing on average. Broker time about 15 hours a side. One listing in five does not sell.
- **Full build-out.** A 600 sq ft office, a full-time coordinator, a second MLS, a bought software stack, $15,000 of outside counsel, a CPA and bookkeeper, and $3,000 a month of marketing. Every listing gets premium media, Zillow Showcase and a free inspection, and leads are bought at $3,200 per client. Broker time about 25 hours a side. One listing in four does not sell.

The arithmetic is fixed cost ÷ (fee − variable cost) [INFERENCE]:

- **Lean at $4,500:** $8,236 ÷ ($4,500 − $351) = 1.99, so 2 closings.
- **Realistic at $4,500:** $62,575 ÷ ($4,500 − $1,902) = 24.1, so 25 closings.
- **Full build-out at any flat fee up to $6,500:** the fee is below the $7,471 variable cost, so no volume breaks even. At 50 closings a year this case needs $7,471 + $140,836 ÷ 50 ≈ **$10,290 per side**, about 2.0% of $510,000.
- **Broker time valued:** the "valued" row prices the broker's hours at **$66.65/hour**. That is the May 2025 median hourly wage for lawyers in the Kiryas Joel–Poughkeepsie–Newburgh metro area ([BLS OEWS via API](https://api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v2/timeseries/data/OEUM002888000000023101108)) [PRIMARY]. It is a floor on his opportunity cost, since billing rates run $150–$400 an hour ([Daeryun Law](https://www.daeryunlaw.com/us/insights/real-estate-consultation-fee-in-new-york)) [CLAIMED]. Realistic case: $62,575 ÷ ($4,500 − $1,902 − 15 × $66.65) = 39.1, so 40 closings.

Twenty-five closings a year is a small share of the market. The eight target counties closed about **12,600** residential sales in 2025, about 25,000 sides ([Pattern for Progress, Q4 & Annual 2025](https://www.pattern-for-progress.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2025-Q4-Annual-Housing-Report-.pdf)) [PRIMARY]. But it is nearly three times what the typical Realtor closes: **nine sides in 2025** ([NAR 2026 Member Profile release, 25 Jun 2026](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/experienced-realtors-anchor-the-industry-as-housing-affordability-remains-top-hurdle-new-nar-report)) [PRIMARY]. The fixed costs are not what makes this hard. Finding 25 sellers a year without buying them is.

The cost of a customer is the swing factor. LocaliQ's 2026 search-advertising benchmarks give a median cost per lead of **$162.39** for "Real Estate Broker" campaigns and **$157.59** for "Residential Real Estate Agent" campaigns. Those figures come from 894 US real-estate campaigns run from April 2025 to March 2026, and the Residential Real Estate Agent campaigns converted clicks to leads at only 1.29% ([LocaliQ, updated 14 Sep 2026](https://localiq.com/blog/real-estate-search-advertising-benchmarks/)) [PRIMARY dataset owner]. Industry blogs put online lead-to-client conversion at about 2–5% ([Jamil Academy](https://www.jamilacademy.com/blog/real-estate-lead-conversion-rate-benchmarks)) [CLAIMED]. That implies **$3,150–$8,120 per paid-acquired client** [INFERENCE]. Zillow's average of **$139 per buyer connection** outside major metros implies **$2,780–$6,950** at the same conversion ([Zillow Premier Agent](https://zillow.com/premier-agent)) [PRIMARY, via search summary]. Even Redfin, with a national brand, spent **$114.5 million** on marketing in 2024 across **61,430** transactions, about **$1,864 per transaction** ([SEC XBRL](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK0001382821/us-gaap/MarketingExpense.json); [Redfin Q4 2024 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1382821/000138282125000042/q42024earningsreleaseex.htm)) [PRIMARY; division is INFERENCE]. Against a $4,500 fee whose contribution before acquisition is about $2,600–$4,150, paid acquisition at these prices erases the margin.

## Kill criteria: where the numbers land

The job asks which of A7, K1, K2 and K7 the numbers hit or dodge. The answer is mixed, and the mix depends on how the firm gets its customers.

**A7 (unit economics work): still open, partly hit.**

- The kill criterion is met if acquisition cost exceeds the fee, or if supervised broker hours per deal exceed what the fee pays for.
- **Acquisition cost:** referral-sourced listings clear the bar easily, with contribution of $2,600–$4,150 at $4,500. Paid search does not clear it ($3,150–$8,120 per client).
- **Broker hours:** at the valued rate, the broker's time costs $533 (8 hours) to $1,666 (25 hours) per side. That is affordable inside a $4,500 fee. But if hours reach Redfin-like levels (about 70 lead-agent hours per side, explained in the human-interaction section), they cost about $4,700 and consume the whole fee.
- **Verdict:** A7 survives only on referrals and only if the software really cuts broker hours to roughly 15 or fewer per side. Both must be measured in the pilot.

**K1 (economics depend on the buyer-agent fee disappearing): dodged for the firm, partly hit for the pitch.**

- The firm's own margin does not depend on unrepresented buyers. It charges its client a fee, and any buyer's agent is paid by whoever agreed to pay them.
- The headline "dramatically cheaper" does depend on the buyer side. With a buyer's agent at New York's average 2.21%, a seller saves **33%** of the average commission. The 66–81% savings appear only when the buyer side also comes through the firm or the buyer is unrepresented (arithmetic in the savings section).
- About 88% of buyers use an agent (WS5 notes, [NAR 2025 Profile](https://www.rirealtors.org/clientuploads/documents/NAR/Homebuyers_and_sellers_trend_2025.pdf)) [PRIMARY].

**K2 (listings must be bought with advertising): hit if it happens.**

- The arithmetic above shows a flat-fee brokerage cannot afford search-ad customers at 2025–26 prices.
- The plan must be built on the father-in-law's referral network.
- Every intake must record where the customer came from, so that drift toward paid leads shows early.

**K7 (outside capital needed to reach break-even): dodged in the lean case, open in the realistic case, hit in the full build-out.**

- **The wait for the first fee.** Launch takes about 8–12 weeks (notes §9). Homes in most OneKey counties take 35–55 days from listing to accepted offer, and 87 in Sullivan (WS1 notes, [OneKey Q3-2026 Dutchess](https://corporate.onekeymls.com/s/DutchessCounty-mrmd.pdf)) [PRIMARY]. Closing takes about 45–60 days after acceptance ([Rand Realty explainer](https://jeselle.randrealty.com/?p=79)) [SECONDARY]. So the first fee arrives 5–8 months after starting [INFERENCE].
- **The cash gap** [INFERENCE: about half a year of fixed costs, plus the one-time items, which fall at the start]:
  - Lean: under $10,000.
  - Realistic: about $31,000–$40,000 of founder cash before the first fee.
  - Full build-out: an open-ended loss at flat fees.
- **The software build is the larger capital item.** If Joe's development time were bought locally, one software developer's median salary is **$145,830 a year** ([BLS OEWS](https://api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v2/timeseries/data/OEUM002888000000015125213)) [PRIMARY]. That is more than twice the realistic brokerage's entire year-one cost. Recovering one developer-year at the realistic case's $2,598 contribution per $4,500 side takes about 56 extra closings [INFERENCE]. Joe's investment is founder money, not outside capital. But WS5's base rate is that capital-dependent entrants died when funding or volume turned. The brokerage should therefore stand on its own closings, and the software should be treated as Joe's risk capital, not as a cost the first years' fees can repay.
- **The market is turning.** The 30-year mortgage averaged **7.03%** in late September 2026. The Federal Reserve raised rates on 16 September 2026, its first rise since July 2023. National contract signings are about 30% below pre-pandemic levels ([OneKey Q3-2026 Dutchess report](https://corporate.onekeymls.com/s/DutchessCounty-mrmd.pdf)) [PRIMARY]. A fixed-cost plan should assume flat-to-falling volume.

**Related findings:**

- **A9 (free inspection):** the inspection alone is $900–$1,800 per listing, paid whether or not the house sells (WS6 notes). It is the largest single reason the full build-out cannot break even. Offer it as a paid or pass-through option, or pilot it on a handful of listings.
- **K3 (fee structure if the father-in-law is also counsel):** this constrains revenue design. New York ethics opinions bar him from being either party's lawyer on any deal where the brokerage earns a fee contingent on closing. The one narrow exception requires a **fixed, prepaid, non-refundable** broker fee with written consent ([NYSBA Op. 1015](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1015/); [Op. 1043](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1043/)) [PRIMARY, via WS2]. *(Corrected 2026-10-23 by fact-check: the exception comes from Op. 1015 ¶16 and Op. 1117 fn. 1, not Op. 1043, which states the bar. The conditions are a fee that is fixed, non-refundable and not contingent on closing, plus informed consent confirmed in writing; "prepaid" is not an opinion requirement, only a way to make non-refundability real.)* The cost model therefore assumes, per Q4, that he is never counsel on brokerage deals.

## Getting in the door: about $2,500 cash, or $20,000 with outside legal review

The upfront list is short because the attorney route removes the usual barriers. DOS's broker application says that "Attorneys who are admitted to the New York State bar are exempt from the educational, experience and examination requirements." He files a paper application and pays **$185 for a two-year licence**. He needs no 152-hour course and no exam ([DOS-0036-f-a, Rev. 09/23](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-application)) [PRIMARY], and attorneys are exempt from continuing education ([DOS Broker FAQ](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-frequently-asked-questions)) [PRIMARY]. One correction to the earlier notes: a one-broker LLC pays **one** $185 fee, not two. Under the licensing statute, only each *additional* member acting as broker pays again ([RPL §441-b](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/441-B)) [PRIMARY].

The MLS is the gate to the portals (WS3), and it is affordable. Joining OneKey MLS through the Hudson Gateway Association of Realtors (HGAR) costs $606 of Realtor dues plus a $100 processing fee ([HGAR dues](https://www.hgar.com/membership/dues-schedule)) [PRIMARY]. On top of that come a $650 start-up fee and $190 a month ([HGAR fee schedule](https://www.hgar.com/membership/fee-schedule)) [PRIMARY]. That is **$3,636 in a January start**.

Coverage is narrower than HGAR's own page suggests. OneKey's Rule 201 (Rules ver. 2026C) covers Dutchess, Orange, Putnam, Rockland and Sullivan, but not Ulster, Columbia or Greene. Those three are Hudson Valley Catskill Region MLS territory, whose fees are given only by phone at 845-338-5299 ([HVCRR](https://hvcrr.com/the-association/join-the-board/)) [PRIMARY]. HGAR's subscription page still lists Ulster ([HGAR](https://www.hgar.com/membership/subscribe-to-onekey-mls)) [PRIMARY]. The newer rule text wins, so this report corrects the notes' "six of eight counties" to **five of eight**.

## Running costs: the MLS is cheap; people, office and marketing are not

Insurance has one trap. New York does not require errors-and-omissions cover (E&O, the professional liability policy for mistakes in a deal) ([Insureon NY](https://www.insureon.com/real-estate-business-insurance/agents-brokers/new-york)) [SECONDARY]. Median E&O premiums for agents and brokers are **$708 a year** for $1M/$1M ([TechInsurance](https://www.techinsurance.com/real-estate-insurance/agent-broker/cost)) [SECONDARY]. The trap is the father-in-law's existing malpractice policy, which will almost certainly not respond.

- In *Admiral Ins. Co. v. Adges* (S.D.N.Y. 2012), a federal court applying New York law held that business-enterprise exclusions barred malpractice cover for a lawyer and his realty business sued over a real estate deal ([Wiley Rein summary](https://www.wiley.law/newsletter-4339)) [SECONDARY].
- In *Lee & Amtzis* (N.Y. App. Div. 2015), the court held that the exclusion also barred cover for the law firm and a second lawyer, because the first lawyer's conduct was "hybrid" legal and business work ([Wiley Rein summary](https://www.wiley.law/alert-3658)) [SECONDARY].

So the brokerage's own E&O is a practical necessity, and the father-in-law's carrier must be asked before launch. This closes a gap the WS2 notes left open.

## AI costs less per deal than the yard sign

The product's model bill is too small to matter to the go/no-go decision, even on pessimistic assumptions. Anthropic's list prices per million tokens on 2026-10-23 were ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)) [PRIMARY]:

- **Opus 5.5:** $4 input, $20 output; cache hits $0.20.
- **Sonnet 5.5:** $2 input, $10 output.
- **Haiku 5.5:** $0.10 input, $0.50 output, for prompts up to 100,000 tokens.
- **Fable 5.1:** $10 input, $50 output.
- **Modifiers:** the Batch API halves prices; US-only processing adds 10%; web search costs $10 per 1,000 searches.

The earlier notes assumed a generous budget of **800,000 input and 75,000 output tokens per side**. That covers listing copy, about 60 messages with thread context, offer and contract summaries, and reading about ten documents [INFERENCE]. The cost per side [INFERENCE]:

- **Sonnet 5.5:** 0.8 × $2 + 0.075 × $10 = **$2.35**.
- **Opus 5.5:** 0.8 × $4 + 0.075 × $20 = **$4.70**.
- **Haiku 5.5:** 0.8 × $0.10 + 0.075 × $0.50 = **$0.12**.
- **Fable 5.1:** 0.8 × $10 + 0.075 × $50 = **$11.75**.

A heavy agentic case on Opus 5.5 uses 5 million input tokens and 300,000 output tokens per side [INFERENCE]:

- 3.5M cache reads × $0.20 = $0.70.
- 0.5M cache writes × $5 = $2.50.
- 1.0M uncached input × $4 = $4.00.
- 0.3M output × $20 = $6.00.
- Subtotal $13.20; × 1.1 for US-only processing = $14.52; plus 50 web searches = $0.50. Total **about $15**.

The worst case runs Fable 5.1 with no caching: 5M × $10 + 0.3M × $50 = $65, or **$71.50** with the US multiplier. Even then, 100 sides a year costs under $7,200 in AI. The realistic case is about $470 (Opus, base budget) to $1,500 (heavy). The real AI risk is quality and fair-housing compliance (A8), not cost. The product should log actual tokens per side so these assumptions are replaced by measurements.

## Humans in every deal: 10–20 broker hours a side if the software works, far more if it does not

New York law fixes where a human must act. The cost question is how many hours that takes.

- **Licensed acts.** Listing, negotiating and offering to negotiate "for another and for a fee" are licensed acts, whoever performs them ([RPL §440](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/440)) [PRIMARY, via WS2].
- **What unlicensed staff may do.** Answer phones, schedule appointments, draft ads "for approval of broker" and type forms "for approval by broker". They must be paid by the hour or salary, never per closed deal ([DOS: Unlicensed Real Estate Assistants](https://dos.ny.gov/unlicensed-real-estate-assistants)) [PRIMARY, via WS2].
- **Listings and advertising.** "All listings ... are accepted by the representative broker" ([DOS Broker FAQ](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-frequently-asked-questions)) [PRIMARY], and only a broker may place property advertising ([19 NYCRR §175.25, DOS compilation](https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2024/07/re-law.pdf)) [PRIMARY, via WS2].
- **Buyer tours.** Since August 2024, OneKey members must sign a written buyer agreement before touring a home ([HGAR Barrister's Briefing](https://hgar.com/barristers-briefing-the-nar-settlement-agreement-understanding-its-impact-and-new-forms)) [SECONDARY, via WS2]. Lockbox access runs through MLS membership ([SentriLock](https://www.sentrilock.com/)) [PRIMARY vendor, via WS1].

**Three plausible fee models.** All figures in this list are [INFERENCE] built on the benchmarks above.

1. **Flat fee per side.** For example, **$4,500 for the seller side**, which is 0.88% at $510,000. Add **$3,500 for the buyer side**, with any seller-offered compensation above that rebated to the buyer. Rebates to buyers and sellers are expressly lawful in New York ([RPL §442](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442)) [PRIMARY, via WS2]; how lenders treat them was not researched. A flat fee is the simplest to explain and the only structure compatible with Op. 1015 if it is prepaid and non-refundable.
2. **Low percentage.** For example, **1% with a $4,000 minimum** ($5,100 at $510,000). It scales with price, so it is fairer to Rockland sellers ($8,000) and protects margin in Sullivan and Greene ($4,000). But it is contingent on closing, so it rules out the father-in-law also acting as counsel.
3. **À la carte.** For example, a **$1,500 base** for the OneKey listing, AI-prepared and broker-approved marketing, e-signature and showing management. Add-ons could include pro media at about $350 (near cost), **$2,000** for broker-led negotiation and contract-to-close, **$250** per broker-hosted open house, **$75** per broker-attended showing, and the pre-listing inspection passed through at cost ($900–$1,800). A typical full bundle comes to about $4,350.

À la carte maps cleanly onto the product's visible checkpoints. It also lets the inspection be a seller's choice rather than a giveaway. It must not include any charge for documents. In WS2's summary of the New York authorities, a broker may fill in a simple bar-approved form only as an incident of brokerage and with no separate charge ([NY Attorney General Op. 96-F11, citing Duncan & Hill](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/96-F11_pw.pdf)) [PRIMARY, via WS2].

- Tours: about $350 if 7 tours go to a licensed showing agent at $50.
- Coordination about $72, plus about $20 of e-signature, texts and AI.
- Realistic-case acquisition about $1,000.
- That leaves about **$2,060** of contribution, or about **$1,260** after valuing 12 broker hours.

So the buyer side helps volume more than margin.

## What the seller really saves: a third, unless the buyer side comes too

The pitch has to survive this arithmetic at $510,000 [INFERENCE]:

| Scenario | What the seller pays in commissions | Saving vs 4.62% ($23,562) |
|---|---|---|
| Today, New York average (2.41% + 2.21%) | $12,291 + $11,271 = $23,562 | — |
| Firm lists for $4,500; buyer has their own agent at 2.21% | $4,500 + $11,271 = $15,771 | **$7,791 (33%)** |
| Firm on both sides, $4,500 + $3,500, seller pays both | $8,000 | $15,562 (66%) |
| Firm lists for $4,500; buyer unrepresented | $4,500 | $19,062 (81%) |

Representing both sides of one sale is dual agency, which needs both parties' advance informed consent on the state disclosure form ([RPL §443](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/443)) [PRIMARY, via WS2].

Adding the seller's other costs shrinks the percentage, not the dollars. Those are the New York transfer tax of $2 per $500, or $2,040 here, paid by the seller ([NY Tax Dept](https://www.tax.ny.gov/bus/transfer/rptidx.htm)) [PRIMARY], and a closing attorney at $995 ([Levin Law](https://ylevinlaw.com/campaign/lower-hudson-valley-real-estate-lawyer.html)) [PRIMARY vendor] to $2,500 ([Rosado Law](https://rosadolawgroup.com/how-much-does-a-real-estate-attorney-cost/)) [PRIMARY vendor].

- **Today:** the seller's total is about $26,600–$28,100.
- **Firm listing plus a buyer's agent:** about $18,800–$20,300, roughly 28–29% less.

The buyer, meanwhile, still pays his own attorney. The software does not remove either lawyer. New York custom and ethics keep a lawyer on each side for the contract, and brokers may not discourage parties from consulting their attorneys ([DOS LI04](https://dos.ny.gov/node/23931)) [PRIMARY, via WS2]. The honest headline is **"save about a third, and much more when you buy and sell with us"**, not "replace both agents and the lawyer".

## Conclusion

The cost model turns the venture's risk upside down. Joe's instinct, and the brief's, is that software makes the brokerage cheap, and it does: the technology lines, AI included, total under $5,000 a year in the realistic case. But that was never where brokerage money went. The money goes to finding customers and to licensed hours. A flat-fee firm can afford the first only through referrals, and the second only if the product measurably cuts broker time below anything the industry has demonstrated. The lean case shows the father-in-law can test this for under $10,000 and a few hundred hours. The full build-out shows how quickly a "full-service, free-inspection, advertised" version reverts to Redfin's 2% price.

For the product, this means the POC should treat cost and time as visible outputs, not hidden ones:

- every checkpoint logs broker minutes;
- every deal shows its own cost to the firm (media, inspection, envelopes, texts, tokens);
- intake records whether the customer came by referral or by advertising;
- the savings calculator shows the seller the buyer-side truth.

Those four numbers, gathered on the first 10–20 deals, will settle A7, K1, K2 and K5 faster than any further research can.
