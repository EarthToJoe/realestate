# Your possible roles in Joe's New York brokerage: what the rules allow

*Research to plan with; not legal advice.* Prepared 2026-10-23 for Joe to forward. L-numbers refer to the
constraint table in [WS2 legal and licensing](WS2-legal-licensing.md#10-the-constraint-table). Labels:
[PRIMARY] = statute, rule, regulator or court text read; [SECONDARY] = reporting or commentary;
[INFERENCE] = our reading, not a stated rule.

**In short.** You can be broker of record of an LLC Joe co-owns. You need no course, no experience and no
exam, provided you are "an Attorney who is currently admitted to the New York State bar". You can also
approve every checkpoint the software reaches (L3, L4, L5). You **cannot** be either party's lawyer in a deal
where you or the firm earn a commission that depends on the sale closing. NYSBA treats that conflict as one no
client consent can cure (L26). Two things weigh on you in particular. First, Article 12-A requires a
**physical New York office with a sign**, because your no-exam route gives you no out-of-state licence to rely
on for the non-resident exemption. Second, acting as a New York lawyer again means returning to active
registration, meeting CLE, and (under Judiciary Law §470) keeping a New York law office. **The one decision:**
are you broker only (commission deals, independent lawyers), or broker and seller's lawyer? The second works
only where the listing fee is fixed, non-refundable and paid whether or not the house sells (L27).

## Being broker and lawyer in the same deal

**The rule.** NYSBA's ethics committee holds that "a lawyer may not act as a lawyer and a broker in the same
real estate transaction, with or without client consent". It applies whenever the lawyer "has a financial
interest in the real estate broker's commission" ([Op. 1043, 2015](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1043/))
[PRIMARY], and the conflict is "non-consentable" ([Op. 1117, 2017](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1117/))
[PRIMARY]. As a co-owner of the brokerage you also may not take closing referrals from its agents, even if you
waive your share ([Op. 1237, 2022](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1237/)) [PRIMARY] (L26, L31).

**The exception, with its conditions** (L27). Where "brokerage services are compensated by a fixed and
non-refundable fee, not contingent on closing", there is no per se conflict (Op. 1117 fn. 1, citing
[Op. 1015, 2014](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1015/)) [PRIMARY]. Op. 1015 adds three conditions: informed
consent "confirmed in writing"; a combined fee that is not excessive; and, preferably, separate offices. Three
more limits attach:
- **Who bills:** the legal fee must be billed by your law practice, never by the LLC Joe co-owns. Rule 5.4 bars
  sharing legal fees with a nonlawyer or practising through a nonlawyer-owned entity
  ([Op. 1082](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1082/)) (L28) [PRIMARY].
- **Which side:** only the seller. You cannot be the buyer's lawyer where your firm represents the seller
  [INFERENCE from Rule 1.7].
- **Title work:** you cannot also act as title agent in the same deal unless that role is purely ministerial
  ([Op. 1283, 2025](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1283-attorneys-referrals-to-title-abstract-company-owned-by-referring-attorney/))
  (L32) [PRIMARY].

Brokerage customers must also be told in writing that brokerage services "are not legal services" (Rule 5.7,
[Op. 1252](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1252/)) [PRIMARY].

**What a deal looks like under each option.**
- *Broker only.* The seller signs a listing agreement on a commission (or flat fee) payable at closing. The
  software drafts, schedules and fills in the offer on an approved form, and you approve each licensed step
  (L5, L22). The seller hires an independent Hudson Valley attorney, who by local custom drafts the contract
  and holds the deposit (L25). The product offers a directory of such attorneys with no referral payments (L31).
- *Broker and seller's lawyer.* The seller pays a fixed brokerage fee up front, non-refundable even if the
  house never sells, and signs a written informed-consent form. Your law practice is separately engaged, and
  bills the seller, to draft the contract and handle the closing. The buyer has their own lawyer. If the buyer
  has none, you may tell them only to get one (Rule 4.3, L30).

**Trade-offs.**
- *Fee:* sellers are used to paying only on success. An upfront fee they lose if the house does not sell is a
  harder sale, and its effect on volume is untested (WS2 handoff, A7) [INFERENCE].
- *Conflict:* the exception removes only the per se bar. You still carry ordinary Rule 1.7 risk in every
  dual-role deal, and you need a fresh written consent each time.
- *Insurance:* see below. Each role needs its own cover.

## Running a New York brokerage from out of state

- **Licence.** A non-resident "may become a real estate broker"
  ([RPL §442-g](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442-G)) [PRIMARY]. The DOS form has a box for
  "Attorney Admitted to NYS Bar", lets non-residents enter nine zeros for a New York DMV ID, and tells attorney
  applicants to stop after page 5. The fee is $185 for two years
  ([DOS-0036-f-a, Rev. 09/23](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-application)) [PRIMARY]. Section 442-g
  also requires an irrevocable consent naming the Secretary of State as your agent for service of process
  [PRIMARY].
- **Office.** "Each licensed real estate broker shall have and maintain a definite place of business within
  this state" ([RPL §441-a(3)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/441-A)) [PRIMARY]. A non-resident
  is excused only if already licensed and established as a broker in a state that grants New York brokers the
  same privilege (§442-g) [PRIMARY]. DOS "does not currently have reciprocity with any other state"
  ([DOS FAQ](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-broker-frequently-asked-questions)) [PRIMARY]. So the LLC needs a
  real New York address with a sign readable from the sidewalk (L4). A post office box alone is barred (DOS
  form) [PRIMARY]. Whether a shared or co-working office qualifies is a question for DOS.
- **Supervision.** The rule requires "regular, frequent and consistent personal guidance, instruction,
  oversight and superintendence" (19 NYCRR 175.21). DOS reads that as policies, training and monitoring, not
  presence "every minute of the day" ([DLS v. Metalios, 2009](https://docs.dos.ny.gov/ooah/decisions/Real_estate/2009/Metalios_Menalaos.htm))
  [PRIMARY]. We found no DOS rule or decision on a broker who lives out of state [gap]. With no salespersons, the
  practical test is that every listing, ad, offer and counteroffer is visibly your own decision (L5). The
  software logs each approval for that reason. Any showings or office staff in New York would need someone
  local you supervise [INFERENCE].
- **Time.** WS4 estimates 80 to 125 hours from you before launch, mostly legal set-up, over 8 to 12 weeks. The
  ongoing hours each week depend on deal volume and are still an open question for you
  ([WS4 handoff](handoff/WS4.md)) [INFERENCE].

## Your bar status

- **What to check.** Look up your registration in the court system's
  [attorney search](https://iapps.courts.state.ny.us/attorneyservices/search) (it blocked our automated read,
  so open it in a browser). Or call the OCA Attorney Registration Unit on
  [212-428-2800](https://iapps.courts.state.ny.us/attorneyservices/contact). Every New York-admitted attorney,
  "whether resident or nonresident", must register every two years. A lawyer who still practises anywhere pays
  $375; one who certifies retirement files without a fee
  ([Judiciary Law §468-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/468-A);
  [UCS FAQ](https://www.nycourts.gov/attorney-registration/frequently-asked-questions)) [PRIMARY]. New York has
  no "inactive" status.
- **If you registered as retired.** Retired means you do not practise law "in any respect, in any
  jurisdiction" and do not intend to (22 NYCRR 118.1(g), via the
  [UCS retirement FAQ](https://www.nycourts.gov/node/656)) [PRIMARY].
  - *Brokering:* brokering is not practising law, so retired status probably does not stop you being a broker
    [INFERENCE]. Whether DOS treats a retired registrant as "currently admitted" for the no-exam route, and for
    the §442-f exemption, is not stated anywhere we found. Ask DOS Licensing Services on (518) 474-4429.
  - *Acting as anyone's lawyer:* you must first return to active status, pay the fee, and meet CLE going
    forward (UCS FAQ) [PRIMARY].
- **If your registration lapsed.** Failing to register "shall constitute conduct prejudicial to the
  administration of justice" and goes to the Appellate Division for discipline (§468-a(5)) [PRIMARY]. The
  courts suspend non-registrants in bulk after notice
  ([1st Dept notice, 2025](https://nycourts.gov/COURTS/AD1/News/Mass-Suspension-Notice-2025-2.shtml)) [PRIMARY].
  A suspended lawyer is very unlikely to count as "currently admitted" or as an "attorney at law" under §442-f
  [INFERENCE]. Cure the registration first.
- **CLE.** While you do not practise in New York you are exempt from New York CLE, but you must certify that on
  the registration form ([UCS CLE page](https://www.nycourts.gov/continuing-legal-education-cle/not-practicing-law-new-york))
  [PRIMARY]. Taking a seller as a client ends the exemption for that cycle.
- **Practising New York law from out of state.** Judiciary Law §470 has not been repealed. A non-resident New
  York lawyer must keep an "office for the transaction of law business ... within the state"
  ([JL §470](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/470)) [PRIMARY]. The Court of Appeals held that this
  means "a physical office" ([Schoenefeld v. State, 2015](https://nycourts.gov/Reporter/3dseries/2015/2015_02674.htm)),
  and the Second Circuit upheld the statute (2016; cert. denied 2017) [PRIMARY].
  - *Repeal attempts:* Governor Hochul vetoed a repeal in December 2023 ([Brooklyn Eagle](https://brooklyneagle.com/articles/2023/12/28/governor-forbids-ny-lawyers-to-work-out-of-state-remotely/))
    [SECONDARY]. The current repeal bill passed the Senate 60 to 1 on 2025-03-10 but was still on the Assembly
    floor calendar when the 2026 session ended ([S2422/A3849](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S2422);
    [NYSBA memo, 2026-02-17](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/470-Memo-2026.pdf)) [PRIMARY].
  - *Sharing the brokerage office:* that office might also serve as your law office [INFERENCE]. But Op. 1015
    favours separate offices, so use at least a separate room, sign and letterhead, and ask the NYSBA ethics
    committee.

## Insurance

Your lawyer's malpractice policy almost certainly will not cover brokerage claims. New York courts enforce the
business-enterprise exclusion in lawyers' policies (*Admiral v. Adges*, S.D.N.Y. 2012; *Lee & Amtzis*, App.
Div. 2015, via [WS4](handoff/WS4.md)) [PRIMARY via WS4]. The brokerage therefore buys its own errors-and-omissions
(E&O) policy, budgeted at a median of $708 a year (WS4) [SECONDARY]. An E&O policy covers brokerage acts, not
legal work, so under the dual-role option you would also need an active malpractice policy for the law practice
[INFERENCE]. If you have been retired for years, you may hold only tail cover, or none. Ask your carrier in
writing two things: whether claims connected with a brokerage you co-own are excluded, and whether such claims
could taint your legal cover.

## What to decide and what to check

1. Decide: broker only, or broker plus seller's lawyer on fixed-fee listings only (L26, L27).
2. Check your registration status in the attorney search, or call 212-428-2800.
3. If it lapsed, re-register and cure before anything else. If you are retired, ask DOS ((518) 474-4429)
   whether retired status counts as "currently admitted".
4. Ask DOS whether a shared or co-working New York office with a sign meets §441-a(3), and how they view an
   out-of-state broker of record.
5. If you will act as a lawyer: return to active status, plan for CLE, set up a separate New York law office
   (§470), and get malpractice cover.
6. Ask your malpractice carrier, in writing, about the business-enterprise exclusion.
7. Tell Joe how many hours a week you can give, so the cost model can be closed.
8. Optional: send the NYSBA Committee on Professional Ethics a written inquiry on the shared-office and fixed-fee
   set-up (518-463-3200).
