Every one of them has put a licensed human back on offers, negotiation and closing; none replaces both agents; none is attorney-led in New York; and the fees have drifted up, not down. In the eight counties no online, discount or AI brokerage has an office: low-fee competition is Redfin at 2% with a $6,000 New York minimum, served from White Plains; Opendoor's statewide cash offers; two small human flat-fee brokers (845 Realty Guys at $9,995, NY Flat Fee Realty at $3,000 or 1%); Hauseit's $399 MLS-only listing; and 27 remote flat-fee listing vendors. All of them sit beside entrenched incumbents (Howard Hanna Rand, Houlihan Lawrence, Keller Williams, Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices) that own mortgage and title arms. **Kill criterion K1 is partly hit**: a "replace both agents" pitch only delivers its headline saving if buyers come without agents, yet 88% of buyers use one, buyer-side pay is still about 2.2–2.5%, and listings that pay buyer agents less sell measurably worse. **A6 (buyers will use a software buyer's agent with no human) is hit** on competitor evidence. K3 and K4 are dodged only by design. **Our position: go, but narrower.** A locally connected launch can win a small, profitable niche as a seller-first, attorney-brokered, flat-fee service that still pays buyer agents. The brief's "known local lawyer" asset is weaker than it assumes, because the father-in-law has been away from New York for almost ten years and 42% of today's owner households moved in since 2010, so **A5 is partly hit** and must be tested with real listings before heavy build.

## Six terms used throughout

A **listing agent** works for the seller and a **buyer's agent** for the buyer; each traditionally takes 2.5–3% of the price, though New York now averages less (below). The **MLS** (multiple listing service) is the shared database licensed brokers use; it feeds Zillow, Realtor.com and Redfin. Here it is **OneKey MLS** for Westchester, Rockland, Putnam, Orange, Dutchess, Ulster and Sullivan, and the **Hudson Valley Catskill Region MLS (HVCR)** for Columbia, Greene and northern Dutchess. A **flat-fee MLS entry** is a broker putting a seller's home on the MLS for a few hundred dollars and doing little else. A **rebate** is part of the commission handed back to the buyer or seller, which New York expressly allows ([RPL §442](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442)) [PRIMARY]. An **iBuyer** buys homes for cash, as-is, for a fee. **Steering** means buyer's agents showing clients fewer homes whose sellers pay them less.

## Seller-side AI and buyer-side AI both exist; nobody does both here

The field has settled on one shape. Software handles search, pricing comparisons, listing copy, scheduling and document assembly. A licensed human handles tours, offers, negotiation and closing. Prices sit at about 1% of price or a flat $700–$5,000, not zero. The pure "AI is the agent" pitch of 2024 has been walked back by every company that survived.

**Homa** is the clearest case. It launched in April 2025 as a free tool that "does not represent buyers" ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/04/25/can-an-ai-agent-get-homebuyers-to-the-finish-line)) [SECONDARY]. By December 2025 its founders said the fully AI do-it-yourself vision had not held up and "human agents still add value", and it charged $1,995 ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/12/27/an-ai-driven-homebuying-model-is-picking-up-steam)) [SECONDARY]. It had 10 Florida closings by late January 2026 ([NBC Miami](https://nbcmiami.com/news/local/some-homebuyers-saving-thousands-thanks-to-ai-but-how-does-it-work/3747934)) [SECONDARY]. By July 2026 it kept **1% of the price** and credited the rest of the seller-paid buyer commission to the buyer, with hourly or flat-fee showing agents and licensed broker associates writing and negotiating offers ([Yahoo/San Antonio Express-News](https://finance.yahoo.com/real-estate/articles/texas-company-using-ai-put-130000110.html)) [SECONDARY]. In October 2026 it raised **$5 million** (Quiet Capital), went live in California and described itself as one in-house broker per state ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/homa-raises-5m-expand-ai-homebuying-platform/)) [SECONDARY]. Its fee rose from zero to $2,000 to 1%. It has no seller product and no New York licence (no Homa entity in a DOS office search that matched every name starting "HOMA": [DOS search](https://data.ny.gov/resource/nsde-gcv2.json?$where=upper(business_name)%20like%20'%25HAUSEIT%25'%20OR%20upper(business_name)%20like%20'HOMA%25'%20OR%20upper(business_name)%20like%20'%25RIDLEY%25'%20OR%20upper(business_name)%20like%20'%25REALPHA%25'%20OR%20upper(business_name)%20like%20'%25TURBOHOME%25'%20OR%20upper(business_name)%20like%20'%25LANDIAN%25'%20OR%20upper(business_name)%20like%20'%25OPENDOOR%25'&$limit=100)) [PRIMARY].

**Ridley** is the seller-side mirror. It sells AI pricing, listing, offer-handling and contract tools for about $1,499, or $3,999 with a licensed agent for strategy, negotiation and closing ([5280](https://5280.com/can-ai-replace-real-estate-agents/)) [SECONDARY]. In July 2026 it operated in Arizona, Colorado and Florida, and its CEO claimed more than 300 homes sold ([AZFamily](https://www.azfamily.com/2026/07/23/ai-home-selling-platform-launches-arizona-offering-flat-fee-alternative/)) [SECONDARY; CLAIMED]. It raised $6.4 million led by Fifth Wall in December 2025 ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/ridley-bets-big-that-real-estates-future-belongs-to-consumers-not-commissions/)) [SECONDARY]. At launch it relied on partner brokerages for MLS access, and **the seller still paid the buyer's agent** ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/ridley-ai-colorado-launch-mike-chambers-social-media-home-sellers/)) [SECONDARY]. Its founder says "the goal is not to fully replace" an agent. Its old domain now shows an expired security certificate.

**LocQube** matters most for New York. It is a Bronx brokerage that bought Pantiga Group, a New York and Connecticut broker founded in 2009 ([LocQube blog](https://www.locqube.com/blog/tech-startup-locqube-acquires-pantiga-group)) [CLAIMED]. It launched an AI selling platform on 9 April 2026 ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/locqube-launches-ai-powered-home-selling-platform-that-puts-sellers-back-in-control-302738628.html)) [PRIMARY]. Its page, dated 20 October 2026, offers free AI pricing, an **$899 self-serve tier** (an AI-written listing on the MLS within 24 hours, with the seller running showings and "expert backup when needed") and a **1.99% Pro tier** with a dedicated agent ([locqube.com](https://www.locqube.com/)) [PRIMARY]. It says it is "available in New York and Connecticut" and names Westchester, not the Hudson Valley. It describes no buyer service and claims 578 sales and 30 in-house agents [CLAIMED]. This is the nearest thing to the father-in-law's idea already operating in the state: seller-side only, without a lawyer's name on it, one county south of the target area.

**reAlpha** bought **Prevu**, a New York-founded rebate brokerage, in November 2025. It now offers its AI buyer assistant "Claire" with brokerage in 12 states plus DC, including New York, and rebates of up to 1–1.5% ([reAlpha IR](https://ir.realpha.com/press-release/re-alpha-nasdaq-aire-acquires-prevu-to-expand-multi-state-footprint-and-offer-its-integrated-realty-and-mortgage-services-in-additional-states)) [PRIMARY]. It is weak: second-quarter 2026 revenue was about $1.1 million, down 11%, with a $3 million loss, $2.2 million of cash, a quarter of staff cut and a pivot to mortgages ([results via StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/AIRE/re-alpha-nasdaq-aire-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-upino8mn4vjy.html)) [PRIMARY content via aggregator].

Three smaller models are worth knowing. **Beycome** is a flat-fee brokerage ($99/$399/$999 listings, buyer rebates up to 2%) with an AI assistant, "Artur", that writes listings, runs comparisons, compares offers and reviews contracts. It operates in 18 states, not New York ([beycome.com](https://www.beycome.com/)) [PRIMARY], yet it holds a New York broker licence at an Albany address ([DOS](https://data.ny.gov/resource/nsde-gcv2.json?$where=upper(business_name)%20like%20'%25BEYCOME%25')) [PRIMARY], a possible sign of entry. **Waymark** in San Antonio is structurally the nearest to the father-in-law's design: an AI does the seller-side tasks and one broker reviews listings, disclosures and offers, for $699–$1,199. It is tiny, with one closed sale cited ([waymarkre.com](https://www.waymarkre.com)) [PRIMARY]. **AgentFree** in Atlanta is attorney-led on both sides at 1% each plus a $500 retainer, without showings ([goagentfree.ai](https://www.goagentfree.ai/)) [PRIMARY].

New York is unserved on several counts. No AI entrant drafts New York's attorney-prepared contract of sale. Opendoor's New York page stresses that it "works with a qualified NY closing attorney on every transaction" ([Opendoor](https://www.opendoor.com/sell/new_york_other)) [PRIMARY]. The attorney-plus-broker bundle has never scaled anywhere. The examples are solo practices in Washington ($3,500 flat), California ($5,000 flat), Florida and Tennessee ([Symmes Law Group](https://www.bankruptcy-law-seattle.com/Articles/attorney-broker-in-one-affordable-flat-fee-real-estate-services-in-wa/), [Kristina Reed](https://www.kristinareed.com/practice-areas/real-estate-law/fsbo-flat-fee-california/), [Attorneys Realty](https://arl.law/), [Rochford Law](https://rochfordlawyers.com/flat-fee-listing/)) [PRIMARY]. The one New York attempt found, "New York Real Estate Advocates" in 2009, left no trace of launching ([Miller Samuel](https://millersamuel.com/interview-joseph-ferrara-esq-sellsius-blog-the-clozing-social-media-new-york-real-estate-advocates/)) [SECONDARY].

The stealth search came up nearly empty. Y Combinator's 2025–2026 real-estate cohorts contain business-to-business paperwork tools, not consumer agent replacements ([YC directory](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/industry/housing-and-real-estate)) [PRIMARY]. HOMLI, a YC company listed in New York City that calls itself an "autonomous real estate broker", operates in Greece ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/homli)) [PRIMARY]. Money is flowing at least as much to tools for incumbents: Breezy raised $10 million for agent AI ([Axios Pro](https://www.axios.com/pro/fintech-deals/2026/02/02/bravo-afterpay-breezy-real-estate-10-million)) [SECONDARY]. HomeLight launched an AI closing agent, "EVA" ([MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/homelight-inc-announced-that-it-has-received-40-million-in-funding-from-blackrock-inc-ce7f59dcd080f620)) [CLAIMED]. Compass reports about 15,000 agents using its AI assistant ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/23/compass-ai-assistant-real-estate-agents/)) [SECONDARY].

The cheapest substitute is not a company at all. In May 2026 a New York Times technology reporter sold his Hudson Valley ranch for just over $600,000 using Google's Gemini and a closing process he ran himself. He estimated he kept about $90,000, while noting that an agent might have got a better price and that the AI gave advice he had to catch ([Newser summary](https://www.newser.com/story/390069/tech-writers-experiment-doesnt-bode-well-for-realtors.html)) [SECONDARY].

## In the eight counties, low-fee rivals are remote, small or human

### Who a seller can hire for less today

No online, discount or AI brokerage has a licensed office north of Westchester. **Redfin**'s only office in the nine counties is its White Plains principal office, with 68 licence records attached, and it has none in the eight target counties ([DOS offices](https://data.ny.gov/resource/nsde-gcv2.json?$where=upper(business_name)%20like%20'%25REDFIN%25'&$limit=50), [DOS licensees](https://data.ny.gov/resource/yg7h-zjbf.json?$select=county,count(*)&$where=upper(business_name)%20like%20'%25REDFIN%25'&$group=county)) [PRIMARY]. Redfin has marketed a "Hudson Valley" service since 2017 and extended it to parts of Ulster in 2021 ([Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/news/?p=59608), [StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/RDFN/redfin-expands-real-estate-brokerage-to-new-vacation-home-6o2jgk2bubcd.html)) [PRIMARY/SECONDARY]. In August 2026 its owner Rocket advertised a full-time agent job for Middletown, Woodbury and Newburgh ([job posting](https://www.purpose.jobs/discover/companies/rocket-companies/jobs/91624291-real-estate-agent-orange-county-middletown-woodbury-newburg)) [PRIMARY], so it may deepen in Orange County. **Opendoor** now makes cash offers "anywhere in New York" and names Poughkeepsie a top market ([Opendoor](https://www.opendoor.com/sell/new_york_other)) [PRIMARY]; the earlier notes said no iBuyer bought in New York, and that is now wrong.

- **845 Realty Guys**, a Poughkeepsie brokerage with one broker in the Hudson Valley since 2004, charges $9,995 flat, works for sellers only and caps intake at five new homes a month ([845realtyguys.com](https://845realtyguys.com/)) [PRIMARY].

### The incumbents and how entrenched they are

There is no single dominant firm across the region. Each county has different leaders, and below the top two or three firms sits a long tail of small offices. NY Department of State licence data (22 October 2026; counts follow the office a licence hangs at) show these totals across the nine counties ([DOS licensees](https://data.ny.gov/d/yg7h-zjbf), [DOS offices](https://data.ny.gov/d/nsde-gcv2)) [PRIMARY]:
- Keller Williams brands: 1,302 licensees in 22 offices.
- Houlihan Lawrence: 1,005 in 27 (840 of them in Westchester).
- Howard Hanna Rand: 733 in 18, plus 218 parked in a referral-only entity, Rand Referrals.
- eXp: 553 in 5.
- Compass: 473 in 19.
- Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices Hudson Valley Properties: 253 in 11, mostly Dutchess.

By head-count, the top firm holds about a third of licensees in Putnam (KW Realty Partners) and a quarter in Rockland (Rand, counting its referral entity). Elsewhere the top firm holds 9–17% [INFERENCE from DOS data]. Head-count is not sales share, and no MLS sales-share data is public. The one firm-level production figure found is Keller Williams Hudson Valley Upstate Network in New City: **2,154 transaction sides and $1.18 billion in 2025** ([RealTrends](https://www.realtrends.com/brokerage-profile/keller-williams-hudson-valley-upstate-network-formerly-group-new-city-ny/)) [PRIMARY]. Howard Hanna Rand calls itself "No. 1 in Rockland and No. 1 in Orange" by market share, undated ([Rand Realty](https://randrealty.com/about-us)) [CLAIMED].

Entrenchment bites in four places:
- **Ancillary revenue.** The big firms own or partner with mortgage, title and insurance businesses: Rand with 1st Priority Mortgage and Hudson United Title; BHHS with Prosperity Home Mortgage and Thoroughbred Title ([Rand](https://randrealty.com/about-us), [BHHS HV](https://www.bhhshudsonvalley.com/)) [CLAIMED]. That revenue funds agent splits and marketing a flat-fee entrant cannot match.
- **Referral capture.** Rand's 218 licensees in a referral-only entity keep inactive agents feeding the network ([DOS](https://data.ny.gov/d/yg7h-zjbf)) [PRIMARY].
- **Association seats.** Howard Hanna Rand holds four seats on the HGAR board, the association that co-owns OneKey ([HGAR board](https://www.hgar.com/who-we-are/our-leadership/board-of-directors)) [PRIMARY].
- **Agent loyalty.** 87% of sellers and 91% of buyers would use or recommend their agent again ([NAR 2025 Profile](https://www.rirealtors.org/clientuploads/documents/NAR/Homebuyers_and_sellers_trend_2025.pdf)) [PRIMARY].

The incumbents are also adopting AI. Compass rolled out an AI platform across its brands; it has 473 licensees and branches in Hudson, Kingston, Beacon and elsewhere ([RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/07/02/compass-rolls-out-ai-powered-home-platform-across-company-owned-brokerage-brands/)) [SECONDARY].

The defences are weaker where they matter less. The local associations and the HVCR MLS are run largely by small-firm brokers ([HVCRR leadership](https://hvcrr.com/the-association/leadership/)) [PRIMARY]. The MLS has consolidated into two open systems that a licensed entrant can join. And most of the roughly 6,900 licensees in the eight counties close only a handful of deals a year [INFERENCE from DOS counts and county sales]. Nationally the typical agent did 9 transaction sides in 2025 ([NAR 2026 Member Profile release](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/experienced-realtors-anchor-the-industry-as-housing-affordability-remains-top-hurdle-new-nar-report)) [PRIMARY].

On off-MLS listings, OneKey publicly refused Compass's demand to loosen its listing-sharing rules on 20 October 2026 ([OneKey](https://corporate.onekeymls.com/news/onekey-mls-rejects-compass-demand-defends-the-open-real-estate-marketplace)) [PRIMARY]. For now the local MLS stays open to a small entrant's listings.

### What local fees actually are

No Hudson Valley-specific commission dataset is public. The best New York figure, an August 2026 agent survey, puts the total at **4.62% (2.41% listing, 2.21% buyer side)**, down from 5.36% in 2024. It rests on only 25 New York agents ([Clever](https://listwithclever.com/average-real-estate-commission-rate/new-york/)) [SECONDARY], so it is a direction, not a number to plan on.

Nationally, buyer-agent pay has barely moved since the August 2024 settlement: Redfin measured 2.42% in the third quarter of 2025, against 2.36% a year earlier ([Redfin](https://redfin.com/news/commissions-q3-2025/)) [PRIMARY]. A mystery shop of 281 buyer agents in 26 metros found 95% quoting 2.5–3% ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/buyers-agents-resist-commission-negotiations/)) [CLAIMED].

Locally, commissions are "always negotiable" in the words of the HGAR CEO, and seller offers to buyer agents may no longer appear on the MLS ([Westfair](https://westfaironline.com/combined/new-real-estate-broker-rules-go-into-effect-in-wake-of-nar-settlement/)) [SECONDARY]. Settlement payments came from the incumbents' parents, with no local practice changes published: HomeServices (Houlihan Lawrence's parent) $250 million; Hanna Holdings (Rand's parent) $32 million; Compass $57.5 million; Keller Williams $70 million ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/10/03/5-more-settlements-granted-preliminary-approval-in-gibson), [Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2024/04/26/homeservices-makes-usd250-million-deal-to-settle-commissions-lawsuits)) [SECONDARY]. New York has no statute requiring buyer-broker agreements; S9564/A8910 sits in committee ([NY Senate](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S9564)) [PRIMARY].

The practical norm, then, is about 2.2–2.5% to each side in OneKey counties [INFERENCE]. That is lower than the brief's "2.5–3% per side", so the savings headroom is somewhat smaller than Joe assumed.

## Why earlier attempts failed: cost, capital and the buyer's agent

Every venture-scale US attempt to sell the same brokerage service for less has died, been absorbed or converged back on the traditional model. None of the post-mortems blames consumers for rejecting low fees; the causes are structural.

**Acquisition cost.** Purplebricks entered the New York market area, which explicitly included the Mid and Lower Hudson Valley, in April 2018 with a $3,200 fee due whether or not the home sold ([Investegate RNS](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/purplebricks-group--purp/purplebricks-to-launch-in-new-york/5157443), [The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/purplebricks-launches-in-new-york-three-months-ahead-of-schedule/)) [PRIMARY/SECONDARY]. It quit the US in July 2019 after US losses doubled to £34.1 million ([Mortgage Solutions](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/news/2019/07/03/purplebricks-exits-us-as-losses-reach-52m/)) [SECONDARY]. Mike DelPrete estimated it spent about **$15,000 to win each new listing**, against about $730 per closed deal at Redfin. The comparison flatters Purplebricks, since its denominator is listings won and Redfin's is deals closed ([DelPrete](https://library.mikedp.com/content/the-rise-and-fall-of-purplebricks)) [SECONDARY]. Portal leads are no cheaper for a flat-fee firm: Zillow's pay-at-close programme takes 35–40% of the agent's commission ([DelPrete](https://www.mikedp.com/articles/2023/10/12/zillow-flex-fee-rises-to-40-percent)) [SECONDARY].

**Capital.** Two models depended on outside money. REX raised $145.5 million and Reali about $290 million ([VC Backed](https://www.vcbacked.co/startup-graveyard/industry/real-estate)) [SECONDARY]. Reali shut in August 2022, citing "challenging real estate and financial market conditions and unfavorable capital-raising environment" ([release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/reali-closing-operations-301611949.html)) [PRIMARY].

**No real cost advantage.** Homie's $5,000 flat fee still paid salaried humans to do every licensed task. DelPrete's verdict was that its model "is the same as any other real estate brokerage". It ended its W-2 brokerage in April 2024 ([Salt Lake Tribune](https://www.sltrib.com/news/business/2024/04/19/homie-utah-company-meant-disrupt/)) [SECONDARY]. Redfin has walked from 1% to 2%, plus a 1% surcharge when the buyer is unrepresented ([Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/disclaimer)) [PRIMARY]. Houwzer went from $995 to $5,000 to 1% ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2020/02/05/flat-fee-brokerage-houwzer-hustles-up-9-5m-in-series-a-funding/)) [CLAIMED].

**Fighting the buyer's agent and the MLS.** REX stayed off the MLS, lost in the market, closed "all parts of New York" in October 2021 ([RealTrends](https://www.realtrends.com/blog/2021/10/11/rex-undergoes-second-round-of-layoffs/)) [SECONDARY], and lost in court. The Supreme Court denied review on 20 October 2025 and rehearing on 15 December 2025 ([docket 25-326](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-326.html)) [PRIMARY]. Homie alleged in court that once it reached "among the five largest brokerages by market share" in Utah, local agents posted refusals to show its listings and "used Facebook groups to coordinate boycotts". The Tenth Circuit affirmed dismissal on 11 August 2026 as time-barred, without ruling on whether the boycott happened ([10th Cir. opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca10-25-04101/pdf/USCOURTS-ca10-25-04101-0.pdf)) [PRIMARY]. An entrant facing an informal boycott in 2026 has no realistic court remedy [INFERENCE].

The steering evidence these failures rest on is solid but dated:
- In eastern Massachusetts, 1998–2011 (653,475 listings), lower-commission listings were **5% less likely to sell and took 12% longer**, because buyer agents avoided them. Newly founded low-commission offices also grew more slowly than higher-commission entrants ([NBER w21489](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21489)) [PRIMARY].
- Across 30 markets in 2021–22, the lowest-commission listings took about 33% longer and faced a **75% greater risk of not selling** in a typical market ([Iowa Law Review](https://ilr.law.uiowa.edu/volume-110-issue-4-1/2025/05/et-tu-agent-commission-based-steering-residential-real-estate)) [PRIMARY].
- No study yet measures steering after August 2024. With buyer agents still quoting 2.5–3%, there is no evidence the penalty has gone [INFERENCE].

**The AI cohort.** The 2024 AI cohort failed faster still, within about two years. Modern Realty (Y Combinator, 2024) is now a legal-software company. Bramble is now Struct, GetMyHome is an agent-tools firm, and Alokee appears to be out of business ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/modern-realty), [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/bramble), [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/530013-70)) [PRIMARY/CLAIMED]. A Hacker News critic of Modern Realty's launch summed up what the survivors learned: "ALL the value of a real estate agent is in the edge cases" ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41638199)) [PRIMARY].

## Can a locally connected launch win? A small niche, yes; the brief's version, no

### The case for

The market selects agents by relationship, not price:
- 66% of sellers chose an agent who was referred to them or whom they had used before.
- 80% contacted only one agent.
- Only 4% named the commission as the most important factor, against 35% for reputation and 22% for honesty ([NAR 2025 Profile, Exhibits 7-2, 7-3, 7-7](https://www.rirealtors.org/clientuploads/documents/NAR/Homebuyers_and_sellers_trend_2025.pdf)) [PRIMARY].

A trusted local name gets the first conversation without a national brand or an ad budget, and that is the only acquisition channel cheap enough for a flat fee [INFERENCE].

There is a segment that already buys less service. 14% of sellers bought limited or MLS-only service (Exhibit 7-5). FSBO (selling without any agent) runs at 10% in rural areas and 6% in small towns against 3% in suburbs, and 60% of FSBO sellers already knew their buyer (Exhibit 8-1) [PRIMARY]. The Hudson Valley has all four kinds of place.

Latent demand for lower fees is real: 42% of homeowners say they would probably or definitely use a broker charging 1.5% ([HousingWire on Consumer Policy Center survey](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/low-fee-brokerage-firms-home-sellers-savings-consumer-policy-center/)) [SECONDARY]. New York's attorney-closing custom also fits a lawyer-led firm better than it fits the Sun Belt AI entrants, none of which handles it.

Locally, the gaps are real:
- No AI or low-fee brokerage has an office in the eight counties.
- The two local flat-fee brokers are one-broker shops, one capped at five listings a month.
- Online vendors leave pricing, showings, negotiation and contracts to the seller.
- No vendor clearly covers the HVCR counties.
- The attorney-held broker licences in the region are dormant shells (sources above).

### The case against

**The father-in-law's network is a decade old.** He has not lived or worked in New York for almost ten years (Joe, 2026-10-23). Census data show 13% of owner households in the eight counties moved in during 2020 or later, and **42.5% in 2010 or later** ([ACS 2024 5-year, table B25038](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B25038&geo_ids=05000US36111,05000US36027,05000US36071,05000US36021,05000US36039,05000US36079,05000US36105,05000US36087)) [PRIMARY data; shares calculated]. NAR's data reward the agent a seller "used before" and the one a friend refers now. Nationally, agents in their first two years get 0% of their business from repeat clients or referrals; agents with 16 or more years get 49% and 32% ([HousingWire on NAR 2026 Member Profile](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-2026-member-profile-experience/)) [SECONDARY]. Returning after ten years, he starts closer to the newcomer than to the veteran. One caveat cuts the other way: the median seller has owned for 11 years ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/news/economists-outlook/top-10-takeaways-from-nars-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers)) [PRIMARY], so many near-term sellers bought while he was still there [INFERENCE].

**The buyer half gets little help from local trust.** In Columbia, northern Dutchess and Ulster, where prices and fees are highest, one local firm reports that 75–80% of its clients come from New York City, Long Island and New Jersey ([KeyCrew](https://keycrew.co/journal/how-the-hudson-valley-became-one-of-new-yorks-most-sought-after-second-home-markets/)) [CLAIMED]. In some Ulster towns, a third to two-thirds of homes are second homes ([Spectrum News](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/nys/hudson-valley/news/2026/03/10/legislation-would-provide-tax-exemption-for-full-time-ulster-county-residents)) [SECONDARY]. No local network reaches those buyers.

**His strongest channel is the most restricted.** His own law clients (estates, divorces, relocations) are the warmest leads, and New York ethics rules regulate exactly that hand-off:
- A lawyer "may not act as a lawyer and a broker in the same real estate transaction, with or without client consent" ([NYSBA Op. 1043](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1043/)) [PRIMARY].
- A lawyer who owns a brokerage may not take closing referrals from its agents ([NYSBA Op. 1237](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1237/)) [PRIMARY].
- Referring a law client into his brokerage needs a written notice that brokerage services are not legal services, plus consents ([NY Rules of Professional Conduct, Rule 5.7](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/NYSBA-NY-Rules-of-Professional-Conduct-as-amended-through-July-1-2026.pdf)) [PRIMARY].
- NAR's survey has no "referred by a lawyer" category at all ([NAR 2025 Profile](https://www.rirealtors.org/clientuploads/documents/NAR/Homebuyers_and_sellers_trend_2025.pdf)) [PRIMARY].
- Lawyers carry no trust premium over agents in Gallup's honesty ratings: agents 17% high or very high, lawyers about 20% ([The Close](https://theclose.com/news-real-estate-agent-trust/)) [SECONDARY/CLAIMED].

**Consumers fear discount service.** 82% worry a discount broker would not provide everything they need (same Consumer Policy Center survey) [SECONDARY]. An "AI replaces your agent" pitch walks straight into that fear.

**Success invites a response.** Homie's own filing describes what happened when a low-fee firm took real share: coordinated refusals to show its listings, and no court remedy ([10th Cir.](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca10-25-04101/pdf/USCOURTS-ca10-25-04101-0.pdf)) [PRIMARY]. Meanwhile:
- Redfin is hiring in Orange County.
- Opendoor buys statewide.
- LocQube is one county south.
- Beycome holds a New York licence.
- Compass is rolling out AI to hundreds of local agents (sources above).

Any local window is measured in a year or two, not a decade [INFERENCE].

### Our position

**A locally connected launch can win a small, profitable niche, but only in a narrower form than the brief describes, and only if the father-in-law, or a trusted local licensee, is visibly present in the region again.** The evidence supports a specific business. It is a seller-first, attorney-brokered brokerage at a flat fee in the low thousands. It pays buyer agents at a market rate by default, which the seller chooses with the data in front of them. It offers buyers an optional rebate service with a licensed human at tours and offers, and it reaches the HVCR counties as well as OneKey. Its first customers would be people who already know and trust him: estate sellers, divorcing couples, relocating owners, and owners selling to someone they know. Within the ethics rules, that means he is their broker, not their lawyer, on those deals.

That business does not need to beat Redfin nationally. In most of the region it only needs to be the cheapest licensed, accountable option, and the table above shows that place is open [INFERENCE].

The brief's version, software that "completely replaces" both agents and the lawyer, does not survive the evidence. Buyers still want and use agents. Every AI entrant brought humans back. The lawyer cannot be removed from a New York closing, and he cannot be both parties' lawyer and their broker [INFERENCE from the sections above; WS2 for the legal detail].

The decisive unknown is not competitive; it is whether his network still produces listings. That can be tested cheaply before the build goes past the demo: he names the first ten sellers he could call this month, and the pilot counts how many sign [INFERENCE].

On the project's own assumptions:
- **A5** (sellers will list with a new attorney-led low-fee brokerage because local trust carries) is **partly hit**. The trust mechanism is real, but the local network behind it is ten years old and the buyer side is out of its reach.
- **A6** (buyers will use a software buyer's agent with no human) is **hit**. The best-documented attempt reversed itself.
- **A10** (no well-funded flat-fee or AI player owns the Hudson Valley) is **dodged for now**. Nobody owns it, but LocQube, Redfin, Opendoor and Beycome are all within reach of it.

## What this means for the product

The competitor record converts directly into product requirements, listed with IDs in the handoff file. The product must:
- Show the licensed broker's approval as a visible step on every listing agreement, price, offer, counteroffer and negotiation message, because that is what every survivor converged on and what buyers and sellers trust.
- Treat buyer-agent pay as an explicit seller decision, shown with local data and the steering risk, never quietly set to zero.
- Price as a simple fixed fee with no closing-day surprises, because hidden percentages and minimums are the most common complaint against flat-fee vendors.
- Route every contract to the parties' New York attorneys rather than pretend to replace them.
- Show which MLS (OneKey or HVCR) each listing reaches.
- Record at intake how each client found the firm, so K5 and the network test are measured, not argued.
- Have AI advice checked before it reaches a customer, since the Gemini-assisted Hudson Valley sale shows a general chatbot will confidently give bad advice.

## Conclusion

The competitive question has a different answer from the one the brief feared. The danger is not that someone has already built "AI replaces both agents"; it is that nobody who tried could make it work without humans, so the advantage has shifted to whoever supplies the most trusted human at the lowest cost. In the Hudson Valley that could be a New York attorney-broker, and no competitor can copy that by raising money. But that advantage is personal, local and present-tense. It decays with absence, and it does not reach the New York City buyers who dominate the priciest counties.

The venture's competitive risk is therefore less about LocQube or Homa arriving and more about whether the founder's network is still alive. The cheapest, most informative next step is to find out with ten phone calls before building further.
