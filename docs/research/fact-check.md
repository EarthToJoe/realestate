# Independent fact-check of the six research reports and the memo (2026-10-23)

*Done by a headless job that did not write the reports. Labels: confirmed = a primary source read today agrees;
corrected = the report was wrong or overstated and was fixed in place; unresolved = could not be settled from public
sources. Edits are marked `*(Corrected 2026-10-23 by fact-check: ...)*` in the report itself.*

## Summary

About 60 checks were made (the dual-role question across six NYSBA opinions; every shared number or fact that
appears in two or more documents; each item in the seven handoff "Contradictions" sections; and about three
load-bearing claims per report that the writers had marked could-not-verify, secondary or claimed). **Eight
corrections were applied** (one also in a handoff), **two items stay unresolved**, and everything else was confirmed
or, where only the writer's own primary reading exists, accepted and labelled "not re-checked" in the table.
**Dual-role verdict in one sentence: the fixed-fee route exists, because Op. 1015 allows a lawyer to be a seller's
lawyer and broker if the broker fee is fixed, non-refundable and not contingent on closing and the seller gives
informed written consent, and Op. 1117 (2017, later than Op. 1043) restates it, so the WS5 handoff's "unsafe"
reading was wrong; but it is advisory, narrow and not re-endorsed since 2017, so it needs written ethics advice
before use.** The most important correction is therefore in WS5 (its K3 row told readers the exception was "not safe
to rely on"); the same logic fixed a mis-citation in WS4 and two over-statements of the conditions in WS2 and the
memo. Nothing in the memo that went to the lawyer needed to be reversed; two phrases in it were tightened (the
"preferably separate offices" condition and "up front", neither of which is an opinion condition).

## Check 1. The dual-role question: how Ops. 1015, 1043, 1117 and later opinions fit

| Opinion | Operative sentences (verbatim) | Link |
|---|---|---|
| **1015** (4 Aug 2014) | Question: whether the lawyer-broker may act for a seller "if the lawyer's compensation as broker is a fixed non-refundable fee not contingent on the closing of the sale". ¶16: "A lawyer may represent a seller of real property while also representing the seller as broker if (a) the services as broker are for a fixed non-refundable fee that is not contingent on the closing of the transaction, and (b) any conflict is properly waived." ¶10: "If the seller gives informed consent confirmed in writing, as required by Rule 1.7(b)(4), then the inquiring lawyer may undertake the representation." ¶16 also: "If the legal and broker services are not distinct, then the provision of broker services is subject to the rules of legal ethics, and the combined fees for legal and broker services may not be excessive." ¶8: the earlier bar "was based on the premise of contingent brokers' commissions", so the "reasoning in our prior opinions thus does not apply". | [Op. 1015](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1015/) |
| **1043** (8 Jan 2015) | ¶3: "a lawyer may not act as a lawyer and a broker in the same real estate transaction, with or without client consent, and whether or not the lawyer charges for legal services." ¶4: "This rationale applies as long as the lawyer has a financial interest in the real estate broker's commission... The disabling conflict that these opinions identify is a lawyer's pecuniary interest in the broker's success and attendant commission." The facts: a lawyer asking to take 25% of a broker's commission as a referral fee. Holding: no referral fee from a commission "even with a client's informed consent". It does not mention Op. 1015 or fixed fees. | [Op. 1043](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1043/) |
| **1117** (4 Apr 2017) | ¶7: "the per se rule does not apply when the broker is to be compensated by a flat fee... not dependent on the closing of the transaction". Footnote 1: "The per se rule of a nonconsentable conflict applies whenever the brokerage services are compensated in the usual way, as a commission contingent on the transaction being closed." It then says that with a "fixed and non-refundable fee, not contingent on closing" there is no per se conflict, "while there could be other foreseeable conflicts", which "could be subject to waiver by informed consent", citing N.Y. State 1015 ¶¶ 8-10. Holding (¶13): a lawyer "may not also serve in that transaction as a broker compensated by commission. The conflict cannot be cured by the client's consent because it is non-consentable." | [Op. 1117](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1117/) |
| 1208 (16 Nov 2020), 1237 (3 Mar 2022), 1273 (28 Aug 2024) | Each repeats Op. 1043's sentence ("with or without client consent") and calls the conflict "per se non-waivable"; none mentions a fixed-fee exception, and none overrules or criticises Op. 1015. 1237: a brokerage owner may not take closing referrals "regardless of whether the attorney agrees to waive" his agency's commission share. 1273 allows referrals from a broker-associate only with no fee or commission sharing and informed consent. 1208 distinguishes a lawyer with no financial interest in the commission. | [1208](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1208/), [1237](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1237/), [1273](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1273-referral-of-real-estate-matters-to-lawyer-from-real-estate-broker-who-is-also-employed-by-lawyer-as-an-associate/) |

**How they fit together.** The bar is on a lawyer who has a financial stake in a *commission that is earned only if the
deal closes*. That stake is what Op. 1043 (¶4), Op. 916 and the older opinions call the "disabling conflict". Op. 1015
(2014) found that a fixed, non-refundable fee not contingent on closing does not create that stake, so the conflict is
an ordinary Rule 1.7 conflict that written informed consent can cure. Op. 1043 was written five months later about a
commission referral fee, did not discuss Op. 1015, and its flat sentence is a quotation of the commission-era rule.
Op. 1117, two years after Op. 1043 and by the same committee, states outright that the per se rule "does not apply" to
a flat non-contingent fee and cites Op. 1015 for it. A reading in which Op. 1043 abolished the exception cannot be
squared with Op. 1117 (and Ops. 1208 to 1273 simply do not address a flat fee).

**Exact conditions (from Op. 1015 and 1117):** (1) the broker fee is fixed, non-refundable and not contingent on closing;
(2) the seller gives informed consent confirmed in writing (Rule 1.7(b)(4)); (3) the combined legal and broker fee is
not excessive, and if the two services are not "distinct" the broker work is itself governed by the legal-ethics rules
(Rule 5.7); (4) it is the seller's lawyer, never a buyer's (our reading of Rule 1.7, not an opinion statement). "Paid
up front" and "separate offices" are not conditions; ¶14 only says same-office delivery weighs against the services
being "distinct".

**Residual risk to state plainly.** Ethics opinions are advisory; the Appellate Division and grievance committees decide.
No opinion after 2017 re-endorses the exception, and the 2020-2024 opinions repeat the unqualified sentence. A
commission-based brokerage owned by the lawyer is barred (K3 stays hit on that path). The exception is usable only
for a flat-fee seller package, and the lawyer should get written advice (own ethics counsel or the NYSBA ethics hotline)
first. Reports changed: WS5 K3 row, WS4 K3 line, WS2 L27 row, memo (two places), WS5 handoff.

## Unresolved

1. **Does OneKey cover Ulster?** OneKey's own consumer page and HGAR list Ulster; WS3 reports that Rule 201 (Rules ver.
   2026C) omits it, while OneKey publishes Ulster reports and carries heavy Ulster volume. Reports say "five by rule,
   Ulster in practice", which is the safest reading and what the cost model assumes (a second MLS placeholder of $2,500
   a year). A phone call to OneKey or HGAR asking "can a new HGAR broker enter and receive Ulster listings on OneKey,
   and is a second MLS needed?" settles it. Impact on cost is at most the placeholder.
2. **Does the fixed-fee dual role survive a grievance committee?** Reading: yes on the text of Ops. 1015 and 1117, but
   no opinion after 2017 re-endorses it and Ops. 1208, 1237 and 1273 repeat the unqualified sentence. A written opinion
   from the lawyer's own ethics counsel or the NYSBA ethics hotline would settle it; until then treat the seller
   package as optional.

   zone; the OneKey word list and AI rules; the DHR phone numbers and other WS2 internal fixes; *Matter of Malul*;
